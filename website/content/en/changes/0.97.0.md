# 0.97 — Air above a table

The formulas printed above a table — the polar moment's and the wind's — are now measured to their ink. They no longer touch each other, nor the table's own rule. No number changes.

## New

- **A table's head breathes.** A built-up formula — a fraction, a root — reaches about 5 pt above and 5 pt below the line it is set on. It was that line, and not the ink, that the spacing was measured against: **1 pt** of white was left between two formulas, and **3 pt** under the last one before the table's rule, which the superscript on a tall bracket all but touched.
  - The head — its title and its formulas — is now measured to its ink. Every gap is the white it says it is: a little under half a line below the title, a full line between two formulas, half a line above the table.
  - This affects **Polar moments** at level 3, under «Kräfte in den Verbindungsmitteln, ungünstigste Überlagerung», and the two **wind** tables, whose formula sat 1 pt above its own rule.

## What changes in your reports

The numbers, the figures and the tables are identical. Only the height of these heads changes:

- the polar moment's block of three formulas (level 3) grows by about 20 pt;
- each of the two wind tables grows by about 10 pt.

The page breaks of the sections holding them may therefore shift by a line or two. A table without a formula — every other one in the report — is set exactly as before, to the pixel.
