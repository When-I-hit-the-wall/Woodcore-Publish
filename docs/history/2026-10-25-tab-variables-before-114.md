## 10quater (suite) — variables tab

**The variables tab (v0.100).** *Variables* (group *Référence*, between *Formule* and *Commandes*; `calc/tools/variables_tab.py`, its words under `vr_`, its reader `calc/variables.py`): **every value the report itself computes**, by domain, with the calls that print one. The engineer: «un onglet "Variables" qui liste toutes les valeurs calculés dans typst […] Les variables serait listées par domaine. Possibilité de filtrer par unité, par nom, etc… Possibilité de copier des commandes var, val ou autre». ⚠️ **What it lists is in no file at all**: `#var`, `#eq`, `#var-unit`, what `show-polar-moment` publishes and what every `verify-*` publishes live in the Typst state `var-registry` and reach no `data/` file, which is §1's standing condition on everything computed in Typst — so the loads tab's arrangement (read the export, show, decide nothing) cannot serve, and this is the first tab of Woodcore whose contents are read out of a **compiled report**. Until v0.117 a tree, one row per domain in the order the registry states them and its variables under it (three panes since, below); each variable with the **Name** one types into a call and, beside it, the **Symbol** the page prints, set as mathematics (`richtext.MathDelegate`) — the two are not the same word (`q_k11` is set q_(k,11), §10bis), which is the whole reason the tab is worth more than a listing. Above it a search box matching every word of the filter in any order against the name, the symbol, the label, the description, the unit, the citation and the value («kn max» finds F_max), and a **unit filter** offering the units the report actually uses, commonest first. On the right, cards: what the chosen variable carries — its domain, its two names, its value, its unit, its label, its description, its citation and its provenance, a row with nothing in it hidden — the **calls to copy**, and one line saying these values are untraced. ⚠️ **The empty domain is the report's own** and is named «Rapport» rather than shown as a blank row; a domain whose name carries `-verify-` is said to be a Nachweis, that fragment being the contract `lib/summary.typ` already finds every member check by (§3.3undecies) — and every other domain is «un domaine nommé», the tab claiming nothing it cannot read off the name. ⚠️ **`#var(key, domain:)` is offered for a named domain only**: `#var` reads its value from `domain:` or from a positional beside the key, so `#var("R_d")` alone can never work, and a variable of the report's own domain has nothing to be carried out of — `build_reference.call_var_registry` refuses it by name rather than writing a call that cannot compile. ⚠️ **It is a browser and has no `dirty`**, which in this window is what tells an editor from a browser: `update_tab_marks` reads it with `getattr(..., "dirty", False)` and `tests/test_window.py` takes every tab that has one for an editor and calls `update_list_marker()` on it, so a `dirty` always False would owe that walk a marker this tab has no list to mark (the loads and commands tabs are browsers the same way). Its `confirm_leave` never refuses a close: it only waits for a reading's thread, that being the one hook the window offers a tab on its way out. ⚠️ **Since v0.117 two views** (*Données · Affichage*, above): the card of calls left the right pane; *Affichage* offers, one or the other with its own options (the engineer: «une commande val, une commande var … afficher l'une ou l'autre»), `val` and `var` for a value of the export, `val`, `var` (a named domain) and `find` for a value of a document — no preview, a snippet holding no document's registry —, `show-calc` for a domain of the export and `find` for one of a document. `eq` is put off (docs/open-questions.md).

⚠️ *Retired by the spec 2026-10-21-commandes-typst-refonte (lot 19): `val` -- write #var("M_d") -- #var(name) reprints a variable declared above, #var(name, domain: ("snow-load", "SL")) reads one of the export. What follows describes it as it was.* **Three panes and every source (v0.117).** The engineer's issue 13: «Organiser l'onglet en trois colonnes : domaines | variables | détail», «Le volet principal devient une liste plate des variables du domaine choisi», «Permettre de choisir, quand on filtre, de filtrer toutes les variables», «Présenter toutes les variables, avec leur provenance affichée. Elles viennent de trois sources : les Excel, Woodcore (charges, connecteurs, moments polaires, etc.) et l'export JSON des variables calculées dans Typst» (spec `2026-10-08-onglet-variables-trois-colonnes`). **On the left the domains**, one flat row each with its count and its provenance — the documents' registries first, the one the window shows leading, then the export's: one domain per case of `data/calculations.json` (`snow-load · SL`), per connection (`connection · <id>`, the polar moments), per connector (`connector · <id>`) and per script (`script · <id>`), named and addressed as `find-calculation` reads them, so `#val("I_p", domain: ("connection", "VU-MP-1"))` is what it offers. **In the middle the variables of the domain chosen**, a flat list — name (★ before a result of its calculation), symbol, value, unit, label, provenance — under a search box, a unit filter, a **provenance filter** (Excel, Woodcore, Typst · CU/BP/RS; it replaced the document filter), **«Tous les domaines»**, which carries the filter over every variable of every domain and adds a column naming each row's domain, and **«Résultats seulement»**, off by default (D-005, D-007). **On the right the detail**: provenance, domain, the two names, value, unit, label, description, citation, level, the formula of the step that computes it, its `choices` with the current one marked, the workbook, sheet and cell it was read from and its raw `source` — what the loads tab showed, which it replaces — and *Ouvrir le classeur* for a value or a case read from a workbook; a domain chosen alone shows what it is. *Affichage* is the fourth pane, shown in that view alone, and its page takes the detail's place, the domains and the variables staying (`install_display(…, centre=2, right=None)`, D-011). ⚠️ **The provenance is decided where the variables are assembled and stored nowhere** (`calc/variable_sources.py`, D-012): a variable read from a document's registry is Typst's whatever its `source` says — `#var("s_k", domain: ("snow-load", "SL"))` copies an export's var, `excel:` source and all, into the registry —; every other is what its `source` prefix says, `excel:` Excel, `python:`, `norm:` and `woodcore:` Woodcore (`variables.PROVENANCE`, docs/architecture/data-contracts.md §3.1), and a prefix the table does not name fails `tests/test_variable_sources.py` rather than reaching the window as «inconnue». ⚠️ **The export is read from disk at every showing**, the four files together some tens of kilobytes, so a polar moment saved on another tab is here when one comes back (D-014); a file not there is a project not exported yet (said in the empty list), one there that is not what its schema says is named in the banner with the field at fault, and the other files are listed all the same (ARCH-003). ⚠️ **A view kept by a version before** carried three splitter sizes for what are now four panes: they are not applied, which would squeeze the detail to nothing (`restore`, review ARCH-RISK-001), and its document filter is gone with the column. The domains of `data/positions/<id>.json` are not listed — the export writes them empty (D-009) —, and the domains of the *Formule* tab are, as a source of their own (spec `2026-10-08-onglet-formule-domaines`).

⚠️ **Since v0.110 the reading is usually free, because the PDF carries it** (the engineer's choice of the three §14 put to him). `woodcore-document` attaches the whole registry to every PDF it writes -- `pdf.attach("woodcore-variables.json", bytes(json.encode(var-registry.final())))`, a few hundred bytes, **no extra page**, and **silently ignored for a PNG or SVG target**, so the 306 frozen example pages and the website's renders are untouched (both measured before it was written). `calc/variables.py`'s `from_pdf` reads it back with the pypdf already pinned, and `best()` is the policy in one place: the PDF's own attachment where that PDF is **newer than every source**, else a compile. Measured on the example project: **0.7 s against 4.4 s**, the same 90 variables in the same 8 domains, field for field. ⚠️ **It chooses a price and never an answer** -- both routes serialise the same `var-registry.final()` -- and a test compares the two on the example project rather than trusting it. ⚠️ **Typst resolves the attachment's name as a path relative to the file that calls it**, so what reaches the PDF is `_woodcore/lib/woodcore-variables.json`; Python matches on the **basename**, which is right whatever a project calls its generated folder. ⚠️ And the freshness test is the PDF's **modification time against the newest source**, not the `stamp`: a PDF carries no record of the sources it was made from, so what can be asked is whether anything has been touched since it was written -- a source saved in the same second reads as *not* current, which errs the safe way. ⚠️ **Since v0.111 a document whose compile left a stamp (`calc/stamps.py`, §12) is asked the stamp instead**: its PDF is current when not one of the files that compile read has changed since, whatever flags it was compiled with — a level chooses what is printed and never what is stored — and a digest and not a time, so a file saved again with the same bytes leaves it current; the modification-time rule above is what is left where there is no stamp, or where the PDF is not the one the stamp was taken of. And a reading's source set is **the document's own** (`stamps.enumerated(here, mode)`), never the whole Typst root, which in three documents is the project's folder and would take the other two documents, the workbooks and the archives for sources of this one's variables.

⚠️ **A reading is a full compile, and that is what decides when it happens (v0.100).** Measured at **8–10 s** on the example project against 9.8 s for `typst compile` of the same report: a state's final value is known only once the document has been laid out to convergence, so there is no cheaper question to ask. The engineer asked for «mieux encore, automatiquement importer depuis typst à l'ouverture de l'onglet», and automatic on *every* opening would be ten seconds of a hung tab per switch — the argument §10quater made for the RFEM tab (retired in 0.117), whose `on_shown` read nothing. So it reads **once**, the first time the tab is opened, in a thread with the tab usable meanwhile and the bar saying «Lecture du rapport…»; then it reads its **cache** until the report, the engine or the export has moved under it, and a banner says so. *Relire le rapport* is the one thing that reads again, its tooltip saying what it will cost. ⚠️ **A reading that fails keeps the values of the last good one on screen** and says so in the banner, in the danger colour, with **Typst's own message under the reason** — it names the file and the line, which nothing in the window could. The bar goes on saying what is listed rather than repeating the failure, and carries it only when a first reading failed and there is nothing else to describe. A file NextCloud holds open is tried once more and then said as what it is rather than as a report that does not compile (§13). A reading is refused while an export or a compilation runs — `data/` is in what it reads — with `app.busy()`'s message box for a click and a line in the bar for the automatic one, a modal put up by a tab merely being opened being worse than a tab that fills a moment later. `WOODCORE_VARIABLES_READ=0` turns the automatic reading off, which the suite does (`typst_check.ENV`'s own arrangement): a run that brings every tab forward to look at its buttons must not pay for a compile, nor leave a thread behind a window it is about to close.

⚠️ *Retired by the spec 2026-10-21-commandes-typst-refonte (lot 19): `var-of` -- write #var(name, domain: (domain, instance)) to read a value of the export, get(name, "value") for its number. What follows describes it as it was.* ⚠️ **How Python asks Typst what a state holds (v0.100, `calc/variables.py`).** `typst eval --in <report> --root <root> 'state("var-registry", (stack: ("",), domains: (:))).final()'`, whose JSON is the whole registry by domain. Three things about it are worth writing down because each was found by trying it. `typst eval --in` evaluates in the **standard library's** scope and not the document's, so the document's own `var-registry` binding is invisible there (`unknown variable: var-registry`) — a state is keyed by a **string** and so can be named again from outside, which is what makes the whole thing possible. `typst query`, the older route, is deprecated in 0.15.1 in favour of `eval`. And ⚠️ **the default must be exactly the one `lib/calc-engine.typ` declares**: a mismatched default is not a wrong answer here, it is the value the *document's* own `update` functions are handed, so the report stops compiling and the error surfaces inside `lib/formula.typ` — a defect that reads as one of the engine's. `REGISTRY_DEFAULT` is held equal to that declaration by `tests/test_variables.py`, the rule §11 already applies to the files `lib/distance.typ` names. 👉 **The level does not enter into it**: a level chooses what is *printed*, never what is stored, so level 1 and level 3 give byte-identical JSON (held by a test) and one reading serves all three reports. The **language** reaches the four labels the glossary composes (`eta_w`'s «Feuchteklasse 2») and no value and no unit, so a reading passes no `--input lang=` at all — what the tab shows is what the report itself holds, and the window's own chrome follows the window, which was the loads tab's rule for the labels the workbook wrote, and is the export's own in this tab since v0.117 (§4bis). What is cached is cached **outside the project**, beside the plan renders (`usersettings.cache_dir("variables")`), for the reason those are; the stamp it is compared against is the count, the total size and the newest modification time of every `.typ` of the report and everything under the engine copy — `lib/`, the glossary, the norm tables and **`data/`**, which counts because `var-of(...)` reads the export. ⚠️ The **PDF is deliberately left out of that stamp**: it is written *by* the very compile a reading pays for, so a stamp that counted it would be stale the moment it was taken, and again after every *Exporter PDF*.

**Choosing for a field** (issue #42, spec `2026-10-11-variables-liees-champs-et-textes`, UI-001, UI-003; `docs/domain/variable-links.md` §3.3duovicies). Since v0.117 the choosing mode «Importer…» of the «Formules» tab opened is also what the button beside a field opens — a member check's N, a static model's point x, a load's magnitude. `MainWindow.begin_variable_pick(on_done, title, accept)` brings this tab forward, the side navigation and Ctrl+1 … Ctrl+9 off, and gives the choice back to **the tab that asked**, which is the one in front as it asks (it was the «Formules» tab by name until then). `accept(var, domain)` — `variable_sources.linkable` — is what the field may take: a number of the export or of a «Formules» domain, of the field's dimension by `calc/units.py`, of a source the field may name; `VariablesTab.begin_pick(…, accept)` lists only the domains holding something it accepts and only those variables, the bar naming the field and its unit («Choisir une variable pour N [kN]»), and «Importer» on a variable it refuses says to choose one in the list. A document's registry is never offered to a field: a link is resolved by Python at the export, without a compile (D-02). The choice ended, every domain is listed again.
