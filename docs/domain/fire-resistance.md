# Fire resistance of members (SIA 265 4.5, VKF 15-15)

> The member checks in the design situation fire, the fire resistance VKF / AEAI 15-15 requires of the load-bearing structure, and what was compared with the reference workbooks and the AEAI directives.

### 3.3tervicies Member checks in the design situation fire (issue #46, v0.118)

The engineer asked for «un onglet de calcul de résistance au feu d'après le classeur de référence» — *«fire, bending» template*, *«fire, buckling» template* and *«fire, steel profile» template* — and for a comparison with the AEAI directives (spec `docs/requirements/completed/2026-10-12-resistance-au-feu.md`). Steel is put off (D-02): SIA 263's fire part and EN 1993-1-2 are not in the library.

**The method is SIA 265:2021 4.5.2** (D-04), read off `SIA265-2021-DE.pdf` pp. 38–40 and transcribed once into `calc/norms/sia265-2021-4.5-brandfall.json`, which `calc/norms/sia265.py` (`fire()`, `charring_rate()`) and `lib/timber.typ` (`timber-files.fire`) both read:

- the ideal residual section (4.5.2.2): `d_char,n = beta_n · t` (50), `d_ef = d_char,n + d_red` (49), `d_red` = 7 mm up to 90 minutes — beyond, the clause gives none and the check cannot be calculated —, the rectangle reduced by `d_ef` on each exposed face (Figur 13; left and right reduce b, bottom and top h);
- `beta_n` of **Tabelle 13**: softwood or beech, solid timber 0.8, glulam 0.7; oak or robinia 0.5; solid timber cladding 0.9 — the grade's family (Tabelle 8 or 9) and its species (Nadelholz, Laubholz) pick the row, a hardwood grade (D30) asking for beech or oak;
- the minimum width of **4.5.2.4** where several faces burn: 80 mm for R30, 140 mm for R60 — none stated above R60, which the page then does not check;
- **R_d,fi ≈ 1.8 · R_d** (51), R_d the design resistance of the residual section at normal temperature — η_w and η_t included, as (51) says and as the Biegung workbook computes (`fm,d · 1.8 · η_w`) —, without k_h (3.4.2.3) and k_sys (5.7.2.2).

⚠️ *Retired by the spec 2026-10-21-commandes-typst-refonte (lot 19): `member` -- write #position(id) for the member's line; its checks are #include domain-file("check", id). What follows describes it as it was.* **In the report**: `#member(name, …, fire: (duration:, sides:, wood:))` writes the member down as its residual section — it publishes `t_fi`, `beta_n`, `d_char_n`, `d_red`, `d_ef`, `b_0`, `h_0`, `k_fi`, then `b` and `h` **as the residual sides** (symbols b_fi, h_fi), so `A`, `W`, `I`, `i`, the member's record and every `verify-*` read them without a line of their own changed; its design values f_m, f_t,0, f_c,0 and f_v carry `k_fi` (f_t,90 and f_c,90, which no fire check reads, do not); f_c,0,k and E_0,05 are not scaled, so buckling's λ_rel is the residual section's and its resistance 1.8 · k_c · f_c,0,d — exactly 1.8 · R_d. At levels 1 and 2 one line «Bemessungssituation Brand: t; d_ef; b_fi; h_fi», at 3 each value with its citation. A round section, a duration over 90, no face, a hardwood without its wood, a width under 4.5.2.4's or a section the fire consumes is refused with a panic naming it. `lib/summary.typ` marks a check whose domain carries `k_fi` «(Brandfall / incendie / fire)» in the Nachweisübersicht, so a utilisation against R_d,fi is never read as one against R_d.

**Saved in Woodcore** (D-06, Q-010): a check of the «Résistance au feu» tab is a source of `Woodcore-Datei/checks/` of an **existing kind** — `bending`, `shear`, `buckling`, `compression`, `tension`, along the grain — carrying an additive object `fire` `{duration, sides: {bottom, top, left, right}, wood, top_level}`, at **`schema_version` 1.2** (1.1 being links alone; `stamp_version` writes the lowest that holds what the source carries, so a check at normal temperature is saved byte for byte as before). `member_check.check_source` refuses a `fire` at another version, on another kind, at an angle, with an unknown key, a duration that is no positive whole number, faces that are not the four booleans, a wood outside Tabelle 13's; `resolve(src, positions, values, building)` adds the resolved `fire` — `duration` and `duration_from` («stated» or «vkf»), the `requirement` VKF 15-15 gives, the faces, the wood, `beta_n`, `d_ef` and the residual `b`, `h` — and says why a check cannot be calculated (no duration, VKF 15-15 requiring none, over 90 minutes, no face, round, a hardwood without its wood, 4.5.2.4, nothing left). The export is **1.2** where one check carries `fire`. `draft_typst` and `#show-check-member` hand `#member` the resolved `fire:`; `compute` gives the window the same rows, held equal to the report by `tests/test_member_check.py` (ten synthetic cases and the example's FE-01, FE-02).

**The duration follows the building** (D-10): `Woodcore-Datei/building.json` (`project.building_file`, `schemas/fire-building.schema.json` 1.0: `height`, `use`, `concept`, `storeys`), written by the tab and read by `member_check.prepare` (`read_building`: absent, a check that follows says why; malformed, the export stops naming the field). `calc/norms/vkf.py` `required_resistance(building, top_level)` reads the column «Système porteur» of **VKF 15-15 tables 1 to 3** (`calc/norms/vkf-2017-3.7.1-15-15-tragwerk.json`, the directive's pp. 12–15, read off a render of the pages) and applies its notes **as the file states them**: [1] (tables 1 and 2) no requirement in a one-storey building or on the top storey; [5] (table 1, where it marks a value or a use) 30 minutes less for two storeys and 2400 m² at most; [8] (table 3) 30 minutes less on the top storey; [9] (table 3) none in a one-storey building. Notes [2], [3], [4], [6], [7] concern compartment walls, open car parks, fire detection and the height category itself, and are the engineer's to apply (the tab says so). **3.2.3 al. 2 c and d** exempt, whatever the height, a single-family house (its underground storeys included) and a building of reduced size: two uses of their own in the file, `exemptions`, in no table. Table 3 has no row for agriculture and isolated lodging: such a check says so. A check may write its own duration over — 3.7.1 al. 2: the tables are standard solutions, from which art. 11 allows a proven departure.

**A duration VKF 15-15 requires is cited by VKF 15-15** (v0.120, spec `2026-10-20-indices-et-niveaux`, REQ-001, the engineer's answer to Q-017 of the base saine). Where the duration follows the building (`duration_from` «vkf»), the export composes once `fire.required`, `{"norm": "VKF 15-15", "norm_ref": "Tabelle 2"}` (the table `required_resistance` read it off), and `data/checks.json` is then at 1.5; `#show-check-member` copies it into `#member`'s `fire: (required: (norm:, norm-ref:))`, and the line t_fi cites it in the report as the window's row does (`member_check._compute`). A duration the engineer states cites SIA 265 4.5.2, as before. `tests/test_member_check.py` holds both, and `_held_equal` compares each row's norm as well as its clause.

**The link with the fire-protection plan is the position** (D-11): a check names its position as every check does, and the fire plan, which follows the position plan, does not change.

**What the forces are** (D-08): E_d,fi, typed as the fire situation gives them; 4.5.2.2's E_d,fi = 0.6 · E_d (categories A and B) is transcribed for the record and not offered.

**What the AEAI directives of the library hold.** 13-15, 14-15 and 15-15 contain no design method for timber: 15-15 §4.1 asks for the fire resistance «démontrée par le calcul, sur la base de la courbe normalisée, ou par des essais», and their §5 refers to the «documents fixant l'état de la technique» the AEAI recognises — the Lignum-Dokumentation Brandschutz 3.1 among them, which SIA 265 4.5 itself names for protected members and connections. The engineer: the Lignum 3.1 «contient en fait les normes VKF-AEAI» (D-05). So the comparison is SIA 265 4.5 for the method and VKF 15-15 for the required resistance; the Lignum 3.1 itself is not in the library and nothing is read from it.
