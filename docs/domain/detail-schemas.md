# Detail schemas

> The one drawing of a connection detail, modified and consumed by connectors and polar moments.
>
> Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md (§3.3decies) on 2026-10-07. The § numbers are kept as stable anchors: `docs/README.md` maps every § to its file.

### 3.3decies Detail schemas: the one drawing of a connection detail (v0.72)

**The dimensions' geometry is shared (issue #9).** A schema's dimensions are computed by `calc/domains/dimension.py`, as a static model's and a position plan's, the anchors resolved here (in mm); their tools show the static models' symbols (`calc/tools/dimension_icons.py`).

**An area element drawn from a position (v0.117).** `schema.area_fixed(record, draw)` says which side of a rectangle a position's section fixes — a `rec`'s height where its width is drawn, its width where its height is, the thickness of a plate or a CLT panel, the diameter of a round section — and `rectangle_with_fixed_side` / `rectangle_with_fixed_width` draw the rectangle with that side imposed, whichever of the tool's three modes is used; the element carries the position, its points in mm, and no new field (`schema_version` 1.1): which side was fixed is not recorded, so a position whose dimension changes afterwards leaves the polygon as it was while the element's `b` and `h` follow it. The window's part is in docs/ui/window.md §10quater.

A **schema** (Schéma) is the drawing of a connection detail: the pieces, the fastener markers, the construction lines and points that helped draw it, and its dimensions. It exists because the same physical geometry was being drawn twice — until v0.73 *Moments polaires* and *Connecteurs* each carried a form of pieces and a table of fastener coordinates — which is the duplication §2 rule 1 forbids everywhere else, and because neither of those forms could draw what a detail actually needs. It is the one place a detail is drawn, and since v0.74 **both tabs name one** instead of carrying their own geometry (below, *What consumes one*).

**Source.** `Woodcore-Datei/details/<id>.json`, one file per schema, written by Woodcore's «Schémas» tab and read by `calc/domains/schema.py` alone — the connections', the plans' and the static models' arrangement, for the same reasons (§13). `schemas/schema-source.schema.json` states its shape (`schema_version` 1.1).

⚠️ **The folder is `details/`, not `schemas/`.** the program's `schemas/` has held every `*.schema.json` contract since v0.1 — this module's own two included — and `source_paths()` globs `*.json` without asking what kind of file it found: pointed at that folder it reads `calculations.schema.json` as a detail and refuses it for having no `schema_version`, which is exactly what happened the first time a real example was exported. `details/` is this module's own word for what it draws («un schéma est un détail à l'échelle réelle»), so nothing else had to be renamed: the module, its commands, `data/schemas.json` and the two contracts that describe both keep their names.

**The frame is the connections'** (§3.3quinquies): **millimetres**, x to the right, z down, angles in degrees from +x turning towards +z — so that a connector or a polar-moment calculation can take a copy of a schema's geometry with no conversion at all.

| collection | what one entry holds |
|---|---|
| `elements` | `id`, `kind` (`linear`, a piece seen edge-on — a polyline of at least two points, its band drawn at the side of its section its `view` shows; or `area`, seen face-on — a polygon of at least three), an optional `position` (a row of `Positions Element` this piece is a detail of), `view` (`h` by default — «on voit leur hauteur» — or `b`), **`b` and `h`** (v0.75: absent, each follows the position; present, written over), `label`, `label_offset` |
| `connectors` | `id`, `kind` (one of `connector.TYPES`, so a marker already says what it will be calculated as), `x`, `z` in mm, an optional `diameter` — normally absent, the diameter being the calculation's and not the drawing's |
| `aids` | `id`, `kind` (`centre`/`point`, a named point; `vertical`, `horizontal`, `point-angle`, `perpendicular`, `parallel`, a construction line through one point; `two-points`, one through two), its geometry, and **`shown`** |
| `dimensions` | statics.py's own four kinds (§3.3octies), taken as they stand: `horizontal`, `vertical`, `parallel` and `angle`, with a schema's own anchors -- a length between two point-shaped aids (`{"point": id}`) or places of their own, an angle between two linear elements or construction lines (`{"element": id}`, `{"aid": id}`) -- the same `offset` measured from the middle of the two ends, the same `bearing` naming the sector an angle is measured in, and the same optional `label` and `show` mode (`value`, `name`, `name+value`, `none`; `schema.dimension_show`, v0.87) |

⚠️ **A named point is an aid**, of kind `centre` or `point` — there is deliberately no fifth, flat `points` collection beside the four. A point worth naming is drawn as an aid, so it is visible (or not) exactly like any other aid, and a dimension's `{"point": id}` anchor names one, following it as it is moved the way a static model's anchor follows a point. A connector marker carries its own `x`/`z`.

**The drawing carries the three factors too** (v0.99, §3.3quaterdecies): `style` on the source — `line`, `point`, `text`, from 0.25 to 3 — and on any entry the factor it states over them. A schema had none at all until v0.99; a source that states none is drawn exactly as it was, all three being 1.

⚠️ **What is exported, and what is not.** A point or a centre carries `shown: true` by default and reaches the report; a construction **line** carries `false` and does not. A line marked shown is drawn more heavily inside the module, so that it reads as deliberate rather than as clutter left behind.

⚠️ **An element knows its section, b and h (v0.75)**, where until v0.74 a linear element stated one number, `width`, the thickness its band was drawn at. A side the element does not state **follows its position**, read afresh at every export off the position's `dimension` — «100/200» mm gives b = 100 and h = 200, cm and m converted (`schema.parse_section`, the one reader of that text in the pipeline, §7) — and a side it states is **written over and stays written over** when Positionen.xlsx changes, the connectors' own reference pattern (§3.3nonies): Woodcore says it in orange, naming what the position holds, with *Reprendre* beside it. **The band is the side the `view` shows**, h for «h» and b for «b»; while that side is unknown — no position, or a dimension that is not b/h, and nothing stated — it is drawn at `ELEMENT_DEFAULT_WIDTH` and the export says so in a warning, never a stop. A 1.0 file is read as 1.1 (`schema.upgrade_source`): its `width` becomes the side its view shows, so its drawing does not move by a pixel, and a 1.1 file still carrying `width` is refused by name. The project's three files were rewritten with the overrides that merely repeated their position taken off, so they follow Excel.

⚠️ **Since v0.94 b and h follow the position's `section`**, which `calc/domains/section.py` wrote at the export (§7), and no longer its text: a rectangle gives its b and h, a round section its d for both, a plate's or a CLT panel's t for both; a steel profile gives none, which is a warning and the default width until a side is stated. The label prints a section other than a rectangle as its text — «S003 GL24h Ø 200 mm», «S002 S355 ROR 101.6×10» — and `schema.parse_section` is gone.

⚠️ **The label is the position, its strength class and the section** — «T001 C24 100/200» (`schema.element_caption`) — unless the element states a `label` of its own. A dimension that is not b/h is printed as the workbook has it («D001 C24 180»), a position the export lost by its number alone. It is set on the **middle of the longest stretch of the piece no other piece covers** (`schema_svg._free_middle`), so a beam through a column carries its number on its own visible half and the column on its own: v0.72 to v0.74 set it on `points[len // 2]`, a two-point element's far END, and the middle of the whole length is the node, under the other label and a connection's rotation centre. The export record carries each element's `b`, `h`, `b_from`, `h_from` and drawn `width` (`schemas` **1.1**).

**`crop` (v0.75)** is the part of the detail the report shows, `{x, z, w, h}` in mm, dragged out with *Cadrage*: the exported frame is the crop, while the canvas shows the whole detail with the crop marked over it and a veil outside; an element or a marker wholly outside it is a warning. A connection's and a connector's own figures keep their frame, which their arrows and legend need.

⚠️ **The editor draws what the report leaves out**: `schema_svg.layout(..., editing=True)` keeps the construction lines and points whose `shown` is false, light and dashed in `AID_COLOUR`. Until v0.75 such a line was not drawn at all in the very tab it was drawn in — it existed only as a selection outline, and only once selected in the list — and `schema.hit_aid_line` did not exist either, so the line *Trancher* cuts on could not be picked on the drawing. A marker stating its `diameter` is drawn at it, never below a floor of legibility.

⚠️ **A named point is a green cross, drawn in front of everything, the construction lines included (v0.77)**, so it is always seen: `schema_svg.POINT_COLOUR`, drawn after the lines in the figure (`body`) and, in Woodcore, by the canvas itself after its own lines (`drawForeground`, `aid_point_marks`, a cross of a fixed size on screen), the SVG it renders leaving the points out (`aid_points=False`); the *Point* tool's icon is that cross. A centre keeps its grey cross and circle, under the lines. ⚠️ **Construction lines are drawn over everything, and to the edges (v0.77).** In the figure the aids come **last** — the centres over the pieces, the dimensions and the markers, the lines over them, the points over the lines — still between `AIDS_OPEN`/`AIDS_CLOSE`, so `aids: false` strips them as before; and a shown line runs to the edges of the frame (`schema_svg.clip_line`, the frame being the crop where one is drawn), where it used to run a share of the detail's size either side of its own point. Woodcore's canvas renders the SVG **without** its lines (`render(..., aid_lines=False)`) and draws every line itself, across the whole view and over everything (`schema_canvas.drawForeground`): a construction line is infinite, and no line written into an SVG can be. Every line is drawn there, whatever its `shown`; one the report prints is heavier and darker.

⚠️ **A shown construction line is cut at the boxes of the labels it crosses** (v0.99, `schema_svg.cut_at_labels`). The aids are drawn over everything, the labels included — v0.77's rule, which stands, a line being what the eye reads the detail against — so a shown line ran straight through a piece's number and **no ground behind the text could mend it**: the line is over the text either way. It is the line that gives way, which is what a drawing office does: it stops at the box and starts again beyond it, and still runs to the edges of the frame. A **dimension line** is cut the same way under its own value (`_draw_linear_dimension`), which is the second half of the engineer's answer — a value floats clear of its line by default, so nothing is cut until it is dragged onto it, which is exactly when it would otherwise be unreadable. ⚠️ **Woodcore's canvas cuts them too** (`schema_canvas.aid_line_segments`): it draws the lines itself, across the whole view, so a cut made only in the export would show the engineer one detail and the checker another — which is the very thing that canvas exists to prevent.

⚠️ **Where two things cross is a snap point (v0.77).** `schema.snap_geometry` gathers what a click may be pulled onto: the aid points and the markers, the elements' vertices and **the corners of their bands** (priority 0); the middles of their segments; and **every crossing** — an element's axis or the edge of its band against another element's, a construction line against an element or against another construction line. A point also slides along a band's edge and along a construction line (`on-line`). The canvas keeps the result beside its drawing, once per state of the schema, rather than solving every crossing on every movement of the mouse; a crossing is kept to a millionth of a millimetre, so a line at 90° lands on 40 and not on 40.000000000000014.

**The drawing.** `calc/domains/schema_svg.py`, as SVG text, kept to what QtSvg and Typst read alike (SVG Tiny 1.2, no `<marker>`, no CSS, no arc command, a halo is a text's own box, translucent (v0.99, below)) — the rule every figure here follows. One file per language under `data/schemas/<id>-<lang>.svg` (§4bis). ⚠️ The three are **identical today**, and knowingly so: everything this figure writes is the data's own — a position number, a piece's label, a dimension's value and its unit — and the engine says no word of its own on it yet. That is the same state the connections' `Beispiel` is in (§13), and the convention is kept rather than collapsed to one file because the first legend put on this drawing would have to restore it. ⚠️ A schema is a **detail at its real size**: its coordinates *are* millimetres, scaled to `PAPER_MM` only when the SVG's own width and height are set — `connector_svg.section()`'s arrangement — and **not** renormalised to 1000 figure units the way a static model's scale-free sketch is (§3.3octies). That is what lets a calculation place its own arrows over this drawing later without a conversion between the two.

**A text is set over a white rectangle, its own box, and never drawn twice with a wide white stroke** (v0.99). The stroke method puts a cloud round every letter rather than a ground under the word — the finding `statics_svg.py` already records for its own labels (v0.77), made here again by the engineer on a schema — and it is made for **every** text the schema writes: a piece's label, a marker's id, a point's name, a dimension's value. The rectangle is the label's own box (`hw`, `hh`), the one a click and a leader read, turned with its text, and it is **translucent** (`LABEL_GROUND_OPACITY`, 0.85): a schema's label sits *on* the piece it names — the middle of the longest stretch no other piece covers (v0.75) — so an opaque ground would take a bite out of that piece's fill. It is one constant, and 1.0 is an opaque white. It is drawn as a polygon of the corners `label_corners` already computes rather than as a `<rect>` with a `transform`, which is one thing fewer for either renderer to agree about.

The labels are `statics_svg`'s: a piece's, a marker's, an aid's and a dimension's value may each be **dragged clear** of what it names, its `label_offset` saved from its own place so it follows its element, and a thin leader runs back to that place once it has left it (the v0.42 rule, §3.3sexies). ⚠️ **A piece's label brought back onto its own piece loses its leader** (v0.77, the engineer's request): `frame["leader"]` is drawn only while the label's centre lies off the piece — outside a linear element's band (or further than the label's half-height from its axis, where the band is thinner than the text), or outside an area's outline — plan_svg's own rule, a number moved along its beam getting none. `frame["moved"]` keeps saying whether an offset is stored at all. Since v0.79 a named point may carry a **`label`**, typed in the *nom* column of the «Points» list and drawn beside it in the aids' own colour, draggable like the others; and a marker's label and a dimension's value dragged clear carry the same leader (periodic audit 260916, B17). **A `*` typed into a piece's label or a dimension's name is drawn `·`** (v0.87, second half), `statics_svg`'s own rule restated in `schema_svg.text_runs` (`TYPED_TIMES`, `TIMES`), so the two figures write one formula alike; the source keeps the `*`.

**Export.** `schema.prepare()` reads and checks every source; a defect stops the export naming the collection, the entry and the field. It builds `data/schemas.json` (schema `schemas` 1.1, checked by `validation.validate_schemas_business()`) and the figures. The record carries the frame, every element, marker, shown aid and dimension with its placed geometry, the ids `highlight` may name, and `unresolved` — an element whose `position` no longer names a row of `Positionen.xlsx`. ⚠️ That is a **warning**, never a stop: the v0.33 rule (§13), applied to a fifth editor. The record carries the drawing's own three factors (`style`) and, on every entry that states one, its own beside its placed geometry — as the plans' and the static models' records already carry theirs. ⚠️ `data/schemas.json` therefore gains one object per schema at the next export, and nothing else of it moves.

**Typst.** `show-schema(schema, width:, highlight:, aids:, caption:)` sets the figure; `highlight` puts a halo under an element, a line-shaped aid or a dimension, inserted at the drawing's own `<!-- highlight -->` marker as a plan's and a static model's are. ⚠️ **A point is ringed over the pieces** (v0.87, the engineer: «Un point doit être affiché au-dessus de tout»): a marker, or a point- or centre-shaped aid, takes a yellow **ring** at a second marker, `<!-- highlight-points -->`, which `schema_svg.body` writes after the pieces and the dimensions and before the markers and the aids section — so the point's own symbol stands on its ring and a construction line still crosses it. Until then its disc sat at `<!-- highlight -->`, under pieces filled at three quarters opacity, and a highlighted pin inside a beam did not show at all. A ring rather than a disc, since a disc drawn over a piece hides the piece around the point; sized from the drawing's extent (`schema-extent`), where the disc was a fixed 6 or 10 mm. A figure exported before v0.87 carries no second marker and takes every halo at the first. The aids the source left shown are printed by default and `aids: false` strips them at a marker of their own — `false` was the default until v0.79, which dropped every named point the engineer had left shown (periodic audit 260916, B16). `schema-ids()` is the data access (§10bis).

⚠️ **Nothing computes.** A schema is a drawing, like a static model: no resistance, no polar moment, no check anywhere behind it, and deliberately no `{vars, steps, checks}` record — so nothing addresses one with `show-calc`, and nobody can mistake a figure for a traced calculation (§1). What *reads* it computes: a connector's shear resistance (§3.3nonies) and a polar moment (§3.3quinquies) are drawn on one, and each names the markers and the elements it takes.

### What consumes one (v0.74)

Two modules, and the same mechanism in both. A **connector** and a **polar-moment calculation** each name a `schema`, name its **connector markers** by id for their fasteners, and name one of its **elements** per member — through which a member reaches its position, and so its strength class and its moisture class. Neither carries geometry of its own any more; the drawing is read afresh at every export, so a marker dragged in the «Schémas» tab moves in every calculation drawn on that schema.

⚠️ **The kind of a marker does not enter into a polar moment.** A marker carries its kind (`connector.TYPES`' own) so that a drawing says roughly what it will be calculated as, but a polar moment distributes forces over *points*: a dowel and a screw are the same point to it. Only a connector's own calculation reads the kind, and it reads its own `type` rather than the markers'.

⚠️ **A polar moment takes construction points too (v0.77).** The engineer's own reading of the module: «Moments polaires» reads connector markers **and** points. A point drawn where a dowel goes is as good a fastener to a polar moment as a marker, since what sits on it does not enter into the calculation; `schema.fastener_places` offers the schema's markers first, then its aids of kind `point` ticked «connecteur» — never a centre, which is what a rotation centre is chosen from — and ids being unique across a schema's collections, the two never share one. *Nouveau* ticks every marker, or every point on a schema that carries no marker (`schema.default_fasteners`): both at once would count a marker drawn on a point twice. ⚠️ **A connector takes the same points (v0.77, the same version's reversal).** For an afternoon a connector took markers only, on the argument that its calculation reads the fastener's kind; it does not — it reads the connector's own `type` — and the engineer, whose A12 to A15 of VU-Skizze were ticked and could not be chosen in «Disposition des broches»: «des points doivent toujours pouvoir être repris par d'autres modules (pas uniquement Moments polaires)». So `schema.fastener_places` is the one list every module reads, `connection.fastener_places` and `connector.marker_points` both go through it, and a schema with neither a marker nor a ticked point is the one case the connectors tab explains in orange.

⚠️ **A schema may carry more than any one calculation takes.** Its elements may include a piece no member is, and its markers two calculations' worth; each calculation names what it takes, and the figure numbers only those. That is what lets one detail carry a beam, a column, a stiffener and two fastener groups without either calculation inheriting the other's.

⚠️ **What is missing warns and never stops** — the v0.33 rule (§13), applied here to a fifth and sixth editor. A schema renamed, a marker deleted, an element that no longer resolves, a centre that is gone: each is a warning naming what to do, the report refuses to print what it names, and Woodcore stays able to open the file and point it somewhere else. In particular an **empty** fastener list is a drawing with nothing ticked yet and not a malformed source — `check_source` lets it through, and the record says `calculated: false` with its reason.

### Modifying one (v0.73)

Six verbs, in `schema.py` as **pure functions of a source** — the canvas holds which entries are selected and the tab brackets one call in one undo step, and none of the geometry knows about either. That is `statics.py`'s arrangement for its own tracking, and it is what lets `tests/test_schema_edit.py` drive every verb without a window.

| verb | what it takes | |
|---|---|---|
| **déplacer** · **dupliquer** | two clicks: from where, to where | `translation(dx, dz)` |
| **tourner** | the centre, then the direction — the second click's bearing from the first, counted from +x towards +z | `rotation(centre, degrees)` |
| **symétrie** | the two points of the axis | `reflection(a, b)` |
| **trancher** | the elements selected, cut on the **one construction line selected with them** | `split_element(source, eid, aid)` |
| **fusionner** | the elements selected, joined into one | `merge_elements(source, ids)` |

**A modification acts on the ids it is given and on nothing else.** What *follows* rather than being acted on is what an anchor already buys: a dimension anchored to an aid point moves when that aid moves, whether or not the dimension is itself selected.

**Each entry is transformed by the shape it has.** An element's points, a connector marker's x/z, an aid point's place, a construction line's place *and its angle*, a two-point aid's two ends. A **dimension** keeps its `kind` — a horizontal one turned about a point still measures the x-distance between its ends, which is what choosing that kind said — while its *free* anchors move, its `bearing` turns, and a **mirror flips its `offset`**, a reflection reversing orientation so the dimension line stays on the same side of the geometry. ⚠️ A **direction under a mirror is folded about the axis** (2·axis − angle) and not turned by a constant, which is why `reflection` returns a function and not a number: mirroring a vertical line across a vertical axis has to leave it vertical.

⚠️ **`duplicate` deliberately does not copy a dimension**, even one in the selection. A dimension is anchored, and a copy would either name the originals' anchors — measuring the original, in the copy's place — or name nothing at all. Duplicating the geometry and re-dimensioning it is the honest order. A copy **keeps its element's position number**, its label and its section (`b`, `h` since v0.75), and takes a new id, ids being unique across a schema; the copies become the selection, so a second duplicate copies the copy, which is what laying out a row of identical pieces is.

⚠️ **Four quarter-turns are the identity, to the digit.** `round_place` is the rounding a click already applies, and both sides go through it — without that a rotation would walk a schema off its own grid over an afternoon's editing. The same holds for a mirror applied twice; `tests/test_schema_edit.py` asserts both against the coordinates they started from rather than against a tolerance.

**Trancher.** A linear element is cut at **every** crossing, so a polyline cut twice gives three pieces; an area is cut where the line **enters once and leaves once**, which is true of every convex piece and of a concave one the line does not re-enter, and anything else is refused **naming the count** rather than returning a shape nobody asked for. ⚠️ A **U** is the shape that exercises that and an **L** is not: whichever way a straight line is laid across an L it enters once and leaves once, so an L splits perfectly well. **The first piece keeps the element's own id**, so a dimension anchored to it still resolves; the rest take the next free ids. **Every piece carries the same position number**, and the same section (`b`, `h` since v0.75), view and label — a cut changes where a piece ends, not what it is.

**Fusionner.** The inverse, and held as one: a linear element's pieces are chained end to end, an area's are spliced on the edge they share, and the collinear vertex the cut left behind is taken out again — so cutting a piece and joining it gives back the shape it started from rather than the same shape carrying two vertices nobody drew. ⚠️ **Only elements of the same position number merge.** Two pieces of one beam are one beam; two different positions are two things, and joining them would put one number on what the workbook calls two. The refusal **names both numbers**.

**Every refusal carries a code.** `schema.Refused` holds both halves on purpose: `args[0]` is a sentence that stands on its own in English, for a caller with no window and for a test that asserts what went wrong, while `code` and `detail` are what Woodcore turns into its own three languages — a refusal an engineer reads in the status line is chrome and follows the window (§4bis). Neither is derived from the other: parsing the sentence back into a code is exactly the fragility the code exists to avoid.
