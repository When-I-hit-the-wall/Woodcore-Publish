# Connectors (fasteners) and their resistance

> A connector file and its SIA 265 6.2.1 shear resistance; the descriptive keys.
>
> Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md (§3.3nonies, §7bis) on 2026-10-07. The § numbers are kept as stable anchors: `docs/README.md` maps every § to its file.

### 3.3nonies The `connector` domain: entered in Woodcore, calculated from SIA 265 (v0.68)

A connector (Verbindungsmittel) is the fastener that joins the members meeting at a node, and since v0.68 it carries **its own calculation**: the design value of its shear resistance, SIA 265:2021 6.2.1. It is no longer a row of `Positionen.xlsx` — §7bis says what moved and why — but a file of its own, `Woodcore-Datei/connectors/<id>.json`, written by Woodcore's «Connecteurs» tab (§10quater) and read by `calc/domains/connector.py` alone. That is the connections' and the plans' arrangement, for the same reasons (§3.3quinquies, §13). `schemas/connector-source.schema.json` states the file's shape (`schema_version` 2.0).

**One connector is one file is one calculation.** To compare two layouts, duplicate the connector — `SD_01`, `SD_02` — which is what the engineer already did in Excel. There is deliberately no variant beneath a connector, unlike a polar-moment calculation: a connector *is* the thing the report names, and a second level would be a second id to keep in step. ⚠️ Two connectors may be drawn on **one** schema, and usually are: the schema is the joint, the connector a calculation of it.

**The geometry is a detail schema's since v0.74 (schema 2.0).** The source names the **`schema`** it is drawn on — a file of `details/` (§3.3decies) — and the **`fasteners`** are the **ids** of that schema's connector markers **or of its points ticked «connecteur»** (v0.77, `schema.fastener_places`), in the order they are numbered; each member names an **`element`** of it. The drawing fields (`b`, `h`, `view`, `angle`, `through`, `against`, `overhang`), the x/z table, `reference` and `orientation` are gone: the pieces are the schema's, read afresh at every export, and `connector_svg.pattern_of()` is that drawing with this connector's fasteners numbered on it. A polar-moment calculation drawn on the same schema therefore reads exactly the same coordinates, with no conversion and no second form to fill in — which is what the move was for (§2 rule 1).

**A member's timber is a reference, resolved afresh at every export** — and it may be written over without ceasing to be one. Since schema 2.0 the **element is the position reference**: the member names an element of the schema, that element names a row of `Positions Element`, and the row gives the strength class and the moisture class. `material` therefore says only whether that class is overridden, in one of two shapes where there were three: **`null`** follows the element's position, and **`{"grade": "GL32h"}`** overrides it, the override winning and *staying* through the next import. Naming a `position` here as well is refused — it would be a second place for what the element already says. This is the static model's magnitude pattern (§3.3octies) and it is here for the same reason: the connector follows `Positionen.xlsx` by itself, so **Importer Excel** is all it takes and there is no «Reprendre» button to forget to press — the button that does exist takes an override *back*. The **species** — Nadelholz or Laubholz, which picks the branch of SIA 265 Tabelle 22 — is never asked for: it is a property of the strength class and is read off `calc/norms/sia265-2021-tab08-vollholz.json` / `-tab09-brettschichtholz.json` with ρ_k, and the species a grade may carry are Tabelle 22's rows (`sia265.species_names()`).

**The moisture class is the connection's, and there is one (schema 1.1).** `moisture_class` sits at the top of the source: SIA 265 applies a single η_w to R_d,Verb (Tabelle 4), and `Verbindung.xlsm` states one class per sheet (`'Stabdübel (Holz-Holz)'!C13`, `'(Stahl-Holz)'!C35`). `null` takes it from the members' positions, **the worst governing** — class 3 is the wettest and carries the smallest η_w — and the record says in `moisture_from` which positions it saw; a number states it here and wins. A 1.0 source, which carried one class per member, is read as 1.1: the worst of its classes becomes the connection's, which is the value its own calculation used — and, carrying its own drawing like every source before 2.0, it is then refused and sent to `migrate_to_schemas.py` by name (v0.79; relabelled 2.0 until then, a real 1.0 file fell on «missing required field 'label'» instead, periodic audit 260916, B23).

⚠️ **`plates.thickness` left the source in schema 1.1**, and a member's plate still carries none. **The plate's bearing** (spec 2026-10-12-audit-issues-47-48-49, REQ-008, D-13, D-14, D-16) reads the connector's one `plate` block instead -- `{t, e1, e2, d0}`, each `{value, unit}` in mm or null, schema **2.2** (the versions are floors: links need 2.1, a plate 2.2), the plate's grade being the source's `steel`. `dowel.plate_bearing` computes SIA 263:2013 Tabelle 16 equation (76), F_b,Rd = 0.85 · e1/d0 · f_u · d · t / γM2 ≤ 2.4 · f_u · d · t / γM2, capped by 6.2.5 at 1.5 · f_y · d · t / γM2 (a Stabdübel in a plate is a Bolzen), with p1 − d0/2 for e1 where p1 < e1 + d0/2, times the plates a dowel bears on (both outer plates, or every slotted one); γM2 = 1.25 of 4.1.3; every coefficient in `calc/norms/sia263-2013-tab16-lochleibung.json` (`sia263.bearing()`). p1 and p2 are read off the drawing, along and across the force (`pattern_rows` at α and α + 90°); e2 < 1.0 d0 or p2 < 2.0 d0 warns, outside (76)'s premise. **F_b,Rd is a result of its own, `F_b_Rd`, beside R_d and never folded into it**: the engineer verifies it against the force on one dowel with `#verify` (`var-of("connector", id, "F_b_Rd")`). A Stahl-Holz connector without its plate stated is calculated as before and warns that the bearing is not checked. The section figure draws the plate at the **Schlitzbreite**, the classeur's own `t`.

### What is calculated, and where every number comes from

`calc/domains/dowel.py`, the first of one module per kind of fastener, chosen on the connector's `type`:

    R_d,Verb = k_alpha · k_red · n_tot · (p · k_beta) · sqrt(M_u,k · f_h,1,k · d) · eta_w · eta_t    (88)
    R_d      = R_d,Verb / n_tot

| quantity | clause |
|---|---|
| `M_u,k = 0,3 · f_u,k · d^2,6` | 6.1.4.4, equation (87) |
| `f_h,0,k`, `f_h,90,k`, interpolated across α | Tabelle 22, per member |
| `k_red`, and **1 where n = 1** | equation (86), and Tabelle 21 for the n = 1 column |
| `k_beta`, bilinear between the corners of Figur 45 | **Anhang A, Tabellen 45 and 46** |
| `p · k_beta = p_A · k_beta,1 + p_B · k_beta,2` | **6.2.1.1** — and not A.1.1, whose line misprints the second index |
| `K_ser`, `K_ser,Verb`, the 0,5 mm slip | Tabelle 25, 6.2.1.3 |
| the minimum spacings, and `L_min` | Tabelle 24 |
| `eta_w`, `eta_t`, the 0,75 of a truss node | Tabelle 4, 2.2.6, 5.3.6.2.2 |

**k_β is Anhang A's, for every configuration**, which 6.2.1.1 offers as the more accurate alternative to the simplified Tabelle 23 and which the engineer chose. Four are implemented, and exactly the four SIA 265 gives a row for: `Holz-Holz einschnittig` (124/125), `Holz-Holz zweischnittig` (120/121), `Stahl-Holz, Bleche aussen` (128) and `Stahl-Holz, Schlitzbleche` (129 to 131). ⚠️ `Holz-Holz mehrschnittig`, a single one-sided plate and `Holz-Beton` are named and **refused with the reason**: Tabellen 45 and 46 do not cover them and 6.1.1.11 sends such a configuration to testing, which is a different answer from «unknown assembly».

👉 **Every value of SIA 265 the calculation uses is read from `calc/norms/`** and written nowhere else (v0.77, reversing v0.68's line): a class's ρ_k and species, η_w, η_t, γ_M/η_M, k_α, the multiples of d of Tabelle 24, the K_ser coefficients — **and the coefficients inside the formulas**, the 0,082, 0,01, −0,3, 0,015 and 1,35/0,90 of Tabelle 22 (`sia265-2021-tab22-lochleibungsfestigkeit.json`), the 0,3 and 2,6 of (87) (`-6.1.4.4-fliessmoment`), the −0,1, 10 and 0,25 of (86) (`-6.1.4.2-k-red`), the corners of Tabellen 45 and 46 and the two conditions above (130)/(131) (`-tab45-kbeta-holz-holz`, `-tab46-kbeta-stahl-holz`). `dowel.py` keeps the *shape* of each formula and composes its printed formula strings from the same numbers the arithmetic uses; `tests/test_norms.py` fails if a coefficient is written into it again. **f_u,k** is the f_u of the dowel's steel grade in SIA 263 Tabelle 1 (`sn_en_14592.f_u_k(grade)`, thin band): SN EN 14592:2022 Anhang G, G.4 and Tabelle G.9 (`sn-en-14592-2022-stabduebel-werkstoff.json`) ask of a dowel's bar a yield stress of at least 235 N/mm² and give no tensile strength, so the grades offered are those of Tabelle 1 that meet it (S235 to S460), **S355 -- 510 N/mm² -- by default** (spec 2026-10-12-audit-issues-47-48-49, REQ-007, D-06, D-20). The grade is `dowel.steel` (a label the schema had since 2.0), and it only fills the value: f_u,k stays writable, a value typed over the grade's -- or one that follows a variable -- clears it, and the report credits SIA 263 Tabelle 1 and the grade only where the value is the grade's (`connector.dowel_steel`). Until 12.10.2026 the 510 was credited to SN EN 14592+A1:2012, an edition not in the library.

### Two places the engineer's own workbook differs, both settled for the norm

`Verbindung.xlsm` is the classeur this module replaces, and it computes both of its sheets correctly — every printed digit of both is reproduced by `tests/test_dowel.py`. Two things in it are nevertheless not what SIA 265 says, and neither has ever shown:

- **`t₂,ᵢ`, Holz-Holz einschnittig.** Tabelle 45 equations (125) carry `√(1/(1+β_f)) + 1`, member 2's own bracket; cells `O34`/`P34` write `√(β_f/(1+β_f)) + 1`, which is member 1's. The two are equal at β_f = 1 — every case that sheet has been used on — and part company as soon as the two members differ in strength class, in grain angle or in predrilling. On C24 against D30 the required thickness moves from 59,7 to 54,8 mm.
- **`k_red` at n = 1.** Equation (86) alone gives 0,915 at a₁ = 7 d; Tabelle 21 gives 1,0, there being nothing in front of a single fastener to reduce it. `'Stabdübel (Holz-Holz)'!C33/D33` does not guard it, though the Stahl-Holz sheet does.

And one the norm states and the workbook enforces only through the data validation of `'Stabdübel (Stahl-Holz)'!C20` (0.35 ≤ t₁/t₂ ≤ 0.5), which a typed-over value bypasses: Tabelle 46 equations **(130)** and **(131)** hold only *wenn t₂ ≥ 2 · t₁* resp. *wenn t₁ ≥ 0,35 · t₂*. Here the condition is reported, with both thicknesses — a **warning**, not a stop, the number still being the norm's own formula.

⚠️ **A third, settled in the workbook (v0.79).** `Verbindung.xlsm`'s K_ser,Verb (C62 Holz-Holz, C66 Stahl-Holz) took no η_w, where SIA 265 3.2.1.3 converts «die Tragwiderstände und die Steifigkeitswerte» of class 1 and Tabelle 25 is «für Feuchteklasse 1»; Python applied it (0,90 in class 2, 0,75 in class 3). The engineer had the two cells corrected — `*Daten!O29` and `*Daten!O24`, the η_w for the stiffness the sheet already computed and did not use — and `tests/test_dowel.py` holds the Holz-Holz sheet against the project's connector cell by cell, K_ser,Verb included (periodic audit 260916, B8). The reference template still carries the defect; `000-Audits/260917-hinweis-vorlage-stabduebel-kser.md` tells its maintainer.

### What warns, and what stops

A defect in the data stops the export (§3.6). Five things only warn, and the record still carries its whole descriptive half so Woodcore has something to repair — the v0.33 rule, which is that stopping would stop the positions' own export with it:

- a member whose **position no longer resolves**, or whose position is not timber (`S002` carries `S355`);
- a **kind of fastener with no calculation module** yet — the connector is described and not calculated;
- a **calculation that refuses** because the norm gives no answer for what was asked (hardwood that is not predrilled). ⚠️ The line is worth stating: `check_source` refuses a **malformed source** and stops, because that is a defect in the data; a calculation refusing means *the norm has no value for this*, and that is a sentence to print, not a reason to stop every other connector's export.
- a **schema or a ticked marker the schema no longer carries** (v0.79, periodic audit 260916, B2): «Das Schema … gibt es nicht mehr», «… trägt die Verbindungsmittel … nicht mehr» — the arrangement is incomplete, so nothing is calculated over what is left;
- a **dowel pattern below the minimum spacings of Tabelle 24**, which 6.2.1.1 makes equation (88) conditional on. It is said loudly -- the resistance is still calculated, and the warning is printed under it from level 2 -- and left to the engineer: the fix is a layout to move, not data to correct.

Each of the first four sets `calculated: false` with a `reason`, and `show-connector-resistance` refuses to print a resistance nobody produced, saying that reason instead; the fifth leaves the resistance calculated and carries its warning.

### The export

`data/connectors.json` (`schema_version` **3.0**, `schemas/connectors.schema.json`). 3.0 (v0.74) is the move to a detail schema: the geometry is named rather than carried, so `schema` replaces `reference` and `orientation`, a fastener carries its marker's id beside its place, and a member names an element instead of a section. Before it, 2.1 added the identity's `name` and `variant` — the two halves the default id is composed of — and the connection's one `moisture_class` with the `moisture_from` that says where it came from; each timber member also carries `source_kind`, `position_id` and `position_grade`, the structured form of its provenance, so that Woodcore can write the orange line under an overridden field in its own language rather than translate a sentence with an id inside it. Every entry keeps the **ten descriptive keys schema 1.0 had**, under the same names and types — which is what bought `connector-summary` in `lib/calc-engine.typ`, `show-connector` and `build_reference`'s Connectors sheet not having to change at all for the move — and adds beside them the ordinary `{vars, steps, checks, tables, results}` record (§2 rule 2), the resolved members, the fasteners, `calculated`/`reason`, the `warnings` the report prints, and `figures`. A value the engineer types on a connector — d, n_tot, f_u,k, α, b, t_i, n, a_1 — carries the source `woodcore:connectors/<id>.json` (`katalog:` until v0.96); nothing reads the prefix back, and the frozen data of `examples/` and `tests/projet-exemple/` keep the old one until they are frozen again.

`results` are `R_d` and `R_d_Verb`, **in that order** (v0.84): equation (88) without n_tot *is* the resistance of one fastener, which is what an engineer reads off the page, so R_d is what the chain ends on and `R_(d,"Verb") = n_"tot" dot R_d` is the line after it. The two numbers are the norm's own either way; what changed is which of them the report derives from the other, and so which of them carries the working. Where the connection is a **truss node** both are reduced — `R_d_Fachwerk` and `R_d_Verb_Fachwerk`, second answers beside the first two and never a factor folded into them, because 5.3.6.2.2 is narrower than the word «truss» (the engineer, v0.84: «il faut calculer un R_d,Verb,Fachwerk ainsi qu'un R_d,Fachwerk»). `checks` is empty and stays empty: there is no design force here, and the report states F_d and the verification itself with `#verify` reading `var-of("connector", id, "R_d")`.

### The norm's formulas, written out (v0.84)

Until v0.84 the record carried **four tables**, and three of them held nothing but the numbers a formula of SIA 265 had produced: *Lochleibungsfestigkeit* (Tabelle 22), *Hilfswert $k_beta$* (Tabellen 45/46) and *Verschiebungsmodul* (Tabelle 25). The engineer asked for the formulas instead — «au lieu de faire un tableau qui n'affiche que les résultats, reprends les formules … et écris-les dans le rapport» — so each of the three is a chain of **steps** now, every one of them `inline` (§10bis): the formula in letters, the same formula in numbers, and the answer, on one row.

| what | what the report prints |
|---|---|
| **Tabelle 22**, per member | `f_(h,0,i)` and `f_(h,90,i)` with the table's own 0.082, 0.01, 1.35/0.90 and 0.015, then `f_(h,i,k)` interpolated across α. A member that is **not predrilled** gets one line and not three: that row of the table gives one value for both directions, so the angle does not enter and there is nothing to interpolate. |
| **Tabellen 45/46**, per member or shear plane | `β_f`, then `k_(β,max) = sqrt(4 β_f/(1+β_f))`, then the required thicknesses `t_(i,1)`, `t_(i,2)` and the corner values `k_(β,i,1)`, `k_(β,i,2)` — each with the bracket of the row it was read off, which the calculation records on the corner as its `shape` — and then `k_(β,i)` as **the branch of Figur 45 the thickness actually falls in**: interpolated between its corners, proportional from the origin below the lower one, or flat past the upper one. Printing the branch that applied is the rule `show-calc` already follows for the snow load's own conditional formula (§3.3bis). |
| **Tabelle 25**, per member | `K_("ser",i) = (c_0 − (c_0 − c_90) α_i/90) · ρ_(k,i)^0.5 · d^1.7 · η_(w,Steif)`, then `K_"ser" = min(…)` where there are two, then `K_("ser","Verb") = n_"tot" · p · K_"ser"`. ⚠️ **η_w is inside the per-member formula**, not on the connection's (the engineer: «il faut toujours multiplier le résultat avec eta_w»): the product is the same either way and this is where the report can *say* it. K_ser,Verb is **level 1**, printed under R_d,Verb at every level. |

⚠️ **The formulas written out are Tabellen 45 and 46's, and not Tabelle 23's**, which the engineer named. Tabelle 23 is the *simplified* k_β that 6.2.1.1 offers as an alternative, and this module has computed the more accurate Anhang A form since v0.68 at his own choice; printing Tabelle 23 would put formulas on the page that do not produce the numbers beside them, which is the one thing this display layer exists to prevent (§13).

⚠️ **Where each member's strength class came from** — «Bauteil 1: C24, aus Position T001» — was the note under the Lochleibungsfestigkeit table and is the **description of that member's own ρ_k** now: one sentence about one var, translated on its own, which is the rule AUD-3-7 was fixed to in v0.79 and is unchanged.

So **two `tables` are left**, each carrying the level and the side it belongs on: *Hilfswert $k_beta$*, which is now a **plate of figures with no grid at all** — SIA 265's Figur 45, one per thickness, whose numbers are the formulas above it (§3.3) — and *Minimale Abstände* (Tabelle 24, **level 1**, below). ⚠️ **Since the end of v0.77 Tabelle 24 is laid out as `Verbindung.xlsm` lays it out** ('Stabdübel (Holz-Holz)'!A47:E53): a row per distance, its value in mm and its multiple of d under «in Faserrichtung» and «senkrecht zur Faserrichtung» (two column groups), and the minimum length of the connection in both directions under them. The «vorhanden» and «Nachweis» columns are gone — the engineer could not tell how the comparison was made, which was the smallest gap between two markers along the schema's x and z — and so is *Lage der Verbindungsmittel*, the pattern figure showing the fasteners; a gap below Tabelle 24 is still a warning (`dowel.check_spacings`). ⚠️ **Its top-left cell names the diameter** (v0.84, the engineer): every cell of that table is a multiple of d and the table said nowhere which d, so the first column carries a `group` of its own, «$d$ = 10 mm», set on the line where the two directions are named — the cell that was empty. A symbol in a table's title, a text cell, a group's name or a note is written between dollars (`Hilfswert $k_beta$`, `$K_("ser","Verb")$`, `$7 d$`), which `render-table` sets as math (§4bis) and Woodcore as rich text.

**Level -1 (v0.117, issue #20).** `#show-connector-resistance(id, level: -1, shown: (R_d: 1, table-2: 1))` prints the headline and the connector's line as a title, the figures where a key is chosen at 2 or 3, and of the calculation what `show-calc` prints at -1: the vars and steps named -- a step whole, its notes from 2 --, the tables named by their place, a result not named not printed. The keys are the export's own, offered as boxes in the tab's «Affichage».

### The layout of level 3, after the Bemessungsbeispiele (v0.84)

Writing the norm's formulas out put some twenty-five of them on the page one under the other, and the engineer read it and said so: «le level 3 est trop brouillon». What he asked for is the presentation of **Bemessungsbeispiele zur SIA 265, p. 291** — the same calculation, set as that book sets it: a line naming what is about to be calculated, then the formulas of that block, then the next name. Nothing about the numbers changed.

The blocks, in the order the report prints them, are the book's own:

| heading | what stands under it |
|---|---|
| **Charakteristische Lochleibungsfestigkeiten für C24** | each member's ρ_k on one row, then Tabelle 22's chain per member. The grade is the one the members share; where they do not share one the block is named without it, the same sentence with its hole taken out |
| **Verhältnis der Lochleibungsfestigkeiten** | β_f (Holz-Holz only) |
| **Holzdicken** | the pieces' thicknesses on one row — a block of its own, where until v0.84 they stood in the material row and the two read as one undifferentiated line |
| **Erforderliche Holzdicken und Hilfswert $k_beta$** | k_β,max, then per piece its corners and the branch of Figur 45 its thickness falls in, each piece named on a line of its own |
| **Reduktionsbeiwert** · **Fliessmoment** · **Bemessungswert des Tragwiderstands** · **Verschiebungsmodul** | k_red per member; M_u,k, which only equation (88) reads and which therefore stands with it and not before k_β; the factors on one row and then R_d and R_d,Verb; Tabelle 25's chain |

⚠️ **The piece is named «Bauteil 1 — Seitenholz»**, the very form the two Figur 45 plates are captioned with: the book writes «Seitenhölzer» and «Mittelholz», and the drawing numbers its members, so the heading says both. Which piece is which is the corners' own answer — a Mittelholz's lower corner sits at the origin — and where the role tells the two apart in no way (a **einschnittig** joint, whose pieces are both Seitenhölzer) the number stands alone. A shear plane is never named this way: the letter that tells two of them apart is in every symbol of its run (`t_(A,1)`, `k_(β,A,1)`), where a member's word is in none of its.

⚠️ **The two Figur 45 plates are kept**, which the engineer asked for in the same message, and so is Tabelle 24: they are the record's two remaining `tables` and they sit below the values (§3.3).

⚠️ **What the report does not copy from the book** is its third column, where the answer of every line is set flush right — `= 25,3 N/mm²`, `= 1,20`. The right-hand column of this report is the citation column (§10), and a second one would either displace it or halve the width a formula is set in.

**Two figures**, `data/connectors/<id>-*.svg`. The **pattern** — the members and the numbered fasteners — is `connection_svg`'s own drawing, called with `legend=False, show_center=False`: a connector carries no N, V or M and turns about nothing. It is drawn once per language all the same (§4bis): it carries no word of the engine's but the note under it where a fastener is drawn larger than its d, so its three files are identical otherwise -- as a schema's are (§3.3decies). The **section** is new, `connector_svg.section()`: the pieces the fastener passes through side by side along its axis, the shear planes between them lettered A and B where Tabelle 46 letters them, the widths proportional to the real thicknesses and the dimensions written with their values — or kept as `t₁`, `t₂`, `Ø d` while a thickness is still open, which is what lets Woodcore show the figure before anything has been typed. It carries no words, so one file serves all three languages: where d is drawn at a bound rather than to scale, the record's **`section_scaled`** says `enlarged` or `reduced`, and the report writes «Ø agrandi, pas à l'échelle» under the figure in its own language, Woodcore in the window's (v0.79; the figure wrote «Ø vergrössert, nicht massstäblich» into every report until then, periodic audit 260916, B13).

**Figur 45, one figure per thickness (v0.77)**, `data/connectors/<id>-kbeta-<i>.svg`. The engineer: «k_β est calculé d'après une formule, ce n'est pas une valeur fixe à 1.34» — it is read off the bilinear line of SIA 265 Figur 45 between the corners of Tabellen 45/46, and level 3 now shows that line: «toujours 2 graphiques, 1 qui relate t₁ et 1 qui relate t₂». `dowel._k_beta_table` states a `curve` per row — a Holz-Holz joint's Bauteil 1 and 2, a slotted-plate joint's Scherfuge A and B, outer plates' one timber — with its corners, the thickness the calculation reads and the k_β it reads there; `connector.figures` draws each with `connector_svg.k_beta_curve` and replaces the curves by `figures` [{file, index, label, role}] on that very table, so the numbers stay the table's rows and are not exported twice. On each: k_βi against t_i, the Seitenholz's two slopes or the Mittelholz's single dashed line from the origin (its lower corner *is* the origin), flat past t_i,2; every corner and the point read written as «symbol = value unit» — `t_(1,1) = 26.9 mm`, `k_(β1,2) = 1.414` — the corners in grey, the thickness read and its k_β in blue, the labels stepped into rows or moved apart with a leader where two would meet. No word is on it, so one file serves the three languages; `render-table` sets a table's `figures` under the table, side by side, each named by the glossary («Bauteil 1 — Seitenholz», «Pièce 1 — bois latéral»).

### In Typst

`show-connector-resistance(connector, level:, title:, width:)` (§10bis). Level 1 names the connection and gives R_d, R_d,Verb and, under them, the connection's **K_ser,Verb** (v0.84, the engineer: it is not one of the `results`, which are the resistances a verification reads, and it is what a deformation calculation needs next), with Tabelle 24 below; level 2 adds the pattern and the section **side by side, each a figure with its caption** (v0.77, the engineer's request — until then one under the other, uncaptioned, the section from level 3); level 3 delegates the whole chain to `show-calc("connector", id)`, so the detail cannot drift from the data — Figur 45 included, which the k_β plate carries and `render-table` sets under it. `connector-ids()` is the data access and `show-connector` still prints the descriptive line, unchanged.

⚠️ **A latent defect of the engine came out here and was fixed at the source.** `substituted-rhs` replaced each operand's symbol by plain string replacement, so a var whose symbol is `d` — the norm's own symbol for a diameter — rewrote the `d` of every `dot` in the formula, and `0.3 dot f_(u,k) dot d^2.6` came out as `0.3 12ot …`, which does not even evaluate. Longest-first does not help: `dot` is not an operand. A symbol made only of word characters is now replaced on **word boundaries**; one carrying anything else (`f_(h,1,k)`, `mu_11`) keeps the plain replace, since it cannot be a substring of an operator name. Verified by regenerating all 167 frozen example pages: **not one changed**.

## 7bis. Connectors: entered in Woodcore (v0.33, moved out of Excel in v0.68)

A connector (Verbindungsmittel) is the fastener that joins the members meeting at a node. From v0.33 to v0.67 it was described like an element and entered like one, as a row of `Positionen.xlsx`'s **`Positions Connectors`** sheet; since v0.68 it is **a file of its own**, `Woodcore-Datei/connectors/<id>.json`, written by Woodcore's «Connecteurs» tab and read by `calc/domains/connector.py` (§3.3nonies, §13).

The reason is the one the v0.33 decision could not have anticipated: a connector now carries a *calculation*, not just a description. Its resistance needs the two members' thicknesses, their grain angles, their predrilling and the fasteners' coordinates — none of which is a column of a sheet, and all of which Woodcore already draws for a polar moment. Splitting a connector between a row in Excel and a drawing in the window would have been two sources for one thing (§2 rule 1).

**What did not change is the descriptive half.** The ten keys below are exported under exactly the names and types schema 1.0 used, so `show-connector`, `connector-ids()` and `Sections/003-baustoffe.typ` did not change a line for the move — the connector still prints as `SD001: Stabdübel, Holz-Holz zweischnittig, Ø 12 mm, L = 160 mm, S235, vorgebohrt (Bauteile: T001, S001)`. What changed is where each comes from: `connector._descriptive()` builds them from the source, and two of them are derived rather than typed.

| Key | Where it comes from | |
|---|---|---|
| `position` | the source's `id`, which is its file name: letters, digits and `_ . + -` (`connector.ID_PATTERN`) | required |
| `type` | the kind of fastener, one of `connector.TYPES` (Stabdübel, Passbolzen, Bolzen, Voll-/Teilgewindeschraube, Nagel, Klammer, eingeklebte Gewindestange, Dübel besonderer Bauart), each with the symbol its id starts with | required |
| `assembly` | the kind of assembly, one of `dowel.ASSEMBLIES` or of the configurations SIA 265 gives no k_β for (`dowel.UNCOVERED_ASSEMBLIES`, refused naming 6.1.1.11, §3.3nonies) | required |
| `diameter` | d in mm, `{value, unit}` — what the resistance is calculated with | required |
| `length` | the fastener's length in mm, `{value, unit}` or `null` — descriptive, no resistance reads it | optional |
| `dimension` | any other dimension, as text — descriptive, never parsed, like an element's (§7) | optional |
| `steel` | the grade of the steel plates of a Stahl-Holz joint, from SIA 263 Tabelle 1 (`calc/norms/sia263-2013-tab01-baustaehle.json`), or empty — always empty for a Holz-Holz joint, which has no plate (v0.77); a Stabdübel carries no steel grade, only its f_u,k in its type block (§3.3nonies) | optional |
| `predrilled` | **derived**: true when every timber member is predrilled, false when none is, `null` when they differ or one is unstated (`connector._predrilled`) | derived |
| `elements` | **derived**, written by Woodcore as it saves: the positions of the schema elements the timber members are, so «(Bauteile: T001, S001)» is never typed — it is the list of the last save, not read again at export: an element's position changed in «Schémas» reaches it when the connector is saved again | derived |
| `description` | free text | optional |

The three lists that were sheets of `Positionen.xlsx` are code and norm now: `data_connector` is `connector.TYPES`, `data_assembly` is `dowel.ASSEMBLIES`, and `data_steel` is `calc/norms/sia263-2013-tab01-baustaehle.json` and `-tab02-schrauben.json`. `export_all.py` warns on every run while the workbook still holds a `Positions Connectors`, `data_connector`, `data_assembly` or `data_steel` sheet: nothing reads them, and a sheet left behind would read as a second source (§13). The engineer deleted the four sheets on 17.09.2026 (v0.79), so the warning no longer fires.

An id that does not start with its type's symbol is only a **warning**, as an element's id is not checked at all (§14).

In Typst, `connector-ids()` gives the connectors in file-name order and `show-connector(id, description:)` prints one on a line, leaving out what the source leaves empty (§10bis). `Sections/003-baustoffe.typ` lists them under *Verbindungsmittel*, after the elements, with the same loop as the elements' — so a connector saved in Woodcore is in the report with no Typst edit.
