### 3.3quinquies Connections: drawn on a schema, geometry in Python, forces in Typst (v0.31; frame, members and figure v0.32; the geometry moved to a schema in v0.74)

⚠️ *Retired by the spec 2026-10-21-commandes-typst-refonte (lot 19): `show-polar-moment` -- write #include domain-file("connection", id) -- its file, written out by the export, printed where the section includes it, N, V and M written in it by the engineer. What follows describes it as it was.* **Laid out by folders (v0.119, issue #64):** a polar moment has its file, `Connections/<id>.typ`, a stub that prints it -- `#show-polar-moment("<id>")`, the forces left to the engineer to write (calc/domains/domain_file.py).

**The file of a polar moment and its figure, now (spec 2026-10-21-commandes-typst-refonte, lot 13; spec 2026-10-22-image-polar-moment).** The export writes `Connections/<id>.typ` out (`calc/domains/unfold.py`, `_generate_connection`): its `#domain`, the members it joins, its geometry as the export worked it out (the centre, the angles, n, I_p, the table of the fasteners), then N, V and M as `#var` at 0 for the engineer to give -- his own once he changes them, or rewrites one as an `#eq` of the report's forces (`#eq("N", `N_Ed`, …, id: "N")`, which the base of the regeneration takes as his line, «own», and «Régénérer» keeps in its place) --, and right after them, on a labelled line of its own (`<id-polar-figure>`), the figure drawn with its arrows: `#figure(image-polar-moment("<id>", N: "N", V: "V", M: "M", width: 60%), kind: image, caption: [Anschluss <id>: … – Verbindungsmittel und Kräfte je Verbindungsmittel])`. It names the lines above, so the arrows follow the values the engineer writes there; a group with no lever arm writes the call without `M:`. Then, by `#eq`, the components of N and V, each fastener's F_x,i, F_z,i and F_i and F_max -- what the report prints and what a `#verify` names. `#image-polar-moment` draws the arrows below exactly as `#show-polar-moment` did, working the distribution out again for them (`polar-distribution`), and publishes nothing; tests/test_arrows.py holds its forces equal to `connection.fastener_forces` and to the file's `#eq`, and its SVG equal to `connection_svg.force_arrows` element for element. A file written before the figure moved -- the figure, a plain image, at its head -- no longer matches its form (the form's last `-a`): it shows the red «forme» line, the export does not rewrite it, and «Régénérer» takes the head figure out and writes the new one after the forces (ARCH-003).

A connection (Anschluss) — the group of fasteners joining the members that meet at a node — is not read from a workbook. It is defined in Woodcore's «Moments polaires» tab (§10quater) and saved as its **source**, `Woodcore-Datei/connections/<id>.json`, one file per connection, which Woodcore writes and nothing else does; `calc/domains/connection.py` is the only thing that reads it. That file plays the part a load's sheet plays, which answers the question §14 left open for this tab. Its id is its file name and the string the report addresses it by, so it is kept to letters, digits and `_ . + -` (`SD001-V1`); the two must agree, and a file whose id is not its name is refused. `schemas/connection-source.schema.json` states the file's shape (`schema_version` 4.0).

**A connection names no connector (v0.76).** From v0.33 to v0.75 every connection was a calculation of one connector: its id was `<connector>-<variant>` (freed to be written over in v0.75), the report printed the connector under its heading, and `linked-connector()` refused a calculation of none. A polar moment distributes forces over points and has no use for what fastens them, so the engineer removed the link (§13). The source has a **name**, in free text, and its id is made from it by `connection.id_from_name()` — the connectors' NFKD slug with no variant, `Anschluss Nord` → `Anschluss_Nord`, `Élément 2` → `Element_2` — and may be written over. A 3.0 or 3.1 file is read as 4.0 by `upgrade_source()`: its `connector` and `variant` dropped, its name the one it states or else its id, its id kept as it is, so nothing is written and no call changes. A 4.0 file still carrying either field is refused by name, a field nothing reads being one somebody would believe. What fastens the joint is the connector's own calculation (§3.3nonies), and where the two are the same joint they are drawn on the same schema — which is what they share now, and all they need to.

**The frame is the schema's (v0.74), and was the reference member's until v0.73.** Coordinates are in mm, the origin where the schema puts it, x to the right and z down — the detail schema's own frame (§3.3decies), which is why a schema is consumed with no conversion at all. Until v0.73 x ran along the grain of the **reference member**, and that member therefore fixed the frame; a schema is drawn as it was drawn, so the schema fixes it now and `reference` says only which member the report calls the Bezugselement and counts the others' angles from. The figure draws the x/z pair at the start of that member's element, as a member's local z is drawn in RFEM.

⚠️ **A member therefore carries two angles, and they are not the same thing.** `local` is its grain from the reference member's — θ_(r,j), what `show-polar-moment` prints as the angle between two members — and `axis` its grain from the **schema's x**, which is what a force-grain angle is measured against, the force's own components being in that frame. Until v0.73 the two were one number by construction, the reference member's grain *being* x; they agree on every detail drawn along x, which is every detail this project has, and a detail drawn at an angle parts them. A record carrying one number for both would then be wrong for one of them, which is why it carries both. Every angle — the directions of N and V, a member's `local` and `axis` angles — is counted from x towards z, which is clockwise on the drawing, so V at 90° points along +z, down. M is positive the other way, turning z towards x (M_y of a right-handed frame), which is anticlockwise on the drawing: a moment written in the report for v0.31 keeps its physical sense. The same conventions hold in the source, the figure and the report.

What a source holds since **schema 3.0** (v0.74): the **`schema`** it is drawn on — a file of `details/` — and from two to six **members** (`MIN_MEMBERS`, `MAX_MEMBERS`), each naming an **`element`** of that schema and nothing else but what the report calls it; the `reference` member; the **`fasteners`**, the **ids** of the schema's connector markers **or construction points** (aids of kind `point`, v0.77) this calculation carries, in the order they are numbered; the **rotation centre**, `centroid` (the fasteners' centroid, what the elastic method assumes for fasteners of equal stiffness), `manual` (an x and z the engineer fixes) or `aid` (a **centre drawn in the schema**, named by its id — what the schema's own «Centre» tool is for, v0.74; one the schema no longer carries falls back to the centroid with a warning rather than putting the whole calculation out of reach over one renamed point); the **directions** N and V act in; and the **reference** cited.

⚠️ **The member form and the x/z table are gone**, and with them `orientation`: the pieces and the coordinates are the schema's, read afresh at every export, so moving a marker in the «Schémas» tab moves it in every calculation drawn on that schema. That is the whole of what v0.74 bought — one physical joint drawn once, where *Moments polaires* and *Connecteurs* each carried their own form of it (§2 rule 1). ⚠️ **What the schema does not carry warns and never stops**: a schema renamed, a marker deleted, an element that no longer resolves, a centre that is gone — each is a warning naming what to do, the report refuses to print what it names, and Woodcore stays able to open the file and point it somewhere else (§13, the v0.33 rule). ⚠️ **Since v0.79 the record says so itself** (`data/connections.json` **4.1**): `calculated` is false and `reason` names what is missing — no schema, a schema the export does not list, a fastener or an element lost, nothing ticked — and `find-calculation` and `show-polar-moment` refuse such a record with that reason (`connection.calculation_state`); a centre lost is not among them, the centroid replacing it. Until then the report printed a polar moment over the fasteners that were left (periodic audit 260916, B2). A new connection cites `bauforumstahl, Arbeitshilfe: Tragwerksplanung im Stahlbau – Gelenkige Anschlüsse` (`DEFAULT_NORM`), a document rather than a norm clause — the article itself is in the reference library, `ExternalFiles/104-MomentPolaire/` (§11bis), with the course document it was given with: `norm` is required, `norm_ref` may be left empty, and a citation without a clause is printed without its `§` (§2 rule 7). The members' sections only draw the figure: nothing calculated depends on them.

⚠️ **A source that still carries its own drawing is not converted as it is read.** A 1.0, 2.0 or 2.1 file holds its members and its coordinates, and turning one into a 3.0 file means **writing a second file** — the schema that drawing becomes — which a reader must not do quietly: it would put a `details/<id>.json` on disk nobody asked for, and do it again differently the next time. `calc/tools/migrate_to_schemas.py` is the one thing that converts them, once and deliberately, and `check_source` refuses anything it has not been through, naming it. What that script does with a v0.31 source (schema 1.0: x right and y up on the drawing, every angle anticlockwise, exactly two members) is what `upgrade_source()` did until v0.73: into the frame of its member 1, which becomes the reference — x = x′ cos φ₁ + y′ sin φ₁ and z = x′ sin φ₁ − y′ cos φ₁, the angles changing sign, a non-through member stopping flush against the other. Nothing about the connection changes, only how it is written down, so the forces the report prints for it are the same. A v0.32 source (schema 2.0) goes through the same script.

⚠️ *Retired by the spec 2026-10-21-commandes-typst-refonte (lot 19): `show-calc` -- write #include domain-file(kind, id) -- a load ("snow-load", "wind-load", "dead-load", "live-load"), a connector, a polar moment ("connection"), a script: its file, written out by the export, printed where the section includes it; `var-of` -- write #var(name, domain: (domain, instance)) to read a value of the export, get(name, "value") for its number. What follows describes it as it was.* **Python calculates the geometry, and only the geometry.** From the coordinates and the rotation centre, `connection.py` computes I_p,x = Σ Δx_i², I_p,z = Σ Δz_i² and I_p = I_p,x + I_p,z in mm² — every fastener counted as a unit point, the discrete counterpart of a polar moment of inertia, hence mm² and not mm⁴; the naming follows the engineer's own Verbindungen workbook, where I_p,x sums the x distances. It also gives every member's angle from the reference member's grain (θ_(r,j), its `local` angle) and from the schema's x (its `axis` angle). A connection enters **`data/connections.json`** (`schema_version` 4.1) as the `{vars, steps, checks, tables, results}` record every calculation has (§2 rule 2), plus the schema it is drawn on, the geometry the report distributes forces over and a `figure` block — the figure's file, the fasteners' positions in its coordinates, the directions of x and z on it, the length of its longest force arrow and the sizes the arrows are drawn at — and it is read under the domain **`connection`**: `show-calc("connection", "A01")`, `var-of("connection", "A01", "I_p")`, `ids("connection")` or `ids("connection")` all work through the same lookup (`find-calculation`), which checks for a connection before it checks for a position, a connection id possibly looking like one, and applies no `calc-block` scope guard, a connection belonging to no position. The vars' levels serve `show-calc("connection", id)`, which prints the record on its own: n and I_p from level 2; at level 3 also the rotation centre, the angles between the members, the directions of N and V, the table *Lage der Verbindungsmittel* (Nr, x, z, Δx, Δz, Δx², Δz²) whose totals row names I_p,x and I_p,z — the dead load's pattern (§3.3ter), no Σ restating the rows — and I_p = I_p,x + I_p,z in numbers. It is exported to its own file rather than into `calculations.json` so that it can be exported alone: Woodcore does exactly that when a connection is saved, without the load workbooks being read again (§13). `schemas/connections.schema.json` states the export's shape, `validation.validate_connections_business()` its rules.

**The figure is drawn once**, by `calc/domains/connection_svg.py`, as SVG text — and since v0.74 it is **the schema's own drawing plus this connection's layers**: `schema_svg.body()` draws the pieces, the construction aids the schema left shown and its dimensions, and this module adds the numbered fasteners, the members' axes (which of the schema's pieces this calculation calls its members, and the line θ and the force-grain angles are measured along), the rotation centre, the x/z pair, the moment's arc, the legend and the force arrows. `connector_svg.pattern_of` goes through the same `overlay()`, so the connector's pattern and the connection's figure are the same detail with different layers on it rather than two drawings to keep in step. ⚠️ A member's label is therefore the **schema's** — «T001 C24 100/200», the position, its strength class and the element's own section (v0.75); in v0.74 it read `T001` alone, and until v0.73 `T001 100/**400**` with the drawn dimension bold. the x/z pair at the start of the reference member; the fasteners, numbered as the report's tables number them; the rotation centre (⊕, and the centroid as × beside it when a fixed point is used); M as a green arc under the centre, from south-west to south-east, turning anticlockwise as a positive M does, its "M" at the tail (v0.41, v0.50), drawn at the radius that keeps it clear of the force arrows the report adds later — every fastener claims a disc of `arrow` and its label, and the arc runs beyond the farthest point those discs reach along the quarter it spans, capped at `MOMENT_MAX` of the widest member, where it may cross an arrow again; it is drawn over the schema's drawing, the members' labels included, and under the fasteners; and a legend whose N and V arrows point the way N and V act, whose F_M line stands for the force from the moment and whose M is drawn as the arc itself. Every action — N/n, V/n, F_M and the moment's arc — is drawn at `ACTION_WIDTH`, and the resultant they add up to at `RESULTANT_WIDTH`, which is 1.5 times it and outlined besides: the two are tied in one place (and mirrored in `lib/calc-engine.typ`'s `action-width` and `resultant-width`), so the resultant cannot end up the thinner of the two (v0.50). Woodcore shows it live while a connection is edited, and the export writes it to `data/connections/<id>.svg` (in a project laid out by folders, since v0.119, `Connections/Exports/Figures/`, named in the JSON from the Typst root -- issue #64, docs/architecture/layout.md §11), removing the figure of a connection that no longer exists. **The force arrows are the call's**, since only the call knows N, V and M: the exported figure carries a `<!-- forces -->` marker where they belong, and `show-polar-moment` -- `#image-polar-moment` since the spec 2026-10-22-image-polar-moment, with the same drawing -- reads the SVG, puts in at every fastener its share of N (blue), its share of V (red), the force the moment puts on it (green: M·r_i/I_p at right angles to the lever arm, the V_b,i of the Arbeitshilfe's Bild 3, v0.50) and its resultant F_i (yellow, outlined, labelled F1, F2, …), all at one scale — an F of 20 kN is twice as long as an N/n of 10 kN — and sets the result in the report. There is no exception to that scale (v0.50): every arrow is drawn at the length its force gives it, so the three actions on a fastener add up, head to tail, to the resultant beside them — which is the first thing a checker does with such a figure. A share can then come out too small to make out, an N/n of 0.06 kN beside an F of 10.7 kN being 1/170 of it, hidden under the fastener's dot; it is drawn all the same, and the figure's second marker, `<!-- note -->`, in the legend's last line, receives *Zu klein zum Erkennen: N/n = 0.06 kN, V/n = 0.31 kN*, so the value is on the page even where the arrow cannot be read (`SHARE_SHOWN`, `share-shown`, about the dot's own radius). Stretching such a share to a minimum length, which v0.32 did, broke that addition — by a factor of 86 on the project's own figure — and leaving it out would break it by the length left out. Woodcore draws its trial forces and note into its preview the same way, with `connection_svg.force_arrows()` and `share_note()`, which the Typst helpers (`fastener-shares`, `fastener-arrows`, `share-note`, `svg-arrow`) follow rule for rule. So the drawing the fasteners were placed on is the drawing the checker reads, with the report's own forces on it. It is kept to what both renderers read alike — QtSvg implements SVG Tiny 1.2, which has no `<marker>`, no arc command and no CSS — so arrowheads are polygons, the arc is a polyline and every colour is a presentation attribute; and the numbers Typst writes into it carry a hyphen for their sign, Typst's `str()` writing a typographic minus that an SVG parser does not read (every arrow of a fastener above the node would vanish without a word).

What follows describes it as it was.* **Typst distributes the forces.** `show-polar-moment(connection, N:, V:, M:, level:, title:, width:)` — `show-fastener-forces` until v0.32 and `show-fastener-polar-moment` until v0.38, each time renamed without an alias — takes the total normal and shear forces in kN and the moment about the rotation centre in kNm, and computes for every fastener F_x,i = (N_x + V_x)/n + M·Δz_i/I_p, F_z,i = (N_z + V_z)/n − M·Δx_i/I_p and F_i = √(F_x,i² + F_z,i²) — N and V shared equally, M in proportion to the distance to the rotation centre and at right angles to it, the three added as vectors. It is the one calculation this engine carries out rather than reads, and deliberately so (§13): N, V and M are written in the report until load combinations export them. A force may be passed as a number or as a var object, whose unit is then checked, so `N: var-of("combinations", ...)` will need no change. The most stressed fastener is the one with the largest resultant; where several carry it — to a relative 1e-9, rounding noise not being a difference — all of them are named, a run of consecutive numbers as a range (`Massgebend: Verbindungsmittel 1–4`, or `1, 3, 13, 15`), and the working is shown for the first. Its force-grain angle β_j is the angle between the line of its force and member j's grain, folded into 0°–90°, printed to a tenth of a degree; against the reference member, whose grain is x, it is simply β_r = arctan(|F_z| / |F_x|). The reference is cited once, in the citation column beside the heading, and the lines below carry none. With `title: none` the citation stands beside the first line the level prints that can carry one — F_max at level 1, n under the figure at level 2, and at level 3 the rotation centre, which sits **under** the positions table since v0.84: a table read from the data carries no norm of its own, so there is nowhere on it to put a citation. From v0.33 to v0.75 a line naming the connector stood under the heading at every level and carried it; it went with the link (v0.76). At level 3 a centre drawn in the schema is printed as a fixed point, as a manual one is: `x_c = Σx_i/n` is not how it was found. What each level prints, in this order:

What follows describes it as it was.* **What it works out, it writes down (v0.85).** The forces are computed here, in Typst, from an N, a V and an M the report states, so no file carries them (§1) and until v0.85 nothing could name one afterwards: F_max was on the page and nowhere else. `show-polar-moment` now writes **F_max**, every fastener's resultant **F_1 … F_n** and the force-grain angle **β_1 … β_m** into a var-domain named after the connection — which is what keeps two connections from writing over each other — so `#val("F_max", domain: "SD_01-8mm")` quotes one, `#var("F_max", domain: ("connection", "SD_01-8mm"))` brings it into a formula, and `#find(object: "variable", domain: ("connection", "SD_01-8mm"))` lists them beside the geometry the export does carry. Shown twice with different forces, the second call is what the names then hold, which is what «the forces of this connection» means at that point in the report.

⚠️ **The update is emitted before the context, and that is the whole of what makes it sound.** It was emitted from *inside* `show-polar-moment`'s own context for one day, and a state written there is not read back the same way by everything: `find`, `val` and `verify` read the registry inside a context of their own and saw it, while `eq` reads it inside its own `var-registry.update` — the folded chain — and did not. The engineer met it as `#eq("eta_n", "eta", "F_max / R_d")` failing two lines under a `#verify(`F_max <= R_d`)` that had just worked, and it showed only when the forces were passed **by name** (`N: "N_Ed"`), which is the usual way to write them. A value half the commands can name is worse than one none of them can. `show-polar-moment` is therefore `{ var-registry.update(…) ; context { … } }`, exactly as `var` and `eq` are (§10quinquies), and the distribution itself moved into **`polar-distribution(c, N, V, M)`**, a pure function of the record and the three magnitudes that the update and the context both call — one arithmetic (§2 rule 1), and an update that reads no state. The price is that the magnitudes are resolved twice, off the dictionary in the update and off the registry in the context; the arithmetic is not. Re-rendered, **no page moved**.

⚠️ **A published var carries no citation**, and that is this section's own rule applied to it: the reference is cited once, beside the heading, and the lines below carry none. What the bauforumstahl Arbeitshilfe gives is the **method**, which is cited where the method is shown; the value is the report's own arithmetic and untraced, so a citation on it would say the number came from that document. `val`'s §10bis row stays true as written for every other source — a var of the export carries its citation because the data states one; what changed is that these particular vars state none. A caller who wants one says so: `#var("F_max", domain: ("connection", "SD_01-8mm"), norm: "bauforumstahl…")` overrides, `var-record` applying `norm` whenever it is given.

What follows describes it as it was.* **Level -1 (v0.117, issue #20).** `#show-polar-moment(id, N:, V:, M:, level: -1, shown: (I_p: 3, F_max: 2))` prints the heading and the groups of lines named, each at its level: `positions`, `centre`, `theta`, `n`, `I_p` (3 its working), `forces`, `components`, `F_x_i`, `F_z_i`, `F_max` (1 its one line, 2 and 3 its working with the fasteners carrying it), `beta` (3 the arctangent), `forces_table`, `worst_table`; the figure where one is chosen at 2 or 3 (lib/shown-variables.json).

**The most unfavourable superposition (v0.37).** At level 3, after the table of the forces as they distribute, a second table gives every fastener's force with N, V and the force from M added component by component with their magnitudes, as if all three acted the same way on every fastener and none relieved another:
- F*_x,i = (|N_x| + |V_x|)/n + |M|·|Δz_i|/I_p;
- F*_z,i = (|N_z| + |V_z|)/n + |M|·|Δx_i|/I_p;
- F*_i = √(F*_x,i² + F*_z,i²).

The engineer asked for it and set its rule: nothing is subtracted. Its formulas stand in letters above it; the F* column is bold, and so are the rows carrying the largest F*. A symmetric group then carries the same force in every fastener. On the project's SD_01-8mm (four dowels at ±60 mm), with N = 20 kN, V = 16 kN and M = 20 kNm, that is 65.293 kN each, which is what the vector sum gives its fastener 3. The table has no force-grain angle: the signs are chosen, not calculated, so the direction of F* is not defined. Woodcore's preview does not compute it.

Guards, each with a message naming what to fix: an unknown id (listing the valid ones), a level outside 1–3, a force that is neither a number nor a var, a var in the wrong unit, and M ≠ 0 on a group with I_p = 0 (one fastener, or all at the rotation centre), which has no lever arm to take it. Without a lever arm and without M, the moment term is simply left out of the formulas.

Woodcore previews the forces for trial values of N, V and M with `connection.fastener_forces()`, the same distribution once more in Python, ties included, so the most stressed fastener can be seen — in numbers and on the drawing — while the fasteners are placed. Nothing it computes is saved or reaches the report, and the two are checked against each other (§13).
