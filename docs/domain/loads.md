> The four load domains, load combinations and why no load is linked to a position.

> Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md (§3.3bis, §3.3ter, §3.3quater, §3.3septies, §3.5, §8) on 2026-10-07. The § numbers are kept as stable anchors: `docs/README.md` maps every § to its file.

### 3.3bis The `snow-load` domain: a roof entered in Woodcore, computed from SIA 261 (v0.117; the workbook's sheets read until v0.124)

**Since v0.124 (issue #94, spec `docs/requirements/completed/2026-10-22-onglet-neige-type.md`) the snow is the first domain a tab of calculation is described by** (`docs/ui/calc-tab.md`, `docs/ui/tab-snow.md`). `snow_source` declares the tab's form (`declaration()`: the building first, the site, the roof; `visible_fields`), says what the tab says (`messages(src)`: an `error` per field the calculation cannot read -- `_problems`, the one place the rules are written, `check_fields` refusing on the first -- and an `info` for the 2000 m of 5.1.4, the 800 m of 5.3.7, C_T under 1, a slope of 60° free to slide (5.3.3, `clause.steep`), a large flat roof, a height step; every threshold the norm file's; `warnings` is the export's share, `EXPORT_CODES`) and its results (`results(instance)`). **Its source** is at schema 1.3 where C_e is a value of the engineer's own (`exposure: "custom"`, `c_e`, taken without a message), at 1.4 where Δh is (`correction: "custom"`, `delta_h` in m, negative too, printed without the map's five corrections beside it; v0.125, issue #99), the versions floors compared as numbers; L and B are entered for every roof (already allowed at 1.0) and read by a flat roof only. **Its file** (`Variable-loads/Snow/<id>.typ`) carries at its head the column of Figur 3 of the case's roof -- `snow_svg.figure`, black and white, no word, the tab's drawing byte for byte, written under `Variable-loads/Snow/Exports/Figures/<id>-fig3.svg` (`project.FIGURE_KINDS["snow-load"]`) -- with the glossary's caption; s_k is an `#eq` of equation (10) written so that `#eq` computes it at every altitude (`max(…, 0.9 "kN/m²")`); the cornice of 5.3.7 is no longer written (the tab says it); a clause named by a word of its edition («Figur 3», «Anhang D») and a symbol written as a word («Dachform») are printed in the report's language, μ₁ as μ₁. A sheet's case (`SL-*`) carries no figure. **The excerpts of SIA 261** the tab shows -- page, box, words, in French and German -- are the norm file's `editions` and `excerpts` (`calc/norms/excerpts.py`), the PDFs shipped in `norm-library/` (`docs/architecture/reference-library.md` §11bis).

⚠️ *Retired by the spec 2026-10-21-commandes-typst-refonte (lot 19): `show-calc` -- write #include domain-file(kind, id) -- a load ("snow-load", "wind-load", "dead-load", "live-load"), a connector, a polar moment ("connection"), a script: its file, written out by the export, printed where the section includes it. What follows describes it as it was.* **Laid out by folders (v0.119, issue #64):** a snow load, a wind load and a permanent load entered in Woodcore each have a file, `Variable-loads/Snow/<id>.typ`, `Variable-loads/Wind/<id>.typ`, `Permanent-loads/<id>.typ` -- a stub that prints the case, `#show-calc("snow-load", "<id>")` …, written when the case is made (calc/domains/domain_file.py); the calculation written out whole comes per tab (docs/open-questions.md).

**Since v0.125 (issue #109, decision D-33) a snow load is a case entered in the tab «Neige», and nothing else.** The `SL-*` sheets of the Belastungsannahmen workbooks were read for their inputs from v0.120 and computed by the code below (`calc/domains/snow.py`); before v0.120 they *were* the calculation, read cell by cell. Both readings are retired, their description moved verbatim to [`docs/history/load-workbook-sheets.md`](../history/load-workbook-sheets.md). A workbook left in the project folder is never opened: each full export names it in one warning (`load_workbook_not_read`, `registry.workbook_note`, docs/domain/calculation-domains.md §3.3). The names a sheet gave its values before v0.120 (`q_k11`, `lastmodell`) are still migrated when an old project is opened (`calc/domains/loads_names.py`, `loads_migrate.py`).

**A snow load entered in Woodcore (issue #36, v0.117).** The tab «Neige» writes `Woodcore-Datei/loads/snow/<id>.json` (`schemas/snow-source.schema.json` 1.0, 1.1 where a field follows a variable, 1.2 where the roof has a height step -- the versions are floors, 1.2 holding links too) and `calc/domains/snow_source.py` computes it from SIA 261:2020 -- never from the workbook (the engineer's decision D-04: both kinds of case live under `snow-load`, an id both carry is refused, docs/domain/calculation-domains.md §3.3). Every value of the norm is read from `calc/norms/sia261-2020-5.2-schnee.json` through `norms/sia261_snow.py`: equation (10) with its floor, Anhang D's corrections, C_e of 5.2.4, C_T of 5.2.5, the polylines of Figur 2 (mu_1, mu_2, mu_3) and Figur 4, read off a render of pp. 26-29 and 115. The roofs are Figur 3's: flat (Figur 4 where its length and width both exceed 20 m, 5.3.5), mono-pitch (half a duo-pitch roof, 5.3.2: model 1 mu_1(alpha), model 2 its half), duo-pitch (model 1 mu_11 and mu_12, model 2 each side halved in turn), shed (mu_2m in the valley) and **the curved roof, the figure's third column** -- the engineer's «modèle avec l'hémisphère», a semicircle loaded where its slope is under alpha_0 = 60°: model 1 a uniform 0.8, model 2 the two triangles mu_3/2 and mu_3 with mu_3 = min(0.2 + 0.09 alpha, 2.0) (D-01). **Where the sliding is hindered, mu_1 is 0.8 on every side before model 2 halves it** (5.3.4) -- the workbook's N37/N38 halve the raw mu_1(alpha) (D-02). A roof steeper than 60° with the snow free to slide carries none (5.3.3, Figur 2), and **a site above 2000 m (5.1.4, `max_altitude`) is computed all the same and warned of in the tab only** (issue #56, spec 2026-10-14-neige-vent-affichage-altitude, REQ-003, REQ-004, D-04: refused until then) -- `snow_source.warnings(src)` hands the tab `above_limit` with the limit and the clause read from the norm file, worded `ld_warn_above_limit` in fr/de/en in its callout, and nothing of it reaches the instance, the export's notices or the report. The limit reads **its altitude h_0, not h_0 + Δh**: 5.1.4 speaks of works above 2000 m, and a reference height above it is computed without a word (spec 2026-10-12-audit-issues-47-48-49, REQ-002). **Said, not computed** (REQ-003, D-04): a roof whose source carries `height_step` (the tab's «Décrochement») prints the extra load of Figur 5 (5.3.6) as a var of its own with its description, and works above 800 m (`cornice.min_altitude` of the norm file, h_0 again) print the cornice of overhanging roof parts (5.3.7). **The report prints no drawing of the snow** (issue #56, D-02, D-03 of spec 2026-10-14-neige-vent-affichage-altitude): the instance carries neither `figures` nor `_files`, so `show-calc` prints no sketch at any level and `figure-1` is no key of it; the sketch of the disposition (`snow_svg`, no word in it) is the tab's alone, asked of `snow_source.sketch(src)` and shown in «Calcul» -- `registry` writing every `_files` an instance hands it, the sketch is not one. Until 14.10.2026 it was printed from level 2. The reader of the sheets above cites SIA 261:2020's own numbers since 12.10.2026 (spec 2026-10-12-audit-issues-47-48-49, REQ-004): 5.3.4 for the hindered sliding, 5.3.6 for the steps in height, 5.2.4 for C_e, 5.2.5 for C_T, Figur 2 and 3 for the shed's accumulation.

### 3.3ter The `dead-load` domain: a build-up entered in Woodcore, summed in Python (v0.117; the workbook's sheets read from v0.18 to v0.124)

**Since v0.125 (issue #109, decision D-33) a permanent load is a build-up entered in the tab «Poids propre et surcharge», and nothing else.** Until then the `EL+AL-*` sheets of the Belastungsannahmen workbooks were read too, each layer looked up in the workbook's own material library and summed here -- the same arithmetic, `dead_load.instance_from_layers`, which stays the one place the sums are made. Their reading is moved verbatim to [`docs/history/load-workbook-sheets.md`](../history/load-workbook-sheets.md); the workbook's library lives on as the materials Woodcore ships (`calc/libraries/materials-library.json`, issue #110). Per layer, as the workbook did it: load [kN/m²] = ρ · thickness · width / spacing, a weight in kg turned into kN at g = 10 m/s² (`dead_load.KG_TO_KN`).

**A build-up entered in Woodcore (issue #35, v0.117).** The tab «Poids propre et surcharge» -- one tab for the two, the engineer's D-08 -- writes `Woodcore-Datei/loads/dead/<id>.json` (`schemas/dead-load-source.schema.json` 1.0, 1.1 where a field follows a variable): the layers top to bottom, each **g_k or a_k at the engineer's choice** (T4), its `library` and `material`, its `thickness`, and for battens, rafters or joists its `width` and `spacing`; `beside` puts a layer in the band of the one above it (the insulation between the rafters); `direct` is a load typed in kN/m². `calc/domains/dead_load_source.py` weighs each layer -- weight · thickness · width/spacing, a weight per surface without the thickness, kg to kN with g = 10 m/s² as the workbook does -- and **`dead_load.instance_from_layers`**, which `read_instance` calls too, makes the sums, the chosen value and the table: one arithmetic for both kinds of case. **The weights came from three libraries until issue #110** (`calc/domains/materials.py`, D-07, superseded): since v0.125 a layer names **one material by its id** (`library: "catalogue"`, dead-load-source 1.2) -- Woodcore's or the engineer's own, the project keeping a copy of his --, chosen in one list searched in three languages, and «Poids propre» creates no material any more (§3.3sexvicies, `domain/materials.md`); the three libraries below stay read for the layers written before, and a project's 1.0 file is poured into the engineer's library when it is opened. Until then: SIA 261 Anhang A, Tabellen 28 and 29, read through `norms/sia261.py`; Woodcore's materials library, read out once into `calc/libraries/materials-library.json` -- not a norm, so not in `calc/norms/`, every entry keeping the workbook's «Quellen», keyed by (Ebene, material) with `MaterialLibrary`'s rule; and the project's own `Woodcore-Datei/materials.json` (`schemas/materials.schema.json`), edited in the tab's own card. ⚠️ That library weighs «Fassade / Glas» 5000 kg/m³ (SIA 261: 25 kN/m³) and «Scobalit» 0 kg/m³, which a layer naming it is refused for. **Four aluminium entries carry the norm's value** since 12.10.2026 (spec 2026-10-12-audit-issues-47-48-49, REQ-006, D-05): Aluwellblech 4 kg/m² (Tabelle 29, the workbook 40), Blech Aluminium 2700 kg/m³ (Tabelle 28, the workbook 3500) and Blech Aluminium 2mm 5.4 kg/m² (Tabelle 28 × 2 mm, the workbook 8) -- `quelle` naming the table, the workbook's value in `info`, held equal to the norm files by `test_dead_load_source`. **The build-up is drawn** (`dead_load_svg`, UI-002, UI-003): one band per layer or group of layers beside each other, heights to scale -- a band never thinner than 7 units, its dimension saying its true thickness --, the heights in a chain at the left with the whole height, the materials' names at the right, and under the drawing the width of one spaced member and the spacing, `e = 625`; the instance carries it as a figure, `data/calculations/dead-load-<id>.svg` (in a project laid out by folders, since v0.119, `Permanent-loads/Exports/Figures/`, named in the JSON from the Typst root -- issue #64, docs/architecture/layout.md §11), which `show-calc` prints from level 2 and the tab shows as it is typed. **Held against the workbook**: nine layers typed into the sheet «Flachdach» of `«load workbook» template` and recalculated by Excel give g_k 0.397, a_k 0.407 and 0.804 kN/m², which the same build-up gives here (`test_dead_load_source.test_a_flat_roof_gives_what_the_workbooks_flachdach_sheet_gives`); a weight per m² is typed there with F = 1.

**Redrawn by issue #111 (v0.125): the tab at the keyboard, dead-load-source 2.0** (spec `docs/requirements/completed/2026-10-24-poids-propre-repense.md`, the prototype v3 validated whole by the engineer on 24.10.2026). **A layer** has a **pose** (`calc/domains/dead_load_layers.py`): `continuous` γ·t, `spaced` γ·t·b/e (`width` b and `spacing` e of battens, rafters, joists), `surface` γ alone (a weight per m², the thickness drawn only), `typed` the load written (`load`, its words `label`); its material is one id (a shipped one, the engineer's `u-…`, `project:<id>` for a 1.0 project's own weight); kg go to kN with g = 10 m/s². **`link`** puts a layer on the level of the line below it -- **two lines at most, one spaced and one that fills** (D-07 of the spec, the engineer: «il ne peut y avoir que deux matériaux sur une même couche»): the line that is not spaced fills, by default (`fill`), the room the pieces leave on 1 m, w = 1000 · (1 − b/e) mm, its load γ·t·w/1000 (60/625 leaves 904 mm, 100/500 800 mm); `fill` false, the width written, 1000 mm where none is; b/e ≥ 1 leaves nothing and is said in red; the thicker of the two gives the level's height, and the air above a thinner filling line is said. A third line on a level, two spaced or two filling lines on one, a link on the last line: refused naming the layer. **A load written on a layer of any pose** (`load`, or `links.load` to a variable) replaces its calculation in the sum (ARCH-006), printed without a mark. **The retained value** (`chosen`) is, where none is written, **the tenth above or equal to the sum** (`dead_load_layers.retained`: 0.909 → 1.0, 0.900 → 0.9), so every build-up's instance carries `gk_ak_gew` and names it its result (P01: what a report printed as 0.916 prints 1.0). **A source of before** (1.0 to 1.2) is read as 2.0 in memory by `dead_load_migrate.upgrade` and never rewritten on reading: `beside` on layer i becomes `link` on layer i − 1, the line that is not spaced keeping the full width it was summed with (`fill` false, width 1000) -- the same sum (Flachdach 0.804 still) --, the pose from the weight's unit, the libraries' names their ids, the workbook's Ebene its role (`EBENE_ROLES`: «Dämmung» insulation, «Dachlattung» roof battens…); two spaced layers beside each other, or three, refused naming the layer; the window writes 2.0 at the next save. **What the build-up says** is `dead_load_messages` -- one evaluation (`evaluate`) the export stops with and the tab says: red (no material, a material unknown or with no weight, a thickness, a width or a spacing missing, a load typed with no value, b/e ≥ 1, a level refused, a retained value below the sum), orange (a load replaced v in place of c, a retained value written v with its auto c, a filling line thicker than its pieces, **battens counted twice** under tiles weighed «inkl. Lattung» -- SIA 261 Tabelle 29's four lines under that heading carry `includes_battens`, held to the heading, and so do the workbook's tiles that weigh as one of them (`materials-catalogue.json` `includes_battens`) --, the layers of the roles `roof_battens` and `counter_battens` (Q-006) said with their load, the sum unchanged), information (the air, a material of the engineer's own) and green. **What each layer weighs** in g_k, a_k and the two: `dead_load_layers.contributions`. **Every build-up at a glance**: `dead_load_overview.overview(here)`, its state «complete» or the number of red messages (Q-007). **Templates** (`dead_load_templates.py`, `calc/libraries/dead-load-templates.json`, `schemas/dead-load-template.schema.json`): eight build-ups of Lignum's *Holzbau mit System* read off the pages they name (33, 35, 377, 379, 389, 415) -- the joisted floor with sand between the battens 1.258 kN/m² (1.333 across the full width; the book 127 kg/m²), with a cement screed 1.475 (the book 150 kg/m²) --, each with what it supposes, and «Mes modèles», the engineer's own, a JSON each in `%APPDATA%\Woodcore\dead-load-templates\` (`usersettings.dead_load_templates_dir`). The compact flat roof's Foamglas (Tabelle 13) has no weight in the book, nor in SIA 261 nor in the workbook: the template takes a walkable flat-roof stone wool of the library, Flumroc prima (120 kg/m³), said in its assumptions (the engineer, 24.10.2026). **The section** (`dead_load_svg`) is drawn at its true proportion: one metre at the scale of the heights, no minimum band, the pieces at their spacing and the filling line between them from the bottom, the names in a column joined by fanned lines (cut by «…», the whole name its title), «1000» under the strip, heights written where they fit; the legend mode under 560 units; `render(layers, width, legend)` and `hit_boxes` from one layout. **The file** prints the section from level 2 (`#from-level(2)`) and the table of the layers at level 3 (a table's `from_level`, `#from-level(3)`); g_k, a_k, their sum and the retained value at every level. **Corrected in the shipped library** (REQ-012, D-06, D-09): «Glas» (rows 150, 234: 5000 kg/m³) carries SIA 261 Tabelle 28's «Glasbausteine, voll», 25 kN/m³, and the façade's «Scobalit» (row 235: 0) row 151's 500 kg/m³, each keeping the workbook's value in `info`; the two Scobalit rows are now one material.

**The tab on the type «Calcul» (v0.125, issue #95, spec `docs/requirements/completed/2026-10-23-onglets-types-et-services.md`).** `calc/tools/dead_load_tab.py` `DeadLoadTab(CalcTab)` names its parameters and takes, in place of a declared form, the table of its layers (`layer_table.LayerTable`) and, since issue #110, under it the card of the engineer's materials changed in his library since the project took its copy (`material_copies.MaterialCopies`: «Reprendre», «Garder», «Ajouter à ma bibliothèque»; its writers guarded) -- the project's own library (`project_materials.ProjectMaterials`) is gone. On the right, the modules of its type: the state of the calculation, «Résultats» -- g_k, a_k, their sum and the value chosen, then the table of the layers --, and the drawing of the build-up, opened in a window by ⧉. «Affichage» is the case's file, `Permanent-loads/<id>.typ`, line by line, **following its case as the snow's does** (`unfold.AUTO_REGENERATE`, D-21): a value changed refreshes its lines in place, a line changed by hand kept; the calculation's form changed -- a value chosen beside the sum, one variable more -- it is written again in the new form at the save or the export, never asked, what was changed by hand in it gone. The domain gives what the type asks -- `messages` (what stops the calculation is its check's; since issue #110 it says in orange a material of the engineer's own changed in his library, or that this computer's lacks), `results`, `figure_svg` -- and **its model of layers**, `layers(src, here)` (`contracts.Layer`: id, kind, thickness, width and spacing in mm, beside, label, ebene; no hatch, no visibility), which `dead_load_svg.render` draws and a service that draws layers will ask by `NEEDS = ("layers",)`; the export's drawing and the tab's are one, drawn once a keystroke (`tests/test_figure_golden.py` holds its digest).

### 3.3quater The `wind-load` domain: a building entered in Woodcore, computed from SIA 261 (v0.117; the workbook's sheets read from v0.25 to v0.124)

**Since v0.125 (issue #109, decision D-33) a wind load is a building entered in the tab «Vent», and nothing else.** The `WL-*` sheets of the Belastungsannahmen workbooks were read for their inputs from v0.120 (`calc/domains/wind.py`) and, from v0.25 to v0.120, read as the calculation itself; both readings are moved verbatim to [`docs/history/load-workbook-sheets.md`](../history/load-workbook-sheets.md). The six global forces of a sheet named before v0.120 (`qk_max_bh`) are still migrated when an old project is opened (`loads_names.wind_renamed`).

**A building's wind entered in Woodcore (issue #37, v0.117).** The tab «Vent» writes `Woodcore-Datei/loads/wind/<id>.json` (`schemas/wind-source.schema.json` 1.0, 1.1 where a field follows a variable, **1.2 with `openings`**, the openings' case its local pressures take -- `rep` or a dominant opening A-D, what every new source is written at; a source of 1.0 or 1.1 holds none and is read with `rep`, which the tab says in orange until one is chosen; spec `docs/requirements/completed/2026-10-23-onglet-vent-briques.md`, CON-002, D-24): q_p0 of Anhang E, the terrain category, b, d, h, z (null for h) and **the table of Annexe C** the building's coefficients are read from, and c_d (1.0 under 6.3.5). `calc/domains/wind_source.py` computes c_h of (12) with its floors and category IV's switch to III above 30 m, q_p of (11), **c_red of Figur 9 for b·h (beta = b/h) and d·h (beta = d/h)** and 1.0 for any other reference area (6.3.2) -- computed, where the workbook reads it off a scroll bar; **each area its own in the rows of a reference direction (0°, 90°, 180°), the larger of the two in every other row** (6.3.3: «less than 15°» off a reference direction, a 15° row not being less; spec 2026-10-12-audit-issues-47-48-49, REQ-001, D-03, D-18), the direction read off the row's label by `sia261_wind.direction`, the rule (`c_red.directions`) in the norm file, a row naming no direction (Tableau 64's) keeping its area's own and the larger printed as `cred_max` where a row takes it --, then the **global forces** q_k = c_red · c_d · c_f · q_p per m², largest and smallest over the table's rows, per force coefficient the table gives (`qk_max_cf*`, `qk_min_cf*` in kN/m², the names the sections cite), and **Q_k = q_k · A_ref in kN** beside them (`Qk_max_cf*`, `Qk_min_cf*`, since spec 2026-10-23-onglet-vent-briques, REQ-002), A_ref read off a **closed table of the norm file** (`forces.areas` of `sia261-2020-6.2-wind.json`, `sia261_wind.area`: each header a sum of k · π^i · b^i · d^j · h^k, never the label evaluated) times the header's factor -- a header naming a dimension the building is not given by (h1, d1, hs) has none, and its force stays per m², said in orange --; and the **local pressures** q_k = q_p · (c_d · c_pe − c_pi) of (13), (14), (17), (18), without c_red (6.3.1): c_pi the chosen openings' column, else the surface's own (an open building, Tableaux 46-50), else 0 (the canopies). **The arithmetic is `calc/domains/wind_forces.py` `analysis(src)`** (V1 of that spec): every extreme with the φ, the c_f or the c_pe and c_pi that gave it, and which value of a pair ±x or a/b, each number keyed to the cells of the norm's table it was read off (`cell:<column>:<row>[:<side>]`, for the tab's linked hovers), the detail per surface and per φ, m, n, o apart (`group` «loc», Figur 22), ĉ_pe · q_p -- what `build_instance` writes and the tab will show, never `calculations.json`; held against the prototype's own arithmetic on eight buildings (`tests/fixtures/wind-prototype-golden.json`). A coefficient the norm marks «*» (a platform canopy's c_f1, computed directly) is not computed and its column is left out. **The file** (`Variable-loads/Wind/<id>.typ`, written again at the save since that spec: `wind-load` is in `unfold.AUTO_REGENERATE`) prints the global forces' table in Q_k [kN] with the numbers -- a cell naming a variable shows its value, `contracts.table_cell`, the one rule of the file and the window -- and the local pressures of the openings' case chosen alone, **transposed**: a column a surface, a row max and a row min, `columns: (auto,) + (1fr,) * n` (a table's `widths: "first-auto"`), cut into ⌈surfaces / 12⌉ balanced blocks (`wind_source.surface_blocks`: Tableau 60a's 29 surfaces in 10, 10, 9), the title over the first, the note under the last, which names the localised surfaces. Its words are the glossary's in the project's language, none German in a French or English file. **The buildings in 3D** (V3 of that spec, REQ-010, ARCH-011, D-23): `calc/norms/sia261-2020-annexc-wind-geometry.json` holds the 37 buildings as numbers -- each its family (prism, open, stand, canopy, eave, curved), the table's own proportions h : b : d (the drawing never follows what is typed), its roof (flat, duo with its slope, mono, an off-centre ridge, sheds, a hipped roof) and the place of every surface its table gives a coefficient of: `[surface, place, x0, x1, y0, y1]`, place `roof` or `strip` (fractions of d from wall A and of b from wall C, laid on the roof and cut at each slope), a wall `A`…`D` or `wallstrip:<wall>` (fractions of its length and height), `hip:<wall>`; the stand, the canopies, the eaves and the curved forms their own keys. ### 3.3septies The `live-load` domain: read off SIA 261's Tabelle 8 (v0.49)

⚠️ *Retired by the spec 2026-10-21-commandes-typst-refonte (lot 19): `show-calc` -- write #include domain-file(kind, id) -- a load ("snow-load", "wind-load", "dead-load", "live-load"), a connector, a polar moment ("connection"), a script: its file, written out by the export, printed where the section includes it; `var-of` -- write #var(name, domain: (domain, instance)) to read a value of the export, get(name, "value") for its number. What follows describes it as it was.* The live load (Nutzlast) is neither entered in Excel nor calculated in Python. It is **looked up**, by the category of Nutzfläche a surface belongs to, in Tabelle 8 of SIA 261: A1 Wohnflächen, A2 Balkone, A3 Treppen, B Büroflächen, C1–C3 Versammlungsflächen, D Verkaufsflächen, E Lager- und Fabrikationsflächen, F and G the Park- und Verkehrsflächen, H nicht begehbare Dächer. Each row gives two characteristic values, the Flächenlast q_k in kN/m² and the Einzellast Q_k in kN. So this is the first domain whose **instances are not sheets but the rows of a norm table**, addressed exactly as every other instance is: `show-calc("live-load", "A1")`, `var-of("live-load", "B", "q_k")`, `ids("live-load")`.

**Where the numbers live.** `calc/norms/sia261-2020-tab08-nutzlasten.json` (`sia261_tables.json` until v0.77) holds Tabelle 8 as the norm gives it — the edition (`SIA 261:2020`), the table's title and citation, one entry per category with its Art der Nutzfläche, the norm's example, q_k, Q_k and the footnotes each of the three carries, and the five footnotes themselves. It is the first file in `norms/`, the folder §11 has reserved since v0.1 for "the sole source of values taken from the norm", and the one place in the pipeline where a number is transcribed by hand rather than read. What it is transcribed *from* is `SIA261-2020-DE.pdf` (§11bis), whose French edition sets the same table on p. 38 — which is where §4bis's French wording of every category and every footnote comes from. `calc/norms/sia261.py` reads it and checks it before anything is built from it: a category listed twice, a value that is neither a number nor absent, half a row left open, a load that is not positive, a footnote referenced but never written — each stops the export naming the category (§3.6).

**Its engine is `norm`.** Neither Excel nor Python computes these numbers, so `live_load.py` declares `ENGINE = "norm"` and `registry.py` builds its instances from `build_instances()`, as it builds the loads' from their sources. Every var's `source` says where it comes from, edition included: `norm:SIA 261:2020, Tabelle 8`, a third kind of provenance beside `excel:` and `python:` (§2 rule 5). Its `action` is `variable`, SIA 261 8.1.1 calling the Nutzlasten free variable actions, so Woodcore lists it with snow and wind and a load combination will read it there (§3.5).

What one category's instance carries:

| var | value | level | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `kategorie` | `A1` | 1 | its label is *Nutzungskategorie*, and it carries the note the table puts on the category itself (H's Dächer) |
| `nutzflaeche` | `Wohnflächen` | 2 | the Art der Nutzfläche |
| `beispiel` | the norm's example | 2 | absent where the norm gives none (Büroflächen) |
| `q_k` | 2 kN/m² | 1 | `description`: the footnotes of its cell |
| `Q_k` | 2 kN | 1 | likewise — note 1 is what says an Einzellast is not combined with a Flächenlast |

`results` are `q_k` and `Q_k`; there are no steps and no checks, a lookup having no working. Every instance also carries **Tabelle 8 entire** as its one `table`, shown at level 3 above the values, with this category's row in `highlight` (§3.3) — the checker reads the value in the table it was read from. Its two value columns are `math` cells, so a value carries its footnote references in superscript as the norm sets them, and `label_math` heads them `q_k` and `Q_k`.

What follows describes it as it was.* **Category E carries no values at all.** SIA 261 8.2.2 leaves the Lager- und Fabrikationsflächen to the project, to be fixed with any dynamic effect in mind and written into the Nutzungsvereinbarung. Its instance therefore has no `q_k` and no `Q_k`, only a `hinweis` var holding that sentence, and it is that absence the report refuses on: `show-live-load("E")` stops with the sentence and says to state the values in the report itself, and `val("q_k", domain: ("live-load", "E"))` finds no such var. E keeps its row in the table every other category prints, with its footnote references and no number, as the norm's own row reads.

### 3.5 Load combinations

**Load combinations** (ULS, SLS, etc.) are calculated **exclusively in Python**, never in Typst.

A dedicated module is reserved for them:

```
calc/domains/combinations.py
```

Characteristics:
- generates variables **like any other domain** (`vars`, `steps`, `checks`, cf. §3.1) — no special structure,
- traces the **formulas** and **combinations** applied (`uses`, `formula`),
- systematically includes `norm` and `norm_ref` (e.g. fundamental combination, SIA 260 §4.4.2.1),
- takes its line loads as plain function arguments and knows nothing about positions. ⚠️ The module does not exist today: the v0.11 sketch was deleted in v0.59 (§13), and it is written from SIA 260 4.4.2.1 when a caller exists (§14).

⚠️ *Retired by the spec 2026-10-21-commandes-typst-refonte (lot 19): `show-calc` -- write #include domain-file(kind, id) -- a load ("snow-load", "wind-load", "dead-load", "live-load"), a connector, a polar moment ("connection"), a script: its file, written out by the export, printed where the section includes it. What follows describes it as it was.* 👉 **Typst must contain no combination logic**: it only displays, via `show-calc()`, the result already traced by `combinations.py`.

## 8. Loads: a domain with instances, never linked to a position (v0.18)

Loads live in `data/calculations.json` as domains whose instances are cases entered in Woodcore (§3.3; Excel sheets until v0.125). What is unchanged since v0.10-v0.11 is the part that matters here: **every** link from a position to a load is gone, at any layer.

- v0.10 removed the *data-level* link (`data/assignments.json`, its schema, `calc/loads/assignment.py`, the influence-width concept).
- v0.11 removed the *code-level* link that replaced it — `calc/export_all.py`'s `POSITION_INPUTS` dict, which named a span and two `load_id`s per position and derived line loads from them. A position carries no span, no influence width, and no load id, in any form.
- v0.18 removes the last place a load could be *stated* outside the Belastungsannahmen workbook: `Positionen.xlsx`'s `Loads` sheet. The hand-edited project workbook now carries positions and reference tables and nothing else; `docs/typst-reference.xlsx` lost its `Loads` sheet too (§10ter). If a load is ever calculated in Python from entries that have nowhere else to live, `Positionen.xlsx` may carry them again — that is the only condition under which it should.

Consequence, accepted deliberately and unchanged: **nothing populates `domains` on a position right now**, so every `data/positions/<id>.json` has `"domains": {}` and no `show-calc`/`show-check` call in `Sections/*.typ` reads a position. The two calculation modules that waited here, `domains/bending.py` (W, M_Ed, sigma_m, f_m_d, utilization) and `domains/combinations.py` (ELU), were deleted in v0.59 for carrying norm values hardcoded and English labels, and are re-derived from the clause when a caller exists (§13, §14).

`Sections/004-einwirkungen.typ` holds **no `calc-block`**: every load it shows is project-wide, and a block's header says "look here on the plan" (§5), which a project-wide load has no answer to. A `show-calc` naming a calculation domain is not scope-checked against an enclosing block either, for the same reason — a sheet belongs to no position, so putting one inside a block would create no link. `calc-block` returns to the report as soon as a position carries a domain of its own; it is unchanged, and the `Sections/004` of v0.12-v0.16 remains the worked example of its use.

👉 What replaces the removed wiring is **open** (§14). The constraint any replacement must satisfy is the one stated here: a position may not carry a load, nor the geometry whose only purpose is to derive one from a load.

**A load's number that follows a variable** (issue #42, v0.117; `docs/domain/variable-links.md` §3.3duovicies). Every number a load source states -- a build-up's chosen value and each layer's thickness, width, spacing and load, the snow's h_0, C_t and the roof's, the wind's q_p0, b, d, h, z and c_d -- may follow a variable of **another load or of a «Formules» domain**, never a script's or a joint's (`links.LOADS`, D-11): the loads are computed first. `registry.build_calculations` builds the sheets and the sources that follow nothing, then each linked source once every load it names is built, a «Formules» domain computed against the loads built so far and a source naming one built last; the instance carries `links`, where its values came from, and the export is `calculations` 1.6 where one does. Loads following each other in a circle stop the export naming the chain, and the tab refuses such a link as it is chosen (`registry.load_graph`). A source naming a script or a joint is a defect of the file, refused naming it; one naming a variable since gone keeps its last value with a warning.
