### 3.3sexies Position plans: drawn in Woodcore over the architect's plan (v0.34)

A position plan (Positionsplan) shows where each position is. It carries the structure's symbols after STE Compact 02 «Plandarstellung im Holzbau»: bearing walls (V), bracing walls (H), walls that do both (W), beams (T), columns (S), bracing members (A), slabs and roofs with their span and diaphragm action (D), openings (Ö), concrete (B), supports on the solid construction (M) and loads taken from above (L). Each carries its position number, and the whole is drawn over the architect's plan with the plan's axes. The reference editor — since v0.53 in this project's own reference library, `ExternalFiles/000-positionsPlan/` (§11bis) — fixed the symbols, and only them. Its code is a third party's and was not reused. Its loads, a *Vorbemessung* with a load path, contradict §8. And its own copy of each element's description would be a second source beside Positionen.xlsx (§2 rule 1).

**Source.** A plan is drawn in Woodcore's «Plan de positions» tab (§10quater) and saved as `Woodcore-Datei/plans/<id>.json`, one file per plan. Woodcore writes it and nothing else does, which is the connections' arrangement, for the same reasons (§3.3quinquies, §13). `schemas/plan-source.schema.json` states its shape (`schema_version` 1.1; a 1.0 file, which moves no number, is read as it is) and `calc/domains/plan.py` checks it. It holds:
- the plan's id (its file name, and the string the report addresses it by), a label, and its **floor**, the second character of its positions' numbers (U, 0, 1, …). A plan is normally one floor; a project of several small buildings, like this one, gives each building a plan of its own on the same floor;
- its **background**, a file in the project folder named by its path from that folder (`01_Pläne/03_Pavillon - 29.07.26.pdf`, page 1): a PDF page, a PNG, a JPEG or an SVG. With it are the SHA-256 the file had when the plan was saved and how visible it is under the symbols;
- the **sheet**, the units the plan is drawn in, those of its background: points for a PDF (the page as a viewer shows it, crop box and /Rotate applied), pixels for an image, viewBox units for an SVG, counted from the top left corner with y down. Without a background, an A3 page in points;
- the **crop**, the part of the sheet the report shows (the whole sheet by default);
- its **scale**: 1:n for a PDF, whose points are paper, or two points and the real length between them, for any background. With a scale, the walls' lengths in metres may be written beside them (`show_lengths`);
- the **style**: three factors from 0.25 to 3, on the strokes, on the symbols placed on a point and on the texts — Woodcore's three sliders;
- **and, over them, the factors one element or one axis states for itself** (v0.99, `calc/domains/figure_style.py`): its own `style`, from 0.25 to 3, **multiplying** the plan's rather than replacing it. An element accepts all three — `line` for a wall's, a beam's and a slab's strokes, `point` for a column's, an Auflager's and an Abfangung's mark, `text` for its position number, that number's box and a wall's length — and an axis accepts `line` and `text` alone, being a dash-dot line and the bubble its name is set in, whose radius is already a text size; `point` on an axis is refused **naming the two it may state**, a field nobody could see the effect of being a field that acquires a value nobody checks (§13). ⚠️ **Additive, and no `schema_version` moved**: a `style` absent, or a factor left out of one, means «follows the plan», so no plan needs migrating and one drawn before v0.99 is read, and drawn, exactly as it was (the v0.69 rule for `group`, §13).
- its **axes**, each a line through a point at an angle (90° vertical, 0° horizontal, anything else oblique), drawn across the crop with a bubble at one end, both or neither. A vertical axis is named by a letter and a horizontal one by a number, an oblique one as the nearer of the two, and a new axis is named by where it lies among those named like it (v0.39, `plan.insert_axis`): drawn between A and B it becomes B, and B and those after it move up by one, as far as the next gap — a name left out on purpose (A, C) stays left out, and an axis drawn in that gap takes it. Letters run left to right and numbers bottom to top, unless the axes already there run the other way; an oblique axis is placed by where it crosses the middle of the crop. A name of any other form (C', 2a) is left alone, and so is a family whose names are already out of order, the new axis then taking the first free name;
- its **elements**: type, subtype (D `scheibe`/`normal`, M `vert`/`horiz`/`vh`, L `linie`/`punkt`, B `wand`/`decke`), label and points, plus a slab's span direction and diagonal, and, for a number moved by hand, how far from its own place (`label_offset`, v0.42). A beam (T) and a bracing member (A) have exactly two points, their two ends, where a wall may turn corners: each is one straight member (v0.45, `plan.point_count`), and a source that gives one more or fewer is refused.

**What a plan says about a position is where it is, and nothing else.** An element's label is a position id of Positionen.xlsx's Positions Element sheet, which stays the one source of positions (§7). The plan carries no material, no section and no load -- ⚠️ with **one exception since issue #54**: how a bracing wall is built (`construction`, on an H or a W), which the stiffness centre is computed from and no position states (below, «The stiffness centre and the mass centre»; §13). Until 0.117 only the sheet's positions were drawn (v0.45): a tool drew nothing where the sheet had none of its letter and opened an alert saying which position to add (v0.47), and *Enregistrer* refused a plan carrying a number the sheet lacked. ⚠️ **Since issue #9 the plan makes its positions**: an element drawn takes the next number of its letter and storey, and *Enregistrer* writes the positions the plan draws and the project lacks, with the plan (below, «What issue #9 added to a plan»); `plan.unknown_labels()` now names the numbers that save will make. Such a number can still reach the export, from a position renamed or removed in Excel after it was drawn. There it is a **warning**, not an error, for the reason a connection linked to no connector is one (§13): stopping the export would stop the positions' export too, and Woodcore could not offer the new number to give the element. Woodcore outlines it in red, lists it, and copies the rows that would bring it back (the id and its data_type name, tab-separated) for pasting into the sheet; nothing here writes to the workbook. The same warning covers a label that does not start with its type's letter and a numbered element outside the crop. Until 0.117 identical elements shared their number (v0.44) and a tool drew the same number every time (`plan.auto_label()`, v0.45). ⚠️ **Since issue #9 a number is one object** (D-007): a tool proposes the next free number (`positions_store.propose_id` over the project's positions and the plan's unsaved ones); a plan drawn before, where two elements share a number, is read as it was, and a number drawn twice is still not a warning. That is the STE numbering, which is the project's own (§7's data_type legend).

**Export.** `plan.prepare()` reads and checks every source. A blocking error names the file and the field: an unknown type, a line with one point, a crop off the sheet, a background missing, outside the project or without that page, and so on. It then builds `data/plans.json` (schema `plans` 1.2, checked by `validation.validate_plans_business()`) and, under `data/plans/` -- in a project laid out by folders (v0.119, issue #64) in `Plans/Position-plan/Exports/Figures/`, their one copy, named in the JSON from the Typst root (`plans` **1.6**), written by `engine.Figures` which removes only what the last export listed, and read by the engine's `figure-path` (docs/architecture/layout.md §11):
- `<id>-plan.pdf|png|jpg|svg`, the background. A PDF is reduced to its one page by pypdf; an image is copied. The copy is there because Typst reads nothing outside the report's own folder, and it travels with the project. An extracted page is kept as it is while the file's hash and the page are unchanged, since pypdf would write the same page with a new document id and Git would see a change that is none;
- `<id>.svg`, the symbols over the crop, drawn by `calc/domains/plan_svg.py`.

export_all.py runs it with everything else, before anything is written, and prints its warnings; Woodcore runs `plan.export()` alone on *Enregistrer*. A plan's record keeps what the report needs to highlight a position or frame a detail: its sheet, crop, scale, style and symbol unit, its elements (each line with its length in metres, and each numbered element with the centre of its number, `label_at`, v0.42) and its axes (each with where it crosses the crop). It also carries its **legend** (v0.39): one line per symbol the plan uses, in STE's order, each with its `key` (a type, a type and the subtype that changes its symbol, or `Achse`), its `text` and its `swatch`, the symbol drawn by `plan_svg.legend_swatch()` as a small SVG in mm. Until schema 1.0 the legend was one SVG file, `<id>-legende.svg`, with its texts in Arial and its symbols drawn at the plan's own sliders, which the engineer found coarse. pypdf is imported only when a plan has a PDF background. Since v0.99 an element and an axis carry, under `style`, **only the factors they state** over the plan's three, and nothing where they state none (`figure_style.record`): the record then says with what the overlay beside it was drawn, which is the convention every other placed value of this export follows. `label_at` needed no thought of its own — `number_frame` reads the element's factors itself — and a wall's `length_m` and an axis's `segment` are geometry, which no factor touches. ⚠️ `show-plan` still sizes its halo from the plan's own `style` and `unit`, so an element drawn at a factor of its own is haloed at the plan's size; the exported field is what a later version reads for it (§14).

**One drawing, two renderers, and they agree exactly.** `plan_svg.py` draws the symbols as SVG text, kept to what QtSvg (SVG Tiny 1.2) and Typst read alike: no pattern, clip path, marker, CSS or paint-order. A concrete wall's hatching is made of segments cut to its outline, and a text's halo is the text drawn twice. Woodcore shows that very SVG over the background, and `show-plan` sets it over the copied page. A white veil under the symbols lets the background show through, since Typst has no image opacity. The symbols' size unit is the crop's longer side divided by 850, times the sliders: at the full text width a position number is about 6 pt, whatever the crop. The drawing carries no scale bar (v0.39): drawn in the crop's corner, it hid part of the plan, so the report draws it under the plan instead. 👉 Verified on synthetic pages with marks at known points: a plain page, one turned by /Rotate 90 and one with a crop box. pypdf, QtPdf and Typst give the same sheet, and a column placed on a mark in Woodcore sits on it in the report.

⚠️ **Since v0.99 the sizes are two levels and the second one is applied in one place** (`plan_svg.entry_sizes`, `Sizes.scaled`): every function that draws one entry — `_symbol` and the `_slab`, `_opening` and `_concrete` it hands on to, `_leader`, `_number`, `_length`, and `_axes` per axis — is given **that entry's** sizes, and `number_frame` alone reads the entry's factors **itself**. It has to: Woodcore's canvas hit-tests a click on a position number with the plan's sizes (`plan_canvas.py`), and `plan.warnings` and the export's `label_at` ask with the plan's too, while `_number`, `_leader` and `_slab` ask with the element's while they draw it — so the scaling is made idempotent by construction (`Sizes.drawing`) rather than by a rule somebody has to remember, a factor applied twice being a number's box quietly 1.69 times its size with nothing on the page saying so. ⚠️ **What a plan that states no factor draws is the drawing it drew before, byte for byte**, and by identity rather than by luck: `entry_sizes` gives back the plan's own `Sizes` **object**. Held against a SHA-256 frozen before a line of v0.99 was written, on a plan carrying one of every kind of entry and on the example project's own four, and the whole of `data/plans.json` and its four overlays came out identical (`tests/test_plan_style.py`).

**A number moved by hand (v0.42).** A number is drawn in its own place: along a line at its middle, turned with the line; at the centre of an area; above a point. Where that place falls on another symbol or number, the engineer drags the number clear in Woodcore (§10quater). The source then keeps how far it was moved, `label_offset` [dx, dy] in sheet units. The offset is taken from the number's own place rather than stored as a point, so the number follows its element when the element is moved or reshaped; it keeps the angle it is read at. `plan_svg.number_frame()` computes where a number is drawn (its box's centre, half-sides and angle), and the drawing, Woodcore's hit-test (`plan.hit_label`) and the export all read it. Four things follow the number:
- once it has left its symbol — further from it than its box is high, or than a column's number sits above the column by itself — a thin **leader** in the number's colour runs from the box's edge to the nearest point of the symbol (`plan.nearest_point`). On a line or an area the leader ends on a dot; a column needs none, being its own mark. A number moved along its beam, or just beside it, gets no leader, and neither does one moved within its slab. The leaders are drawn under every number, so none crosses a box;
- a slab's diagonals are broken behind the number where it now is, and run whole when it has left the slab;
- an Auflager's green square stays beside its number;
- a number moved out of the crop is a warning, since the report would cut it off.

A wall's length stays at the middle of the wall, being a dimension of the wall. A plan with no number moved is drawn byte for byte as before. Woodcore's warnings, and the export, apply the same rules to a 1.0 source, which simply has every number in its own place.

⚠️ *Retired by the spec 2026-10-21-commandes-typst-refonte (lot 19): `show-plan` -- write #figure(image-positions-plan(id), kind: image, caption: [...]) <fig-id> -- a native figure, cited by @fig-id. What follows describes it as it was.* **Typst.** `show-plan(plan, width:, legend:, highlight:, detail:, zoom:, axes:, scale-bar:, caption:)` (§10bis) sets the background page and the symbols in a clipped box, under them the scale bar and the legend, and the caption «Positionsplan <id>: <label> (Mst. ca. 1:n)».
- `highlight` puts a halo under one position or several; Typst inserts it into the SVG at its `<!-- highlight -->` marker, from the elements' geometry.
- `detail: true` shows only their surroundings, 1.5 m around them when the plan has a scale — around their symbols and their numbers, a number moved away included (`label_at`, v0.42). It keeps **the scale of the whole plan**, so the symbols keep their size; `zoom` enlarges it.
- `axes: false` leaves the axes out: Typst removes what the SVG holds between its `<!-- axes -->` markers.
- **The scale bar** (v0.39, `plan-scale-bar`) sits under the plan, flush with its left edge, where the plan has a scale: a round length of about a fifth of the plan's width, but not much under 2.5 cm, in alternate black and white parts (a 2 m bar in four half metres, 1 m or 5 m in five), with the metres under its ends. It is drawn from the printed scale, so a detail and a zoom get the right one. `scale-bar: false` leaves it out, and `calc-block`'s details do.
- **The legend** (v0.39, `plan-legend`) sets each swatch beside its text in the report's own typeface, at 8 pt in dark grey, read down one column and then the next. It takes as many columns, three at most, as its width holds without a line wrapping: that width is the plan's, but not under 11 cm. A plan exported with schema 1.0 — by a Woodcore window not restarted since — still has its legend as one SVG, which is shown as it is.

**What v0.116 added to a plan** (the engineer's message of 06.10.2026, evening; §13). ⚠️ **A plan has a type** (`plan-source` **1.3**, additive): `plan_type` is `floor` (a plan in section, «Coupe en plan»: the default), `section` (an elevation section, «Coupe en élévation») or `loadpath` (the load-path plan, «Plan de descente de charges»). It is **derived from the older fields when absent** (`role`, `section_plan`) and refused where it disagrees with them, so no earlier source moved; the export stays 1.3 and writes `plan_type` only where the source states it. The window's «Type de plan» combo, under the ID, replaces v0.114's «Ce plan est une coupe»; a **section** hides the storey and the numbering character and the «Coupe…» link and offers the storey lines, the storey boxes and the load transfer level; a **load-path plan** takes every usual tool and the six load symbols and keeps the storey rows; **switching the type removes what the new type does not carry**, in one step of «Annuler», the status line saying how many entries went, and a tool the type lacks is refused. The list says « · coupe » or « · descente de charges » after such a plan.
**What v0.117 added to a plan** (the engineer's message of 07.10.2026; spec `2026-10-07-performance-erreurs-plans-legendes`). ⚠️ **`plan-source` 1.4**: a column (S) or a point Auflager (M) may carry its **`angle`**, in degrees, clockwise on the drawing — its square drawn turned about its centre (the engineer: «un carré comme aujourd'hui, qu'il est possible de tourner») —, which 1.3 refused on anything but a load symbol, hence the version; any other element is still refused one. An element of the types with a length (`plan.LENGTH_TYPES`: V, H, W, T, D) may carry **`show_length`**, true or false, over the plan's `show_lengths`, which writes since v0.117 the walls', the beams' and each side of a slab's length — one rule, `plan.shows_length`, nothing without a scale. **The legend's quantities** (`plan.quantities`, `plan_svg.legend_items`): on a plan with a scale each legend line carries `quantity` — the lines summed in metres, the areas in m² (the outline's area, `plan.ring_area`, fire.py's own since), the points counted —, additive, so the export stays **1.3**; `plan-legend` prints them under the symbols («Métrés», `plan.legend.quantities`, `plan.legend.pieces`), the decimal a point, and a sheet's legend adds up the quantities of its plans. **«Coupe…»** opens the section it links and arms its storey line on the plan's storey. **An export document** (`Plans/bundles.json` **1.1**, `schemas/bundles.schema.json`) may state its `id`, `description` and `storey`; absent, each is its first plan's — the plan's id, label and storey, read and never copied (`sheets.bundle_fields`) —, and the id names its PDF, `Plans/<id>.pdf` (a bundle whose plan id names a sheet or another bundle falls back on its name).

⚠️ **The six load symbols** of STE Compact 02 pp. 14–15 are two types, `PL` (point load) and `LL` (line load; `plan.LOAD_TYPES`), each with a subtype `vert` (orange), `horiz` (violet) or `vh` (both), and an optional `angle` on a point load: a vertical point load is an orange square with a leader to an orange label box, a horizontal one a violet double arrow with its label, both an orange square on the violet double arrow with the label boxed black; a vertical line load an orange dotted line with orange end ticks, a horizontal one a violet double line, both the violet double line with orange dots inside. They are numbered `P`/`L` + the storey's numbering character + two digits (`plan.next_load_label`), free text after. ⚠️ **A load is not a position**: it is excluded from the labels, from the warnings, from the missing rows the window copies for Positionen.xlsx and from «Ajouter aux positions», and it is allowed on a load-path plan only; it carries no magnitude, which §8 forbids a plan to carry (§13). Its legend keys are `PL:*` and `LL:*`, its entry takes the three style factors, and `validation.PLAN_KINDS` names it.

⚠️ **The load transfer level** is a section-only collection, `load_levels` — `{storey, floor?, points, label_offset?}`, no style of its own —: a red dash-dot line of two clicks (Shift keeps it horizontal), a red arrow pointing down onto each end and a movable label «Niveau de reprise des charges (x0xx)» built from a chosen storey's numbering character, with a leader once it is moved. A section that carries one exports **figures** per language (`plans/<id>-<lang>.svg`, German being the overlay's), since the label carries words; `plan-figure` then wraps in a `context` and reads the language's file, and the record exports `load_levels` with their designations. ⚠️ **A storey box carries STE's designation at its centre**, «x» + the numbering character + «xx» (`x1xx` for the first floor), set between the `<!-- storey-boxes -->` markers, and **no storey name at its top left corner any more**; the box may carry its own `floor` (written when it is drawn; else derived — the attic above the highest, else the storey's name). The ends of a storey line and the four corners of a storey box are **snap points** (§10quater), and so are a load transfer level's two ends.

⚠️ **Another plan shown over the open one** (`superposition`, optional, additive, no `schema_version` moved; the field is named so because `plans.json`'s records already have an `overlay`, the symbols' SVG): `{plan, elements, opacity, colour, tint_elements, offset}` — the id of the plan shown, whether its elements are drawn too, the opacity from 0.05 to 1, the colour (`normal`, `grey`, `green`, `blue`, `yellow`, `red`, `pink`), whether its elements take that colour as well, and the **offset**, a translation in the open sheet's units. It is **a display aid and is never exported or printed** (`build_record` is unchanged, the frozen hashes still hold); a missing plan is not an error, the module says so. Both plans being calibrated, the other plan's point q is shown at s·q + offset, s the ratio of the two scales (1 where either has none, which the module says); the offset is absolute, starts so that the two crops' top-left corners coincide, and is set by **two clicks** — a point of the plan shown, then the point of the open plan it must meet (`superpose-align`, one undo step; «Réinitialiser l'alignement»). The elements of the plan shown **cannot be selected**; its points are snap targets behind «Accrocher aux éléments».

⚠️ **Documents, frames and layers** (`calc/domains/plan_documents.py`, `plan_pages.py`). `plan-document` **1.1** adds an optional `owner`, `plan` or `fire`, written when a document is imported; absent, a document is the fire tab's if it carries only fire plans of an own frame and the position tab's otherwise (`plan_pages.document_owner`), so the documents of a project made before this version go where they belong without being rewritten; the other kind's frames that follow a plan stay shown, muted. A page may carry **several frames**: the tick makes a plan of the whole page, the **first** frame drawn on it crops that plan, **each further frame is a plan of its own** (the next free id and a storey proposal), and a frame drawn on an unticked page ticks it; unticking, or Delete on selected frames, archives their plans and asks once if one holds elements. Layers are switched by an **incremental update of the PDF** (a few kB, both xref forms, pixel-identical), the visible pages rendered first at screen resolution with the old image kept, a combination seen before from the cache, and **the plans' export run 1.2 s after the last toggle in a thread** (`LAYER_EXPORT_MS`, finished before any other export, before leaving and before closing); `flatten_page` is cached on disk keyed by the file's hash (`FLATTEN_VERSION`).

⚠️ **The documents a plan is drawn on** (`calc/domains/plan_documents.py`), in a project laid out by folders (v0.119, issue #64, D-09, D-18): copied at their import into `Plans/Position-plan/Imported Plans/` -- `Plans/Fire-plan/Imported Plans/` for the fire plans' -- under their own name, from anywhere, the original untouched, the document naming the copy; a file of that name with other bytes is `doc_replace`, which the window asks about (`plan_doc_replace_ask`) before the copy is written over and the document that names it read again, the plans drawn on it following by its id; one written over there from outside Woodcore is read again -- pages, layers, SHA-256, the rotations of the pages it still has kept -- as a tab of plans is shown (`write_refresh_documents`, a writer of `WRITERS`, not called on a project open read-only) and at the export (`refresh_all`). A document imported before is copied there by the migration and by every export after it (`adopt`). In a project laid out before nothing is copied, as before.

⚠️ **Sheets** (§10bis, §10quater): `Plans/bundles.json` (`woodcore-sheet-bundles` 1.0, written whole and atomically, one that cannot be read never overwritten) holds the **PDF bundles** — a name and the ordered ids of the sheets it merges — and each sheet's `.typ` states its kind, `sheet(kind: "position"|"fire")` (a sheet written before v0.116 is read off its objects, position by default); `data/plans.json` records gain `name`, additive, for the plan title.

⚠️ *Retired by the spec 2026-10-21-commandes-typst-refonte (lot 19): `show-plan-quantities` -- write #include "/Positions/quantities.typ" -- the quantities written once in raw Typst by the «Métré» tab. What follows describes it as it was.* **What issue #9 added to a plan** (the engineer's issue When-I-hit-the-wall/Woodcore#9, 08.10.2026; spec `docs/requirements/completed/2026-10-08-plan-positions-cotes-numerotation-sections.md`). ⚠️ **`plan-source` 1.5**: a plan carries its **dimensions**, a collection `dimensions` of its own -- `{kind, points, axis?, offset?, style?}`, `kind` `horizontal`, `vertical` or `parallel` (two points), `chain` (three or more on one line, its `axis` one of the three) or `angle` (the corner, then a point on each side) --, **points of the sheet and not references** (a plan has no lasting name for an element, so a dimension does not follow an element moved), and **its value is never stored**: `plan.dimension_parts` computes it from the points and the plan's scale, with the geometry the static models and the schemas share (`calc/domains/dimension.py`). A length on a plan with **no scale** is not drawn and is a warning (`plan_dimensions_no_scale`); an angle needs none. `plan_svg` draws them between `<!-- dimensions -->` markers written **only** where there is one, so a plan without is drawn byte for byte as before; the export adds `dimensions` to a record (values and unit), additive, the export staying **1.3**. `figure_style` lets a dimension state `line` and `text`. ⚠️ **The lengths beside the elements** are drawn after every number, between `<!-- lengths -->` markers written only where one is drawn (`tests/test_plan_style.py`'s `FROZEN` taken again once, the lines unchanged). ⚠️ **One measure of a length**: `plan.distance_m`, `plan.element_length_m` and `plan.metres_text` are what the drawing, the export, the legend's quantities, the ruler, the dimensions and the summary read. **Typst**: `show-plan`, `doc-plan` and `sheet-viewport` gain `dimensions:` and `lengths:`, both true by default and false taking their markers' part out; `calc-block`'s details print no dimension. **The quantities** (`calc/domains/quantities.py`, `data/quantities.json` 1.0): one row per element that carries a position -- its length along a line, its area for a slab, its volume for timber alone (rec, cir: length × section; pla, clt on a slab: area × t; a wall's and a column's height are not on a plan; a steel profile's area is in SZS C5 and in no file of Woodcore) -- and the totals of the project and of each plan by material and section, printed by `show-plan-quantities(scope: "all"|"plans"|"positions")` and on a sheet by `sheet-quantities`, which add up nothing but the rows of chosen positions (held equal to `quantities.summary`). ⚠️ **Numbers** (D-007, D-009): an element drawn takes the next number of its letter on the plan's storey -- `positions_store.propose_id` over the project's positions and the plan's own unsaved numbers, S101, S102 …, S201 --, **a number is one object** (two elements no longer share one, except on a plan drawn before, which is read as it was), and **nothing is written while drawing**: «Enregistrer» makes the positions the plan draws and the project lacks, with the section chosen or the last of their type's, in one write with the plan; a position no plan draws any more is then offered for deletion, and the ones after it are numbered again everywhere (`calc/renumbering.py`, docs/domain/positions.md §7).

**Construction aids, and an object moved or copied (source 1.6, issue #18).** A plan may carry `aids`: lines without ends the plan is laid out with, `{kind, point, angle}` -- kind horizontal, vertical, two-points or parallel, saying only how it was made --, stored as an axis without bubbles is, drawn and snapped to in Woodcore behind «Accrocher aux axes» (`SnapIndex`), turned with its page (`rotate_source`, which turns the dimensions too since), checked by `check_aids`, and never read by `plan_svg`, the export or a sheet. A file of 1.5 has none and is read as it is; a plan saved since is 1.6. An object of the plan -- an element, an axis, a storey line or box, a load level, a dimension, an aid -- is moved by `translate_entry` (`shifted`, to a hundredth of a sheet unit, the drags' own) and copied by `copy_entry`: a copied element keeps its position number, a copied axis is named by where it lands (`insert_axis`). The geometry of an aid made through two points or parallel at a distance is `calc/domains/lines.py` (§13).

**Several objects, and copying between plans (issue #61).** The canvas selects several objects -- Ctrl+click adds one or takes it out, Ctrl+A takes everything the plan holds but the building's outline -- and «Déplacer», «Supprimer», «Copier» and «Dupliquer» act on them all, in one step of «Annuler», «Copier» and «Dupliquer» leaving the outline (a plan has one). Ctrl+C asks for a base point and takes the selection into one clip of the process; Ctrl+V on this plan or another puts it down, its base on the point clicked (`calc/domains/plan_clip.py`, no Qt): between two plans that both have a scale the geometry is scaled about the base -- every `points`, an axis's or an aid's `point`, a storey box's `x`, `y`, `w`, `h`, a dimension's `offset` --, never `label_offset`, `angle`, `diagonal`, `span` or a `style`; every element takes the next number of its prefix on the target's storey (`propose_label`, two occurrences of one position sharing their new one, a load the plan's next load number), the new positions taking the type and section of those copied, read at Ctrl+C, as pending positions until the plan is saved; a pasted outline replaces the target's; what the target cannot hold -- storey lines and boxes and load levels off a section, a load off a load-path plan, an outline on a section, an axis already there -- is left out and counted, by `check_source`'s own predicates. No file changes: a pasted plan is a plan drawn by hand, written at the version what it holds asks for (`version_of`). A clip is the project's and pasted on a position plan alone (decisions.md §13, issue #61).

**The stiffness centre and the mass centre (source 1.8, issue #54)** (the engineer's issue When-I-hit-the-wall/woodcore#54, 13.10.2026; spec `docs/requirements/completed/2026-10-13-plan-positions-centre-rigidite-centre-masse.md`; `calc/domains/plan_centres.py`, Qt-free). A floor plan and a load-path plan show where a horizontal force moves the floor without turning it, **S**, and where the earthquake acts, **M** -- a bracing laid out symmetrically keeps the two close. ⚠️ **Both are computed from the drawing and stored nowhere**: the plan's file holds what they are computed from, and `plan_centres.state` is what the window, the drawing and the export all ask.
- **The method is a document and no norm** (§2 rule 7): «Centre de rigidité d'un étage — calcul mécanique général» (12.10.2026), reproduced whole in the spec's Annexe A, its sections 5 and 9. With each wall's stiffness `k` along its plane, its unit direction `(c, s)` and a point `(x, y)` of its line of action, its lever arm is `r = x·s − y·c`; the storey's stiffnesses are `K_xx = Σ k·c²`, `K_yy = Σ k·s²`, `K_xy = Σ k·s·c`, `K_xθ = Σ k·c·r`, `K_yθ = Σ k·s·r`; and with `D = K_xx·K_yy − K_xy²`, `x_S = (K_xx·K_yθ − K_xy·K_xθ) / D` and `y_S = (K_xy·K_yθ − K_yy·K_xθ) / D` -- the barycentre of the stiffnesses where the walls are parallel to the axes. No SIA norm gives it: SIA 261:2020 uses the stiffness centre as known, SIA 265:2021 does not name it. **Its assumptions** are the document's: a floor rigid in its plane, walls linear elastic that carry forces in their own plane only, one storey at a time. **What it does not cover**: a flexible floor, several storeys, a core whose wings work together, the non-linear slip of a timber wall's fasteners.
- **The walls** are the plan's H and W elements and no other -- a bearing wall (V) and a concrete wall drawn as an area (B «wand») do not count --, one wall per **straight run**: a wall that turns a corner is as many walls as it has runs, and segments of one element that follow each other along one line (within half a degree, `ALIGNED_DEG`) are one wall of their whole length.
- **A wall's stiffness is its length to a power its construction mode fixes** (the engineer's rule): `construction` on the element is `timber_frame` (1), `clt` (1.5 or 2, the plan's `clt_power`, the engineer's choice) or `concrete` (3) -- `plan_centres.POWERS`, the one place that says them, the schema's enums held equal by a test. Only the ratios count, so the length is taken in sheet units and S is computed on a plan without a scale. ⚠️ **There is no default mode**, and **S is computed for one mode only**: a wall without a mode, walls of two modes, CLT walls without their power, walls all in one direction (`D` not positive) or no wall at all give no S, each with its reason -- an `engine.Note` the window's card and the warnings both say. Where every line of action passes through one point S is that point, and a remark says the storey has no torsional stiffness.
- **The building's outline** is `outlines`, a list of **one** polygon at most (three points at least, no `style` of its own, refused on an elevation section), drawn with the window's «Contour» tool; **M is its area centroid** (`plan_geometry.centroid`), not the mean of its corners and no barycentre of masses. It is no element: it carries no position, is in no legend line of the symbols, no quantity and no list (§13).
- **Printing** is the plan's own switch, `print_centres`: the window always draws the three (`plan_svg.render(centres=True)`), the export where the plan says so. They are written between `<!-- centres -->` markers -- two pairs, the outline under the symbols and the two centres over the numbers --, S a ring crossed by a cross in the bracing walls' violet with its letter, M the quartered disc of a centre of gravity with its letter, the outline a thin broken line; the legend gains a line for each one drawn (`Steifigkeitszentrum`, `Massenzentrum`, `Umriss`, marked `centres: true`), and the plan's record a `centres` block -- `{source, reference, unit, outline?, mass?, stiffness?}`, S with the mode, the power and the number of walls it was computed from. A plan that prints none is exported **byte for byte** as before. Asked for and not computable is a **warning** (`plan.warnings`), as is a bracing wall whose position has a `clt` section and which is drawn in another mode (`sections=`, the positions' section types, which `export_all.py`, `plan_pages.export_drawings` and the window hand over).
- **Versions.** `plan-source` **1.8** is written only by a plan that carries one of the four fields (`plan_centres.uses_centres`), and **contains 1.7**: `plan.version_of` gives the highest version a plan needs -- 1.6, 1.7 with a text, 1.8 --, every writer of a plan asks it, and a 1.0 to 1.7 file is read as it is. `data/plans.json` is **1.5** where a plan carries `centres`, containing 1.4. **Typst**: `show-plan`, `doc-plan` and `sheet-viewport` take `centres:` (true by default; false takes the markers' part out, and the marked lines out of `plan-legend`), and `calc-block`'s details print none. A fire-protection plan has none of it (§3.3vicies).
