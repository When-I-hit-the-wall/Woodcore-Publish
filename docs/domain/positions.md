# Positions

> Positions are Woodcore's own data: type, material, section, moisture class.
>
> Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md (§7) on 2026-10-07. The § numbers are kept as stable anchors: `docs/README.md` maps every § to its file.

## 7. Positions: type, material, section (v0.14)

**Laid out by folders (v0.119, issue #64):** positions.json lies in `Positions/` (`Project.positions_dir`), and `Positions/Positions.xlsx` is written from it at every change -- the Positions tab, a renumbering, a plan making positions -- and at an export where it is missing (`positions_store.write_workbook`): the sheet and the headers of Positionen.xlsx, **never read**; positions.json is the source.

**The dimension is typed in boxes since v0.117** (docs/ui/window.md §10quater, «The «Dimension» section»): the window offers a box for each number a type's dimension has — `rec` a width and a height, `pla` a thickness, `cir` a diameter — and writes the same `dimension` string this section describes; `calc/domains/section.py` holds the one description of it (`dimension_fields`, `split_dimension`, `join_dimension`, beside `parse`, which stays the judge). No field of the record, no `schema_version` and no export changes, and a dimension nobody touched is never rewritten.

Each position (`Positionen.xlsx`'s `Positions Element` sheet — `Positions` until v0.33, when the connectors got a sheet of their own, §7bis — one row per element) carries exactly eight fields, nothing else — seven descriptive ones, and since v0.36 the moisture class the element is used in:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `position` | id, `<TypeSymbol><NNN>` (e.g. `T001` for a `Träger`/beam, `S002` for a `Stütze`/column — see the `data_type` sheet's symbol legend, entered by hand, not computed) |
| `type` | structural element type, e.g. `Träger` |
| `material` | what the element is made of, e.g. `Fichte`, `Stahl`. Until v0.77 it held the strength class too; the engineer split the two. **Facultative** (v0.16): leave the cell blank and the summary line simply omits it |
| `resistance class` | the strength class (Festigkeitsklasse), e.g. `C24`, `GL24h`, `S355` — exported as `strength_class` (v0.77), what a connector's timber and a schema element's label «T001 C24 100/200» read. **Facultative** |
| `section type` | the section's type (v0.94): `rec` for a rectangle, its dimension «b/h»; `cir` a circle, «d»; `pla` a plate, «t»; `clt` a cross-laminated panel, its layers «20/30/20/30/20» (t their sum) — in mm, cm or m; or one of the 24 families of SZS C5:2018's designations (p. 154: IPE, IPEA, IPN, UPE, UPN, HEA, HEB, HEM, HD, HL, T, IPET, HEAT, HEBT, L, RND, VKT, ROR, RRK, RRW, FLA, FLB, BLE, ZNP), whose dimension is the designation's size, «101.6x10». **Facultative**: empty, the dimension stays descriptive, except a «b/h» read as `rec` with a warning naming the position |
| `dimension` | descriptive text, parsed nowhere but by a detail schema's element for its default b and h (§3.3decies): `"<b>/<h>"` for a cross-section (`"140/400"`), a bare thickness for a panel (`"180"`), or whatever else describes the element. **Facultative** (v0.87) |
| `units` | the unit that text is given in, e.g. `mm` — printed right after the dimension, and never without it. **Facultative** (v0.87) |
| `moisture class` | the element's moisture class (Feuchteklasse), `1`, `2` or `3` — headed `Wood moisture class` until v0.77, which is still read — — exported as `moisture_class`, the one column read as a number (below). **Facultative**: left blank for an element that is not timber |
| `description` | free text |

These eight columns are the **whole** of a position: `export_all.py` builds `data/positions/<id>.json` from the row and nothing else, and appends an empty `"domains": {}` (§3.1). No span, no influence width, no load id — see §8.

**`dimension` is descriptive and nothing else** (v0.14). `export_all.py` copies the cell into `data/positions/<id>.json` as the string it was typed as — a numeric cell like a panel thickness is coerced to text — and no code splits, parses, or does arithmetic on it but the one lenient reader named at the end of this paragraph. A beam is `"140/400"`, a CLT deck is `"180"`, and neither needs a special case, because neither is ever read back as a number. **Dimensions a calculation needs are given manually in Typst**, at the point of calculation; the earlier `{b, h, unit}` object (and the `DIMENSION_COLUMN` constant that pinned the old `dimension (b/h)` header) is gone. There is no shared section catalog to reference either (§3.2) — if two positions happen to share a cross-section, that's just two identical strings, not a foreign key. The unit of that text lives in the sheet's `units` column (v0.15) rather than being typed into the dimension itself, so `show-position` prints `140/400 mm` from two separate cells. ⚠️ **One exception since v0.75**: a detail schema's element reads its b and h off its position's `dimension` (`schema.parse_section`: two positive numbers apart by `/`, `x` or `×`, in mm, cm or m), as a default the element may write over and never as a stop — a «180» panel simply has no b/h. It is the one reader of this text in the pipeline, and it is lenient on purpose (§3.3decies, §13).

`material`, `description`, and since v0.87 `dimension` and `units` are **facultative** (the engineer: «les dimensions d'une position ne sont pas obligatoires») — a blank cell (or the column missing from the sheet entirely) comes back as `""` through `_column(..., optional=True)`, passes validation (`validate_positions_business` no longer requires a dimension), and is dropped from the summary line rather than printed as an empty segment. `position` and `type` remain required. ⚠️ `position.schema.json` keeps `dimension` and `units` **required keys**, as it keeps `material`: the export always writes them, `""` where the sheet has nothing. A **unit is never printed alone**: `show-position` — and so `calc-block`'s header — leaves out a blank dimension with its unit, and reads a record with no `dimension`/`units` key at all; Woodcore's position combos go through `connector.position_dimension(record)` («100/200 mm», «180», or `""`), where the plan and schemas tabs printed «mm» alone, and `schema._section_reasons` says «—».

⚠️ *Retired by the spec 2026-10-21-commandes-typst-refonte (lot 19): `moisture-class-of` -- the moisture class is the position's own, set in Woodcore's «Positions» tab: write it in the text where the report names it. What follows describes it as it was.* **The moisture class is the one column read as a number** (v0.36). It is a condition the element is used in, which the calculations of that element will take their factors from, not a description, so `export_all.py`'s `_moisture_class()` exports it as the number `1`, `2` or `3` under the key `moisture_class` (typed as a number, or as text `"2"`), and as `null` where the cell is blank — a concrete wall or a support has none. Anything else stops the export naming the position (`Positions Element, T001: moisture class is 4 -- expected 1, 2, 3 or nothing`, the header named as the sheet spells it); the allowed classes are SIA 265 Tabelle 3's, read by `validation.moisture_classes()` from `calc/norms/sia265-2021-tab03-feuchteklassen.json` (a restated `MOISTURE_CLASSES` until v0.77), and `validate_positions_business` checks the record again. Its cells are facultative but **its column is required**: a header renamed in Excel would otherwise give every element no moisture class without a word, and the first calculation to need one would find none. The header is pinned as `MOISTURE_COLUMN`, spelt as the engineer typed it. `show-position` prints it after the section, `T001: Träger, Fichte C24, 100/200 mm, Feuchteklasse 1`, so it appears in `Sections/003-baustoffe.typ`'s list and in every `calc-block` header; a blank one is left out. A calculation written in Typst reads it with `moisture-class-of("T001")` (§10bis), a Python domain module off the position record. A connector reads it through its members' positions when the connection states no class of its own (§3.3nonies); no element calculation reads it yet (§14).

**The strength class has a column of its own (v0.77).** The engineer split `material` into what the element is made of and its strength class, `resistance class`, exported as `strength_class`; `show-position` prints the two together, `T001: Träger, Fichte C24, 100/200 mm, Feuchteklasse 2`. Everything that read `material` as a strength class reads `connector.strength_class_of(record)` now — `known_positions`, a connector member's grade, `schema.element_section` and so the label — which falls back to `material` for a record that carries no `strength_class` key at all, i.e. one exported before the split, and never for one whose strength class is merely blank: `Fichte` is not a strength class. The key is optional in `position.schema.json` and the version stays 1.2, the change being additive. ⚠️ `MOISTURE_COLUMN` is `moisture class`, and `Wood moisture class` is still read (`MOISTURE_COLUMN_OLD`), so an older workbook is not refused for a header alone.

⚠️ *Retired by the spec 2026-10-21-commandes-typst-refonte (lot 19): `member` -- write #position(id) for the member's line; its checks are #include domain-file("check", id). What follows describes it as it was.* **The section type has a column of its own, and the section is read once (v0.94).** The engineer: «dans le excel positions.xlsx, il doit y avoir une colonne pour le type de section … La manière de donner la section (b/h pour rec, d pour cir) doit également changer». `section type` stands after `resistance class` (`export_all.SECTION_TYPE_COLUMN`, a ninth column beside the eight above) and `calc/domains/section.py` is **the one reader** of a dimension: it reads the text for its type and writes the position's `section` (§3.1) — `schema.parse_section`, the lenient reader of v0.75, is gone, and `#member` and a schema element read the `section` rather than the text. A dimension that does not fit its type, and a type the list does not know, stop the export naming the row; an empty type leaves the dimension descriptive, as it always was, but a «b/h» is read as `rec` and said in a warning, so that a workbook written before v0.94 keeps the sections it had. The steel families are codes and names (`calc/norms/szs_c5.py`, `szs-c5-2018-edv-kurzbezeichnungen.json`), and a position's steel section carries the numbers of its designation and no property; **since v0.118 the profile tables are transcribed** (`szs-c5-2018-profile-*.json`) and the steel checks read the properties from them through the designation (§3.3quatervicies). The type `USR` names a free profile of `Woodcore-Datei/profiles/` by its id (`position` 1.5, kind `user`; docs/domain/steel.md). ⚠️ **Woodcore writes the sheet since v0.94** (`calc/positions_workbook.py`, §10quater's *Positions* tab), reversing v0.34's rule that it never did (§13): a row added, changed, renamed or deleted, the next free number proposed (`propose_id`, a gap never refilled, ids compared ignoring case), the `section type` column added where a workbook lacks it, with its list on `data_type` and a drop-down refusing anything else (`ensure_section_column`). What openpyxl drops — the engineer's drop-down lists, written as the x14 extension Excel uses for a list on another sheet — is read before and put back after it saves, moved as Excel would move it where a column is inserted or a row deleted; what cannot be kept safely is refused before the file is touched. A write goes to a file beside the workbook, is read back and checked, and only then replaces it; a workbook Excel holds (`~$`, a file held open, read-only) is refused, a brief hold by NextCloud or an antivirus waited out for 3 s, every refusal a `WorkbookError` with a code the window translates. ⚠️ **`show-position` is gone** (v0.94): wherever this document names it, `#member(id, level: 1)` prints the same line — `T001: Träger, Fichte C24, 100/200 mm, Feuchteklasse 1` — for any position (§3.3undecies).

**A position's type is displayed in the language of the project** (v0.116, §4bis). `type` is stored once, as its STE key; `positions_store.type_label` and `type_key` translate it for the window — a combo whose data is the key and whose text is the translation, a search that finds a position by its translated type — and the report prints it through the glossary at `position-line`, so `#member` at level 1 and a `calc-block` header follow the compiled document's language. A free type stays as typed; the letter of the id is unchanged.

Every `Positions Element` column is read through `export_all.py`'s `_column()` helper, which fails with a blocking `export failed: Positions Element: no column '<name>' -- found: ...` if a header was renamed in Excel, instead of the bare `KeyError` traceback that a direct row lookup produced. A workbook that still has a sheet `Positions` and no `Positions Element` is refused with the message that the sheet was renamed.

`export_all.py` also writes **`data/positions-index.json`** — the ids of every position it exported, in `Positions Element` sheet order:

```json
{"schema_version": "1.0", "positions": ["T001", "S001"]}
```

It exists for exactly one reason: **Typst cannot list a directory**, so a report that wants to show *every* position has to be handed the list. It is validated like any other artifact (§3.0/§3.6) against `schemas/positions-index.schema.json`, and a duplicate id is a blocking error — two `Positions` rows sharing an id would silently overwrite each other's `data/positions/<id>.json`. `lib/calc-engine.typ` reads it with `ids("position")` (a data loader sitting next to `load-position`/ `load-calculations`, not a display command — there is no new `show-*` here), and `Sections/003-baustoffe.typ` loops over it:

```typ
#for id in ids("position") {
  position(id, category-show: ("type", "material", "strength-class", "descr"))
  parbreak()
}
```

Adding a row to the `Positions` sheet therefore adds that element to the report's material list with no Typst edit at all.

`Positionen.xlsx` also carries one reference-only sheet, which the engineer fills in as needed and no Python code reads: `data_type`, the `Symbol -> type` legend used to pick a position's id prefix by hand.

**Numbered again, deleted, renamed, kept (issue #9, 08.10.2026; spec `docs/requirements/completed/2026-10-08-plan-positions-cotes-numerotation-sections.md`).** ⚠️ **A position deleted takes the numbers after it down by one**, everywhere: S110 deleted, S111 becomes S110 and S130 S129 -- in `positions.json`, the plans' labels (never a load's `L101`), the member checks' `position`, the schema elements' `position` and every `.typ` of the three documents and of `Plans/` (never `_woodcore/`, the archives, a form, the journal). `calc/renumbering.py` computes a **preview** first -- the table, what still cites a position deleted, every occurrence with its line, each of which may be unticked (a steel grade «S355» in prose when a position S355 exists) -- and writes **all or nothing** (`sharing.commit`, positions.json last) after copying every file into the archives with a note of intent; it is **no step of «Annuler»**, the copy is how it is undone (`renumbering.restore`), and a renumbering left half done is listed by `renumbering.unfinished`. The JSON sources are rewritten token by token, the `.typ` files by one pass of one expression (an id whole: not S1100, not `#D101`, not `S101.png`, not `S110-old`). ⚠️ **A position still cited is refused**, and one still drawn on a plan too; **«Copier les valeurs»** deletes a cited one all the same: its values are kept as `S110-old` (then `-old2`) in `Woodcore-Datei/positions-archive.json` (`schemas/positions-archive.schema.json` 1.0), its citations rewritten to that name, the export writes it as a position with `archived: {was, deleted}` listed apart in the index (`archived`), and the report prints, wherever it is called (`#member`, `calc-block`, `show-check-member`), «Calcul sur les valeurs de l'ancienne position S110» in orange. **A rename is the same table of one entry**: the citations are rewritten, not broken. This **replaces** «a number deleted is never taken again» (`propose_id`'s rule) for the plans, where numbers stay continuous by the cascade. **The section in fields**: `section.FORMS` is the one table of what a type's dimension holds, form by form, read off SZS C5's profile tables for the steel families (ROR and RND in **D**, RRK/RRW h·b·t then a·t, HD h or h·m, L a·t or a·b·t, BLE t, b·t or b·l·t …), `STEEL_NUMBERS` and `STEEL_FORMS` derived from it; a CLT panel is a field a layer. `calc/tools/section_fields.py` is the widget the Positions tab and the plan's pane share.

**The lengths and the quantities in the Positions tab (issue #14).** A position's drawn length -- every element of it on every position plan, added up -- and the quantities of what the plans draw, by material and section, are shown in the Positions tab and computed nowhere but in `calc/domains/quantities.py` (`length_by_position`, `summary`), on the plans `plan.position_plan_sources` reads -- the export's own reading for data/quantities.json. The length is no field of a position: positions.json never holds it (docs/ui/window.md §10quater).
