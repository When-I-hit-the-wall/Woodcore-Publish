### 3.3vicies Fire-protection plans: a second kind of plan on the same documents (v0.112 on Gian's branch, ported onto the position plan's code in v0.115)

The engineer asked for Gian's fire-protection plan (Brandschutzplan) on master, **extremely similar to the position plan** and built on the same code, and answered two rounds of questions on 06.10.2026; §13 records each. A fire-protection plan shows a floor's fire concept — what keeps a fire in, what lets people out — over the architect's plan.

**Source.** `Woodcore-Datei/fire/<id>.json`, written by Woodcore's «Plan de protection incendie» tab and read by `calc/domains/fire.py` alone — the position plans' arrangement (§3.3sexies, §13). `schemas/fire-source.schema.json` states its shape, **`schema_version` 2.0**. It holds the plan's id, name and description, its **frame**, its **style** (`line`, `text`: there is no symbol on a point here, so `point` is no factor of a fire plan), the **six collections** and a **legend** box.

⚠️ **The frame is one of two.** A plan that names a position plan (`plan`) **follows** it: its background (a file or a page of an imported document, with the document's rotation and layers), sheet, crop and scale are read afresh from that plan at every reading, so a change made in «Plan de positions» reaches here — one source for one thing. The one field it may state for itself is its **crop** (a position plan often carries three floors on one sheet and a fire plan is one floor): absent, the position plan's is taken, present it is this plan's own and stays so, with «Reprendre» — the pattern of a value that follows another source and may be written over (a schema element's b and h, v0.75). A plan that names none carries a **frame of its own**, in the very four fields a position plan states, checked by the very function (`plan.check_frame`, `background.document` included), on a page of an imported document or, for a plan of Gian's, on a file named directly.

⚠️ **1.0 is read as it is.** Gian's sources (schema 1.0, a plain-file background) are valid unchanged in 2.0, which adds `background.document` — plan-source 1.2's own field — and nothing else a 1.0 file lacks. `fire.upgrade` copies a source and sets its version; `read_source` checks the upgraded copy; **nothing is written until the plan is saved**, and the tab then writes it as 2.0. Two of his real plans are the migration fixture, `tests/projet-exemple/Woodcore-Datei/fire/`, unchanged.

| collection | what one entry holds |
|---|---|
| `zones` | a closed outline drawn by hand, of one of three kinds: a vertical escape route, a horizontal one, a surface to be kept clear; a name and the area, which the drawing measures with the frame's scale |
| `walls` | a line and one fire-resistance class of the VKF sheet (`calc/norms/vkf.py`, `vkf-2016-symbole-brandschutzplan.json`), drawn as that class's coloured band |
| `doors` | two points, a swing door (the sheet's quarter disc) or a sliding one (its flat arrow), with the fire resistance the sheet gives a door (EI 30, E 30) or **none** — which is no symbol of the sheet and is drawn in outline —, where its hinge is and to which side of the wall it opens |
| `routes` | an escape route's length: a thin green arrow with the length along it |
| `exits` | an emergency exit: the thick green arrow, whose place, direction and size are its two points |
| `widths` | the width of a passage: a green figure across it |

Ids are unique across the whole plan and a text (a surface's name, a length, a width) is moved clear by `label_offset`, from its own place, so that it follows its entry. **Nothing is delimited by the program and nothing is checked against a VKF limit**: an area, a length and a width are the drawing's own, measured with the frame's scale; Gian's automatic proposal of rooms (`fire_zones.py`, numpy) stays dropped, a proposal that needs correcting on every plan costing more than the outlines it saves.

**One entry may be drawn heavier or more opaque than its neighbours.** The three-factor logic of §3.3quaterdecies, with `figure_style.ENTRY_KEYS`' fire rows — a surface and a route state `line` and `text`, a wall and a door `line`, a width `text`, an exit nothing — and, beside them, an **`opacity`** an entry may state; the legend has a size of its own (`legend.scale`). It is shown in the window as an overridden b/h is: orange, with «Reprendre».

**Export.** `fire.prepare(source_dir, plans_dir, project_root, data_dir, plans)` reads and checks every source after the position plans have been prepared, and builds `data/fire.json` (schema `fire` **2.0**, `validation.validate_fire_business`) and, under `data/fire/`, `<id>-<lang>.svg` once per language — the legend carries words — and the background of a plan that has one of its own. A plan that follows is set over that position plan's exported background, which is in `data/` once. Version 2.0 adds **`legend_lines`**, the legend as `[{key, text, swatch, …}]` lines the report sets in its own typeface (as a position plan's, §3.3sexies), so the legend can be left off the plan and printed apart. A background on a document goes through `plan.document_background`, a plain file through `plan.file_background` — both extracted from `plan.prepare` so the two kinds export one way (stem `fire/<id>-plan`). `export_all.py` runs it after `plan.prepare`, writes it and seeds an empty `fire.json`; `project.fire_dir` and `SOURCE_FOLDERS` carry the folder, so the archive and the copy of a project hold it.

**The documents and the turn of a page are shared.** `calc/domains/plan_pages.py` (no Qt) is what both tabs ask, so that neither knows how the other kind is stored: `drawings_on(here, docid, page=None)` gives, for a page, the position plans drawn on it and the fire plans drawn on it **or following a position plan that is**; `rotate_page(here, docid, page, delta)` turns the page in its document and every drawing on it (`plan.rotate_source`, `fire.rotate_source`: every point, a hole's, a text's `label_offset` as a vector, the legend's anchor, and for a plan of its own the crop, the calibration and the sheet; for one that follows only its own crop) — **all checked before the first file is written**, so a refusal leaves everything as it was —; `export_drawings(here)` exports both. A door's `side` is a side of its wall and does not turn.

**In the window** (§10quater): the tab is `FireTab` on `DrawingTab`'s hooks and `FireCanvas` on `PlanCanvas.frame_source()` — the frame the canvas reads, which a fire plan builds from the plan it follows overlaid with its own crop at every call. **The other kind's crops are shown muted in the document view** (`DocumentView`: a crop dictionary may carry `other: true`, drawn dotted in the soft ink, ignored by a click, and a hollow marker beside the tick of a page that carries the other kind).

**In Typst** (§10bis): `#show-fire-plan(plan, width:, legend:, scale-bar:, caption:)` and `fire-plan-ids()` print a plan on `plan-figure`, the arithmetic `show-plan` shares: it reads a record's own **per-language figure** (`p.figures.at(report-lang())`, a fire plan's legend carrying words) and takes **`legend: false`**, which strips the legend between its markers (a no-op for a position plan's overlay, whose pages are byte for byte what they were). A legend line says its own wording — `engine`, else `term`, else the STE key — so a fire line points at its own glossary keys (`fire.legend.*`). **On an export sheet** (`lib/sheet.typ`, §10bis): `sheet-fire-viewport(plan:, x:, y:, scale:, width:, legend:, scale-bar:, caption:)` draws a plan like `sheet-viewport` does a position plan, **with its legend on the plan** while `legend: true`, and `sheet-fire-legend(plans:, x:, y:, width:, title:)` is a legend object of its own, movable, merging the `legend_lines` of the plans it lists — **both are possible**; a fire plan has its own state, `sheet-fire-plans`, ids of the two kinds being free to collide, and a refusal names the fire-protection plan tab. The sheet editor lists them as objects (`+ Plan incendie`, a legend switch). **In the Structure tab**: the object `doc-fire-plan(plan:, legend:, scale-bar:, width:, caption:)` — `show-fire-plan` with a frame standing in its place while no plan is chosen — mirroring `doc-plan` end to end (command, model, pane reading `data/fire.json`, «+ Objet», glossary).

⚠️ **Nothing is computed.** A fire plan is a drawing and carries no `{vars, steps, checks}` record: nothing addresses one with `show-calc`, the rule a position plan, a static model and a schema follow (§3.3sexies, §3.3octies, §3.3decies).

**What v0.116 gave it, and what it did not** (§2 rule 16). The fire plan took **every change of the engine** and none that belongs to the domain. Taken: the tracking and snapping of `PlanCanvas` (a surface's and a polygon's tools snap too, and a dragged entry no longer snaps to itself), the right pane by selection (a legend card beside the symbols' size), the crop and the calibration as cards, the left pane's splitter, **several frames per page** (the first frame on a page that carries one position plan follows it with an own crop, later ones carry a frame of their own — `source_for_frame`, `uncropped_on`), the selection tool in the document view, the fast layers, **documents that belong to one tab** (`owner`), the sheets' kind, options and PDF bundles, and the superposition of another **fire** plan (a fire plan is overlaid with fire plans only, drawn with `fire_svg`, without the legend). Not taken: plan types and the sections, the storey lines and boxes, the load symbols and the load transfer level, and the list of the positions — a fire plan is a floor's concept and lists no positions. A fire plan that follows a position plan is turned with it when a page is turned, as before.

**The ruler (issue #9).** The fire-protection plan has the position plan's ruler -- two clicks, the distance in metres where the plan is calibrated, nothing written -- but **not its dimensions**: the engineer's answer (Q-013), a fire plan measuring what it needs itself (its widths, its escape routes' lengths). A later version may give it the same collection; until then `fire.py` reads no `dimensions`.

**What this plan took of issue #18, and what it did not.** «Déplacer», «Copier» and «Objet sélectionné» (Dupliquer, Supprimer) act on a fire plan's entries as on a position plan's objects -- every entry selected, its points and a surface's islands moved (`fire.translate_entry`), a copy given an id of its own (`fire.copy_entry`, `next_id`) --, through the same tools (`plan_canvas_edit.py`, rule 16). **The construction aids are the position plan's alone for now** (D-015): the fire plan's source is `fire-source` 2.0, whose collections all carry an id and points, which an aid has not; adding them is a version 2.1 of their own, asked for when needed.
