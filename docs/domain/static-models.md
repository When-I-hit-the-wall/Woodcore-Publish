# Static models

> Static systems drawn in Woodcore and exported as figures; nothing solved.
>
> Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md (§3.3octies) on 2026-10-07. The § numbers are kept as stable anchors: `docs/README.md` maps every § to its file.

### 3.3octies Static models: drawn in Woodcore, exported as a figure (v0.51)

**The dimensions' geometry is shared (issue #9).** `statics.dimension_direction`, `dimension_line`, `dimension_corner`, `angle_rays`, `dimension_angle`, `dimension_value`, `dimension_show` and `dimension_text` resolve the anchors and hand the points to `calc/domains/dimension.py`, which the schemas and the position plans read too; the kinds are `dimension.KINDS`. What a static model draws is unchanged (its own `statics_svg`), and its dimension tools show the symbols the three tabs share (`calc/tools/dimension_icons.py`).

A static model (statisches System) is the sketch an engineer draws before a calculation: the members, where they are held, where they are hinged, and what acts on them. **It draws a system; it does not solve one** — no reactions, no N/V/M, no stability check, no cable-slack check. That boundary is the engineer's own request and the one this module is most likely to be asked to cross (§14).

**Source.** A model is drawn in Woodcore's «Modèles statiques» tab (§10quater) and saved as `Woodcore-Datei/statics/<id>.json`, one file per model. Woodcore writes it and nothing else does, which is the connections' and the plans' arrangement, for the same reasons (§3.3quinquies, §3.3sexies, §13). `schemas/statics-source.schema.json` states its shape (`schema_version` 1.1) and `calc/domains/statics.py` checks it.

**The frame is the connection module's** (§3.3quinquies), and for the same reason: metres, **x to the right and z down**, angles in degrees from +x turning towards +z — clockwise as the figure is read, so a load at 90° points down and a vertical load is +z. Nothing is turned over between what the engineer types, what Woodcore shows and what the report prints.

**Ids are unique across the whole model**, not per collection: the report's `highlight` names an id and must not have to ask which collection it meant. A new entry is named by its collection's letter — P a point, B a member (Balken), A a support (Auflager), G a release (Gelenk), F a point load, q a line load.

| collection | what one entry holds |
|---|---|
| `points` | `id`, `x`, `z` in metres, an optional `label`, its `label_offset` and its own `style` (`text` alone, v0.99) |
| `members` | `id`, `kind` (`bar`, `cable` or `spring` — a cable takes a positive normal force only and a spring acts along its own axis, so each is drawn its own way), `p1` and `p2`, existing and distinct, and a spring's own `stiffness` in kN/m; its own `style` (`line`, `text`, v0.99) |
| `supports` | `id`, the `point` it holds, `type` (`fixed` Einspannung, `pinned` Festlager, `roller` Loslager), an `angle`, 0 putting the ground below, and for a Festlager or a Loslager its `hinge`, `shared` (the pin on the node, the default) or `through` (a bar running over the support, the pin set back under it); a `hinge` on an Einspannung is refused, and its own `style` (all three, v0.99). **At most one support per point** |
| `releases` | `id`, the `member`, the `end` (`start`/`end`) and the `dof` (`moment`, `shear`, `normal`), with an optional `stiffness` in `kNm/rad` on a moment release, which makes it a rotational spring, and its own `style` (all three, v0.99) |
| `point_loads` | `id`, the `point` it acts on, the `reference` member its angle is counted from and how far `along` that member it is drawn, the `direction`, an optional `magnitude`, an optional `width`, a `label`, a `show` mode, an optional `color` (v0.87) and its own `style` (`line`, `text`, v0.99) |
| `line_loads` | `id`, the `member`, `from`/`to` along it, the `shape`, an optional `magnitude` and, for a linear one, its `magnitude_end`, plus the same direction, width, label, show, color and `style` |
| `dimensions` | `id`, the `kind` (`horizontal`, `vertical`, `parallel`, `angle`), the two ends `from` and `to`, the signed `offset` the dimension is drawn at, an optional `label` (its name), an optional `show` mode (v0.87) and its own `style` (`line`, `text`, v0.99) |

**One entry may be drawn heavier than its drawing (v0.99).** The model carries three factors — the strokes, the
symbols placed on a node, the texts — and any entry of any collection may carry a **`style` of its own beside
them**, whose factors **multiply** the drawing's: `{"line": 2}` on one member draws that member's stroke at twice
the drawing's, and nothing else on the figure. What the three factors are, how far they may go and which of them
each kind of entry may state is `calc/domains/figure_style.py`, read by the plans, the static models and the
detail schemas alike; `figure_style.allowed("statics", collection)` is the row of its table this module's
collections stand in. ⚠️ **A factor an entry does not draw is refused naming the ones it may state**: a point
draws its label and nothing else — a node is never drawn in the report (v0.56) — so it states `text` and nothing
else; a member draws no symbol on a node, so `point` on one is refused; an arrow is not a symbol on a node
either. A field nobody could see the effect of is a field that acquires a value nobody checks and a meaning
nobody remembers, which is the argument that took `plates.thickness` out of a connector's source in v0.70.
⚠️ **`schema_version` does not move**: a field whose absence means «follows the drawing» is additive, the v0.69
rule (§13), so no file is migrated and a source written before v0.99 is read and drawn exactly as it was — held
by a frozen figure in `tests/test_statics.py`.

**What a factor reaches, and what it deliberately does not.** `statics_svg.layout()` works the pair out once
per entry (`entry_sizes`) and hands it down as that entry's own `sz`, so the symbol, the room it takes in the
figure's frame, its label's box, its leader and the halo `show-static-model` may put under it all come out at
one size — and `meta()` records the factors the entry stated under `style`, so the export says what each part
of the figure was drawn with. **A load's arrow length is carried by its own `line`**, which is the one place a
factor changes more than a weight: two loads of equal magnitude no longer draw equal arrows where the engineer
asked for one of them to stand out. That is deliberate and said out loud — a figure whose arrows are in
proportion to their magnitudes is what a load stating nothing gives. ⚠️ **Two things keep the drawing's own
factors, and they are decisions rather than omissions.** How far a member is drawn back from a node a release
frees (`_release_cuts`) is the **node's** and not one member's: both ends meeting there are drawn back by the
same amount, which is that function's own rule since v0.55, and a cut taken from each member's own `line` would
open a break wider on one side of one node than on the other. And a **spring's zigzag amplitude** follows the
drawing's `point`, a member stating no `point` of its own: a spring drawn with a heavier `line` has a heavier
stroke and the same amplitude, which is what a thicker pen on the same symbol looks like. ⚠️ A dimension's
`offset` is the model's own geometry in metres and no factor reaches it: a heavier dimension is a heavier
dimension in the same place.

**An articulation is a degree of freedom set free at one end of a member**: free in moment (a hinge), in shear or in normal force, and a `stiffness` on a moment release makes it a rotational spring — four words in Woodcore's combo. **A translational spring is not an articulation at all: it is a member of kind `spring`**, which is where an engineer looks for it and where its own stiffness belongs. ⚠️ A shear or normal-force release that still carries a `stiffness` — the form of v0.51's first hours — keeps its freedom and loses the stiffness, with a warning, at the export and when Woodcore opens it (`statics.drop_release_stiffness`, v0.79); until then `check_source` refused it and stopped every model's export (periodic audit 260916, B18).

**A release is drawn on the node it frees**, not beside it, and its two lines say which way the halves may move against each other: **along** the member for a normal-force release, so they are parallel to it; **across** it for a shear release, so they are perpendicular. The member is cut back from that node so the mark is visible.

**Triangular is `linear` with one end zero, trapezoidal is `linear` with two.** Woodcore offers three buttons and fills the two fields; the drawing, the check and the record each keep one code path.

**A load's direction** is `vertical` (down), `horizontal` (along +x), `angle` (the `angle` from +x) or `member` (the `angle` from a member's axis — the reference line the engineer asked for). An angle belongs to the last two and is refused on the first two; a point load in `member` mode names its `reference` member, a line load is measured from the one it runs on. **An angle starts at 90°**, not 0°: a load given at an angle is nearly always across its member or straight down, never along it.

**A point load's `along` is where it is drawn on its reference member**, in metres from the point it acts on, or as a fraction of the member — `0.5l` is midspan. It is what puts a load in the middle of a beam without inventing a point there, and it is the second review's replacement for the dx/dz `offset` with its `eccentric` flag, which is gone from the source, the schema and the drawing.

**A magnitude is either a number or a variable of the export, and it is optional.** `{value, unit}` states it here; `{domain, instance, var}` names a var of `data/calculations.json`, read afresh at every export — so the figure follows the workbook by itself and the source records where the number came from (§2 rules 1 and 5). A reference that no longer resolves, or that names a var whose value is text, is a **warning** and never a stop: that is the v0.33 argument exactly — stopping the export would stop the loads' own export with it, and Woodcore would be left nothing to point the load back at. **A load may also state no magnitude at all** (v0.51, second review): a value is there to be printed and to scale the arrow, and a system sketched before its actions are known has neither. Such a load is drawn at the full arrow length, in its own colour, and shows its name alone — it is a load, not a load that failed to resolve, and the two must not look alike.

**A referenced variable may be taken with a factor and a constant** (v0.51, second review): `factor` multiplies it and `offset` is added in the var's own unit, so a design value stands on the figure beside the characteristic one it comes from — `0.8 · q_k11 + 0.2`. A `description` beside them says what the value is, and goes into the figure's notes. The note gives the expression in letters **and then the number it came to**, which is this report's own rule everywhere else (§10bis): `s_d = 0.8 · q_k11 + 0.2 = 1.307 kN/m² [snow-load/SL-Dach1] × b_2 = 1.5 m – Schnee, abgemindert`. Without that number the line reads as though the variable's name were the value, and a checker cannot follow 1.307 × 1.5 to the 1.96 kN/m on the arrow.

**The influence width is the module's one piece of arithmetic, and the whole model may carry one.** The actions are calculated per square metre and a system carries per metre, so a load may carry `width` — b_e in metres, with the name and the description the figure prints. Since the second review the **model itself** may carry one too, in the source's own `width`: it applies to every load, and a load's own `width` overrides it for that load alone — the exception the engineer asked for, one field rather than a flag. A model's width is said **once**, in the notes at the foot of the figure, never beside each load it applies to; a load's own is said beside that load, as before. One consequence had to be decided: a model's width cannot be applied to a load in kN, and refusing it there would make a single global b_e impossible on any model carrying one point load. So a **model's** width passes silently over a load whose unit it cannot divide, while a **load's own** width on such a unit is still refused by name — the engineer set the width on that load deliberately. The magnitude is multiplied by it and **one metre comes off the unit**: kN/m² → kN/m, kN/m → kN. A unit that is neither per metre nor per square metre has no width to be multiplied by: a value typed on the load stops the export naming it, while a variable of the export that turns out to be one is a warning, the figure writing `?` for it (the var may simply have changed since). Where the width is shown, the figure writes `b_e = 3.6 m` on a second line under the load's own label and its description in the notes under the drawing — a long description would crowd the arrow. The chain stays traceable: the export keeps the value before the width, its unit, its provenance, b_e and the product.

**What a load writes beside itself** is its `show` mode: `none`, `name`, `value` or `name+value` — the four the engineer asked for. A load that **tapers** is written from one end to the other, `0 … 12 kN/m`: neither end on its own is the load the figure draws, and printing the near one alone labelled a triangular load rising to 12 kN/m as `0 kN/m`.

**A load has a colour of its own** (v0.87): an optional `color`, one of five — `red`, `green`, `orange`, `blue`, `violet`, each an ink dark enough for white paper and apart enough to tell two load cases from each other on one figure (`statics.LOAD_COLOURS`, the names and the inks held in one place, which `statics_svg` reads). Its arrows, its band and its cap are drawn in it. **Red is the default and is saved by leaving `color` out**, so a model saved before the choice draws exactly as it did. ⚠️ **Its label stays black**: it is text set on a white box and reads the same whatever the arrows' colour, and colouring it would have changed every existing figure (§13). Woodcore sets it in a *Couleur* column of both load lists and a combo of the load's panel (§10quater). `color`, like a dimension's `show` below, is an optional, additive field of `statics-source` and `statics` (and of `schema-source` and `schemas` for the dimension), so no `schema_version` moved.

**Several loads on one member stand one above the other** (v0.51, second review), not on top of one another: each band sits on the one before it, and the running height is kept per member and per direction — two loads pointing opposite ways on one member are two stacks. Their labels go **inside** their bands, between the arrows' feet and their heads, since a label above a band would be read as belonging to the load above it. A band too shallow to hold its label — one the scale made short, or one carrying its influence width on a second line — has the label step outside past its cap, away from the member, and the load stacked above it then starts beyond the label rather than on it.

**A node is never drawn in the report** (v0.56): a dot there would claim an articulation, and what the report draws at a node it draws as the thing itself -- a hinge's circle, a release's mark, a wall's hatching. Woodcore alone marks the nodes one may click, and not where a release, a fixed support or a shared pin already stands (v0.51, second review; v0.56).

**A dimension measures the model and changes nothing in it** (v0.55). Four kinds, one collection: `horizontal` is the distance along x, `vertical` along z, `parallel` the true distance between the two ends, and `angle` the angle between two members' axes, in degrees. **Each end is an anchor**: `{"point": "P1"}` follows that point as the model is edited -- which is the whole reason an anchor is stored rather than a pair of coordinates -- and `{"x": .., "z": ..}` is a place of its own; for an angle the two ends name members instead (`{"member": "B1"}`). **The `offset` is where the dimension is drawn**, one signed number in metres measured from the **middle of the two ends** at right angles to the dimension line, so the line follows the points as they move. For an angle it is the **radius** of the arc, and a **`bearing`** beside it — the direction of the third click from the corner — says which of the four sectors the two axes cut the plane into is the one measured, the arc then running the short way between the ray of each axis on that side (v0.57). A negative radius writes that sector's reflex. ⚠️ A member's stored direction runs p1 → p2 and is no ray of the corner, which is why the sector cannot be read off the two directions alone; a dimension written before `bearing` takes the ray from the corner towards each member's own body, and its sign, which meant the other side and not a reflex, is ignored. The value is the drawing's own -- a length in metres, an angle in degrees to a tenth, as `show-polar-moment` writes its force-grain angles. **What a dimension writes is its `show` mode** (v0.87), a load's own four: `value`, `name` (its `label`), `name+value` («l = 6.00 m») or `none` (`statics.dimension_show`, `dimension_text`). A name asked for and not given writes nothing, and `name+value` without one writes the value, a load's own rule. ⚠️ A dimension saved before `show` reads as it was drawn then: `name` where it carries a `label` — which until v0.87 replaced the value — and `value` where it does not. A dimension between two ends in the same place, an angle of a member with itself, and an angle between two members that do not cross are each refused by name: there is nothing to measure, and no corner to turn about.

**A dimension is drawn between the structure and the loads, in a region of its own.** A length is a witness line out from each end, starting clear of the model and running a little past the dimension line, the dimension line itself with a slim filled head at either end -- slimmer than a force arrow's, which is what tells the two apart -- and the value over it, turned with its own line and set on the side the line was carried to. An angle is the arc, sampled as a polyline (SVG Tiny has no arc command, `connection_svg`'s rule), a head at either end of it, and a thin tie from the corner out to each end, so an arc drawn beyond the members is still read against the corner it turns about. Both are in the drawing's own ink, set apart by their thinner line rather than by a colour. Their region is marked `<!-- dims -->...<!-- /dims -->` and their values are drawn inside a second one, as the loads' labels are: `dimensions: false` therefore takes a dimension away with its value, `loads: false` leaves it standing, and the two may be used together or apart in the same frame at the same scale.

**The drawing** (`calc/domains/statics_svg.py`) is one SVG, with no background under it, kept to SVG Tiny 1.2 like every other drawing here (§3.3sexies): arrowheads are polygons, a moment spring's spiral is a polyline, a member interrupted by a hinge is drawn as the stretches that clear it, and a label's halo is **a white rectangle**, its own box (`hw`, `hh`, the one a click and a leader read) turned with its text — until v0.77 it was the text drawn a second time with a wide white stroke, which over Woodcore's grid or a member left a «bubble» around every letter, and the engineer asked for a rectangle. **A subscript is a second `<text>`, set smaller and lower** — Tiny has neither `baseline-shift` nor a `<tspan>` both renderers read alike; checked, and QtSvg honours a `<tspan>`'s own advance but neither its `dy` nor its `baseline-shift`, so a subscript written that way would sit on the baseline — and so `b_e` prints as b with a small e, `q_(k,11)` carrying a braced group.

**A subscript is placed where the run before it ends, so how wide the font sets that run has to be right rather than close.** Two things make it right. `ARIAL_WIDTHS` is **Arial's own advance for each character**, measured once from the font and written into the module, which has to hold the numbers rather than ask a font at export time; a flat width per letter was 10 to 20 per cent out on a real word, which over a label like `q_k` is invisible and over a note like `g_k = 2 kN/m² × b_e = 3 m` put the second subscript on top of the text after it. And `FONT` names **one family, `Arial`, not a list**: QtSvg takes the whole `font-family` string as a single name, so `Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif` matched nothing and it fell back to a face whose advances are not Arial's. resvg reads a bare family too, and a machine without Arial substitutes Liberation Sans, which has the same advances — so Woodcore and the report set these texts alike. `plan_svg` names the same one family since v0.79, where it named a list (periodic audit 260916, B20), and `tests/test_constants.py` holds every figure module to one. A run's own leading space is measured rather than written, a `<text>` dropping the spaces at its ends, without which `q_k = 3.458` comes out as `q_k= 3.458`. **A `*` typed into a label is drawn `·`** (v0.87, second half, the engineer: «Un * tapé dans l'étiquette d'une charge d'un modèle statique s'imprime tel quel» — his «1.35*G_1,k»): a load's label and description, a b_e's name and a dimension's name are text and not formulas, so `star-as-dot` (§10quinquies) never reaches them, and an asterisk on a figure reads as a footnote mark. `text_runs` replaces it by U+00B7, the middle dot, where the text is split for drawing and for measuring alike, so a label's box is sized for the character it shows — and not by the dot operator U+22C5, which Arial does not carry (the middle dot's own advance, 682 of 2048 units, is in `ARIAL_WIDTHS`). The source, the lists and `statics.json`'s `text` keep the `*` as it was typed. Woodcore renders the same drawing **without its white ground** (`background: False`), so its grid shows through. **Its frame is computed, not given** — a load arrow reaches well past the last node — so `layout()` places everything and notes the box of each thing, `meta()` hands the placed geometry to the record and `render()` writes the text, which is `connection_svg.py`'s arrangement and not `plan_svg.py`'s.

**The figure's coordinates are not metres**: the model's longer side is normalised to 1000 units and every symbol size is a constant of the module. That is what makes a 2 m model and a 20 m one come out identical — the same figure to the printed digit — and what keeps a font size in the range a renderer reads. ⚠️ QtSvg silently drops a `<text>` of a quarter of a user unit while still painting its white halo, which is what a figure drawn in metres asks it for.

**Two scales, one per kind.** A kN and a kN/m cannot share one, so point loads are scaled among themselves and line loads among themselves; within a kind, equal magnitudes give equal arrows. A magnitude under `MIN_FRACTION` (0.35) of the largest of its kind is drawn at that fraction — the rule `connection_svg` followed until v0.50, for the same reason — and the figure then says so in its notes (*Vergrössert, nicht massstäblich: F2 = 0.2 kN*). A magnitude that could not be read is drawn grey, its label says `?`, and the notes name it: what the checker reads is the figure, so the figure has to say it.

**Export.** `statics.prepare()` reads and checks every source, resolves every magnitude against the calculations `export_all.py` has just built — nothing is read back off disk — and builds `data/statics.json` (schema `statics` 1.1, checked by `validation.validate_statics_business()`) and `data/statics/<id>.svg`. The record carries the frame, the size unit, what was drawn and where, and for every load the number shown with where it came from. There is deliberately **no `{vars, steps, checks}` record**: a model is a drawing, not a calculation, and nothing addresses one with `show-calc`.

**Typst.** `show-static-model(model, width:, loads:, highlight:, caption:)` sets the figure (§10bis). `loads: false` strips the arrows, the loads' labels and the notes at the figure's `<!-- loads -->` markers, leaving the bare system **in the same frame at the same scale** — so a report can print the system and then the system with its actions, one under the other, and the two line up. `highlight` puts a halo under a point, a member, a support, a release or a load, inserted at `<!-- highlight -->` as a plan's is: which part is highlighted is the call's to say, not the drawing's. `static-model-ids()` is the data access, and `Sections/005-tragwerksanalyse.typ` loops over it, so a model drawn in Woodcore reaches the report with no Typst edit.
