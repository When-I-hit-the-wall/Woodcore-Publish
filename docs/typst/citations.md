# Norm references and the bibliography

> Systematic norm citations, registration and show-bibliography.
>
> Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md (§10) on 2026-10-07. The § numbers are kept as stable anchors: `docs/README.md` maps every § to its file.

## 10. Systematic norm references

Every `step`, every input `var`, and every `check` carries `norm` and `norm_ref` (§3.1-§3.4). `show-calc()` displays them automatically next to the equation, without the engineer having to retype them in Typst — the reference is carried by the data, not by the presentation.

In the built example, the citation is rendered as **`SIA 265 §4.2.1`** (no comma before the clause, no brackets) — or as the reference alone, without `§`, when its `norm_ref` is empty (`norm-text`, v0.32) — in a small font, in a dedicated right-hand column laid out with `grid()` so it stays level with the equation it cites for every level of detail. An **annex** takes no §: a `norm_ref` «Anhang D» prints «SIA 261 Anhang D», «annexe D» in French and «Annex D» in English (v0.79). An early attempt used Typst's `place()` to float the citation into the literal page margin, but `place()` forces a paragraph break inline and desynchronized the note from its equation by one line; the grid-based row layout (`annotated-rows()` in `lib/calc-engine.typ`) avoids that entirely and is the pattern to reuse for any new citation.

**And the document behind the citation is named once, at the end** (v0.99). «SIA 265 §4.2.1» says which clause and not which SIA 265 — its title, its edition, its publisher are nowhere on the page. So a citation is **registered as it is made**, and `#show-bibliography` prints at the end of the report the four fields of every document actually cited: its designation, its full title, its edition and its publisher (§10bis). ⚠️ **Nothing about how a citation is printed changed**, and that was the condition: `norm-text` is untouched, no page of a report already written moves, and a report that never calls the command is the report it was. ⚠️ It is **not** Typst's `#cite`/`#bibliography` and carries no CSL: those set a citation as a key in the text and let a style decide its form, which is neither the form this report cites in nor a form a checking engineer reads (§13).

**Where the entries come from**, in this order. `calc/norms/norms.json` (§11) holds the documents the engine knows, thirteen of them, each with its `kind` — `norm` or `literature` — and every field read off the edition's own title page in `ExternalFiles/000-Normes/` (§11bis, §2 rule 13). A project's own `references.bib`, in the report's folder, holds what only that project cites — a manufacturer's ETA, an office handbook, a course document — and **wins for a key both carry**, a project knowing which edition it was calculated against. A citation neither names is printed **as it was cited**, in a third block of its own: never invented, never lost.

**A citation string is what a call writes**, and an entry is found by its key or by one of its `aliases`: the data writes «SIA 261» and the entry's designation is «SIA 261:2020», the engineer writes «VKF14-15» and the entry is «VKF / AEAI 14-15». One citation reaches one entry, which the reader refuses to let two entries claim.

**What is registered.** The **data is walked** — every `norm` field of `data/calculations.json`, `data/connections.json` and `data/connectors.json`, wherever it sits in a record — which is what covers `show-calc`, `show-input`, `show-polar-moment`, `show-connector-resistance` and `show-live-load` at once, with no edit to any of them, and it is why the walk is `show-bibliography(data: true)`'s default. What the report **writes by hand** registers at the call: `var`, `eq`, `val`, `var-unit` and `var-line` register their `norm:`, `distance` the norm its tables come out of, `#cite-norm` what it cites. `lib/timber.typ` needed nothing of its own — every one of its checks cites through `var(..., norm: "SIA 265")` and `eq(..., norm: "SIA 265")` — and neither did `verify`, which prints no citation at all (§10quinquies).

⚠️ **A registration is emitted before its command's `context`, never inside one.** A state written inside a context is read back by a later context and *not* by the folded chain an update function reads — the panne §3.3quinquies records for v0.85, where `show-polar-moment` published its forces from inside its own context and `find`, `val` and `verify` saw them while `eq` did not. `bib-note` therefore returns content and every caller emits it at the top of its body: `var`, `eq` and `var-unit` beside the registry update they already emit, and `val`, `var-line` and `distance` as `bib-note(norm) + context { … }`, which is the one change their signature line needed.

⚠️ **A norm is registered whenever a printed value carries one, whatever the level prints of it**, level 0 included. Whether the clause appears in the column is a matter of level; whether the document is a source is not — and a variable declared at `level: 0` is named by the formula under it, which does print. So the three reports of §3.7 cite the same documents for the same numbers, which they would not if the list followed the level.

⚠️ **`state.final()`** is what lets the list be printed wherever the engineer puts it and still hold what the whole document cited — one convergence pass more, which Typst carries. `Sections/007-literatur.typ` is where it stands (§10bis).

⚠️ **A set of rows where none carries a citation is laid out in one column** (v0.61.2). The citation column is a fixed 3.8 cm and takes 4.4 cm with its gutter, whatever it is put inside: at the full text width an uncited line wrapped four centimetres early with an empty column beside it, and in a `grid` cell at half that width -- the two load cases of `Sections/005` sit side by side like that -- it swallowed more than half the space and the text broke after three centimetres, hyphenated. Reserving room for a note that does not exist was the whole of it. Rows that *do* carry one keep the fixed column, so cited lines go on lining up down the page; verified by rendering the report before and after, where only the two pages of `Sections/005` changed.
