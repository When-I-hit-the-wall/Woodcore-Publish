# The static models tab

> *Modèles statiques*: moved verbatim from [`window.md`](window.md) (§10quater) on 2026-10-10 (issue #31), the file having reached 218 KB. `window.md` keeps the window's frame and the index of the tabs; a reference to `docs/ui/window.md §10quater` finds this file there.

## 10quater (suite) — static models tab

**The static models tab (v0.51).** *Modèles statiques / Statische Systeme / Static models* is where a static system is drawn (§3.3octies). Like the plan tab it lives in modules of its own: `calc/tools/statics_tab.py` holds the tab and its texts in the three languages, which woodcore_gui.py merges into its `STRINGS` and lists in `TABS`, and `calc/tools/statics_canvas.py` the drawing surface. ⚠️ **Since v0.117 two views** (*Dessin · Affichage*, above): the card of calls left the properties; *Affichage* offers `show-static-model` — width, loads, dimensions, the parts highlighted among the model's ids, the caption, and the diagram, greyed for a model that carries none (`@diagram`) — and the loop over every model.

On the left, only the saved models, with *Nouveau*, *Dupliquer* and *Supprimer*.

In the middle, the tool bar, **seven named groups in boxes of their own** (v0.51, second review; the seventh is v0.55's), whose icons are the very symbols the figure draws (`statics_svg.swatch`, each kept in its own proportions and a release's cropped to its node, since a stretched or distant symbol is one nobody can read):

| group | what is in it |
|---|---|
| Éléments | *Point*, *Barre*, *Câble*, *Ressort* |
| Appuis | encastrement, appui fixe, appui roulant — their symbols alone |
| Articulations | rotule, N-libre, V-libre, ressort de rotation — their symbols alone, **one button each**, the combo of four words being gone |
| Charges | *Ponctuelle*, *Linéaire*, *Trapèze* — **one button per shape**, the shape combo likewise gone; their symbols alone since v0.87, the names in their tooltips |
| Cotations | *Horizontale*, *Verticale*, *Parallèle*, *Angle* -- the four dimension tools, each taking **three clicks** (v0.55); their symbols alone since v0.87 |
| Navigation | *Sélection*, *Vue*, *Ajuster* (a tool button of the bar's height since v0.87), and the grid's step over the *Accrocher* toggle |
| Historique | the two curved arrows, *Annuler* and *Rétablir* |

Four things make the bar readable, all of them the engineer's on seeing the first one. Every button is **the same height**, whether it carries a name or its symbol alone. A name is set **under** its symbol rather than beside it: beside it each button was 139 px and the six groups needed four rows. In the bar a tool is named by **what tells it from its neighbours**, the box above it saying the rest — under *Charges* a button reads *Ponctuelle*, not *Charge ponctuelle* — and the full name stays as its tooltip and everywhere else. And the bar **wraps** rather than scrolling *while it has the height to*, so the middle panel demands only the width of the widest single group and the panels beside the drawing can be widened as far as the engineer likes. ⚠️ **Given less height it scrolls instead, and that reverses what v0.51 settled** (v0.66). The engineer met it on a 0.5 m model, where four rows of tool bar stood over a drawing area 400 px tall. The bar's pane is the engineer's to drag now: with room it wraps exactly as before, and dragged shorter it lays the groups into the rows that still fit, grows to the right, and shows a horizontal scroll bar — so the height comes back to the drawing and no tool goes missing. ⚠️ The **vertical** scroll bar is off, and that is the rule the whole thing rests on: a tool bar scrolled up and down hides a tool with nothing to say so, where a horizontal bar says exactly that. ⚠️ The symbols are drawn in the figure's own ink, which is black: on Windows' dark theme they came up all but invisible, so the button takes the ink and the fills from the palette, as the undo and redo arrows already did.

**The bar is compact since v0.87** (the engineer: «diminue la plage que prennent les outils afin que ce soit plus ergonomique»), and its measures are one place for this tab and the schemas' (`flowbar.py`: `TOOL_ICON_PX`, `tool_height`, `symbol_px`, `size_tool`, `tool_group`). Every control is **one fixed height**, a symbol with its name under it and nothing to spare; a symbol is drawn at **20 px over a name** and **26 px alone**, rendered at the screen's pixel ratio so it stays sharp on a scaled display; a named button is exactly as wide as its name, where it was at least 104 px whatever its name, and a symbol-only one kept close to square; the group boxes are tighter. The loads and the dimensions carry their symbols alone, a symbol that is an arrow or a dimension line saying what it draws; the members keep their names, a bar, a cable and a spring being told apart by a line's weight and pattern, which a word says better. The bar's pane **sizes itself to the bar** the first time the tab is shown, and shows no sideways scroll bar it does not need. Measured at the window's default 1400 × 780 in French: the bar went from 264 px in 3 rows to **132 px in 2**, and the drawing from 223 to **355 px**. ⚠️ Accepted price: the load and dimension symbols are faint at that size (§14).

**The static models' and the schemas' bars are the plan's since v0.94** (the engineer: «Ces volets prennent trop de place … Les icônes sont suffisamment compréhensibles … Au survol, après un court moment (0.5 s), une info-bulle apparaît qui donne le nom de la commande et comment l'utiliser. Les boutons … doivent être de même hauteur que ceux … du module "Plan de position"»; and on the separators, «Séparateurs fins, sans titres»). Every tool is its **symbol alone** in a square button, the controls that had none given one in `flowbar.ICONS` — *Sélection*, *Vue*, *Cadrage*, *Ajuster*, *Accrocher* (a magnet toggle, `snap_button`, where it was a check box), *Annuler* and *Rétablir*, and the schemas' *Grille…* and *Effacer les lignes* —, the members icons of their own on the diagonal (`statics_tab.MEMBER_ICONS`, the figure's own swatch being a hair at 20 px), the grid's step and the schemas' *Surfacique* mode compact (`size_combo`; a `SymbolCombo` whose list keeps the words). **Every button is as tall as a plan tool**, measured rather than written: `flowbar.tool_height()` lays out a probe the way `plan_tab.tool_button` makes one, 27 px at the default font, the symbols drawn at 20. **The groups are parted by thin lines with no titles** (`QFrame#toolSeparator`), a group opening a row putting its line outside the bar, and the navigation and the history open both bars as they open the plan's. **A tooltip after 0.5 s** gives the tool's name in bold and how it is used, in lines of 56 characters (`flowbar.DelayedTips`, on these tools only — Qt's own delay is left alone elsewhere), the next tool's at once while one is up, the usage being the canvas's own hint where there is one. `ToolBarArea.snug()` grows the pane by the row it lacks when the tab is first shown. Measured at 1400 × 780 in French in Windows' own fonts: the static models' bar from 242 px in 3 rows to **56 px in 2**, the drawing from 232 to **418 px**; the schemas' from 324 px in 3 rows scrolling 220 px sideways to **85 px in 3 rows and no scroll**, the drawing from 172 to **411 px**. It supersedes the seven named groups in boxes of the table above and the compact measures of v0.87.

The grid's step is auto, **5 m**, 1 m, 0.5 m or 0.1 m. A click puts down what the tool draws — a point, a member between two points (a click on an existing point takes it rather than making another), a support on a point, a release on the near end of the member clicked, a line load on a member — and a click that lands on nothing of the kind says so in words rather than drawing into thin air. **A point load takes two clicks** (v0.51, second review): the first names the point it acts on, the second the member its angle is counted from and along which it is drawn; a click elsewhere ends the step and leaves it without one. **A support just put down is then turned by the pointer**, in steps of 45°, until the next click sets it: that is how an encastrement against a wall or a roller on a slope is drawn, and the panel takes any angle after it, to a tenth of a degree.

**A dimension takes three clicks** (v0.55), the first tool here to take more than two: the beginning of the dimension, its end, and then the point its line is to run through -- what is saved of that third click being how far it lies from the middle of the two ends, so that the dimension follows the model afterwards. A click that lands on a point of the model anchors to it; one that lands on nothing keeps its own place. The **angle** tool's first two clicks name members instead, and its third gives the arc its radius and says which of the two angles is meant, by the side it falls on. Each refusal is said in words: two ends in the same place, the same member twice, two members that do not cross. Escape gives a half-drawn dimension up, and Tab + a distance + Enter types the third click as it types any other.

Then, **under the drawing**, the **five lists**: *Points* (id, x, z, **articulation**, appui, angle, nom), *Barres, câbles et ressorts* (id, type, point 1, point 2, raideur, nom), *Charges ponctuelles* (id, point, **barre**, **décalage**, direction, angle, valeur, unité, b_e, nom, affichage, **couleur**), *Charges linéaires* (id, barre, forme, direction, angle, valeur, valeur fin, unité, b_e, nom, affichage, **couleur**) and, since v0.55, *Cotations* (id, type, de, à, décalage, valeur, **nom**, **affichage**). Three of those columns are the second review's, and the *couleur* and the dimensions' *affichage* v0.87's — each a dropdown, with the same combo in the entry's panel (§3.3octies). In the dimensions list, **de** and **à** take an id -- a point's, or a member's for an angle -- or a place of its own written `x / z`; **valeur** is what the model measured and is read only, since it is the model's answer and not a cell to type into, and the list shows it whatever the figure writes; and **nom** — *texte* until v0.87, when it replaced the value — is the dimension's name, which its *affichage* writes on the figure beside the value, instead of it or not at all. **The columns of every list can be widened and narrowed by dragging the header's dividers** (v0.87, `flowbar.ResizableColumns`): each starts at the width of what it holds, and a column the engineer has dragged keeps his width through every refill of the list and through `view_state`, a width snapping back at the next keystroke being a width nobody can set. A point's **articulation** is a dropdown of the four tools, offered where exactly one member end meets at that node and closed with a word where several do, the answer not being one thing there. A point load's **barre** is its reference member and its **décalage** how far along that member it is drawn, in metres or as a fraction — `0.5l` is midspan. And a uniform line load's **valeur fin** is closed rather than left to be typed into, a uniform load having no far value; clearing a value cell leaves the load with none at all. They sit there rather than in the left panel because there they are as wide as the drawing, which is what makes their tabs and their columns readable. Everything can therefore be entered twice over: by clicking on the grid, or by typing into a list. A cell that cannot be read is painted and **the row keeps its old value**, so a half-typed number destroys nothing; an id cell edited renames the entry and every reference to it, and a clash refuses; and the cell the engineer clicked keeps the focus rather than being taken back to the first column. **A point's support is in its own list**, with its angle, since a support belongs to a point; a release belongs to an end of a member and is set in that member's panel.

**The centre column is three panes the engineer can drag** (v0.66): the tool bar, the drawing, the lists. The drawing is what height is wanted for, and the other two were spending it — so each gives it back by being dragged shorter, and the three heights travel in `view_state()` like everything else here. Neither pane collapses: one dragged to nothing is one the engineer has to know to drag back out of an edge. The lists also carry a floor of their own, a tab bar, a header, one row and the two buttons, because a `QTableWidget` asks for several rows and would have stopped the splitter long before the drawing gained anything. The warnings stay **outside** the splitter and keep their fixed height, a fixed height being the point of them.

**The pointer's coordinates and the tool's hint are written inside the drawing** — the hint at the top left, the coordinates at the bottom left with a small **x/z axis pair beside them**, so the frame is never in doubt — where the eye already is rather than in a strip below it. Only the warnings keep a box of their own.

**The grid follows the zoom**: 5 m, 1 m, 0.5 m and 0.1 m, with heavier lines every 10, 5, 5 and 1, the finest step still far enough apart on screen being the one shown, and the snapping following whatever is shown; the combo forces one instead. **The wheel always zooms and the middle button always pans**, whatever tool is chosen and whatever is half-drawn, between two absolute limits in screen pixels to the metre — a limit taken from the model's own size moved with the model, so a first point left the view zoomed in past use and refusing to come out. **`fit()` fits the figure, whatever its size**, and opens on a four-metre grid only for a model whose points span nothing — none at all, or one (v0.64.1). Holding *every* model to four metres, which it did until then, made a 0.5 m bench a twenty-fifth of the drawing area: the floor grows the rect to a **square** of that side, and a square letterboxed into an area 1070 × 400 px is fitted to its height, so the width blows out to 12.4 m. What holds the top end is MAX_ZOOM, which is absolute and needs no floor on the model. ⚠️ A model four metres and over was never touched by the floor and is fitted exactly as before; `tests/test_fit.py` holds both halves. **The tracking works as AutoCAD's does**: resting the pointer on a point acquires it, the cursor then locks to one of the eight polar directions, to the axis or the normal of a member meeting there, or to where the ray crosses the line of a member extended; **Tab** opens a distance field over the readout, filled with the distance so far, and **Enter** puts the point down there, the new end becoming the next anchor. All of its arithmetic is `statics.py`'s — `tracking_rays`, `nearest_ray`, `extension_lines`, `typed_point`, `track` — so the canvas holds only which state it is in, and the whole behaviour is verified without a window. ⚠️ Tab is the tracking's and not the focus chain's: `focusNextPrevChild` returns False, or the focus would leave the canvas the moment a direction is locked.

On the right, the model (id, label, the three sliders, and since the second review its **own influence width** — the same form a load's b_e uses, since both carry the same thing), then the selection: a point with its **support** row and that support's angle to a tenth of a degree, a member with its length, a spring's **raideur**, and an **articulation** row per end (with a rotational spring's stiffness), a load with its **intensité** — two radio buttons, *Valeur* or *Variables* (renamed from *Variable de l'export* at the engineer's request), the latter three combos filled from `data/calculations.json` with the value read shown under them, and since the second review a **facteur**, a **constante** and a **description** beside them, so a design value is written as `0.8 · q_k11 + 0.2` with the expression shown under the form — and its **largeur d'action**: a checkbox, b_e, its name, its description and whether the figure shows it, which overrides the model's for that load — and since v0.87 its **couleur**, as a dimension its **affichage**. Under them, **«Épaisseurs de cet élément»** (v0.99): a card of the factors this one entry states over the
model's own three — a slider in per cent per factor it is drawn with, and **no row at all** for one it is not,
a factor an entry cannot show being no field it could be typed into (`figure_style.ENTRY_KEYS`). It is shown
the way an overridden b/h is shown (v0.75): while the entry states none, a row stands at 100 % and follows the
drawing; the moment it states one, its name and its readout are written in `theme.colour("override")` — the
orange the connectors' tab has written an overridden value in since v0.70 — and **«Reprendre»** appears beside
them, which takes the factor off. Setting a slider back to 100 % by hand does **not** take it off, which is the
b/h rule stated in v0.75, «typing the very value the position holds still writes it over»; and the last factor
taken off takes the whole `style` with it, so an entry that overrides nothing is saved exactly as it was before
the factors existed. The rows are `calc/tools/stylefactors.py`'s, shared with the plans' and the schemas' tabs
for the reason the three already share `identity.IdentityRows`. ⚠️ **A drag is one step of «Annuler»**: the step
is pushed when the slider is taken hold of and dropped again where the drag ended where it began, which is
`edit`'s own rule applied to a change that arrives one pixel at a time — where the **drawing's** own three
sliders still leave a drag outside the history altogether (`on_style`), which is a separate thing and was not
this version's to change (§14). And the card's own rows are read again rather than the panel rebuilt after each
change: rebuilding it from inside a slider's own signal would delete the slider being dragged. A variable a load named is remembered by the window while a typed value is tried, so changing one's mind twice does not lose it. Then the calls to copy and the permanent *Annuler les modifications | Enregistrer* row. *Enregistrer* checks the draft by `statics.py`'s own rules, refuses an id already taken (ignoring case, as the file system does), asks before a rename, writes `statics/<id>.json` and exports the models at once by the `statics.export()` export_all.py runs. As in the other editors, the draft survives a language switch, and leaving it or closing the window asks first.

**Fields that follow a variable** (issue #42, v0.117; `docs/domain/variable-links.md` §3.3duovicies). A selected point has a card «Coordonnées», its x and z in metres; they, a spring member's stiffness, a rotational release's stiffness and the influence width b_e of the model or of a load each have the button 𝑥 that links them to a variable of the export (`StaticsPropertiesMixin.linked_field`, `linkable_spin.LinkableSpin`), the link being one step of «Annuler». **A load's magnitude names its variable through the «Variables» tab** (UI-005): the three lists *Domaine*, *Cas*, *Variable* it had are retired; *Variables* shows the variable named, its symbol and domain, and «Choisir…» opens the choosing mode, offering a force, a force per metre or per square metre or a moment; choosing *Variables* on a load that named none opens it at once, and giving up leaves the load a value. Factor, constant and description are as they were. The export's values are read again as a model opens and every linked field given the value its variable has now (`statics.refresh_links`), so the drawing is the one the report will print; *Enregistrer* writes each one's last value and 1.2 where the model follows a variable (`statics.stamp_version`).

**The Text tool** (issue #43, v0.117; `docs/domain/variable-links.md` §3.3duovicies-bis). The last group of the bar, *Textes*: a click puts a text `T1` down at the point clicked, selected, and the tool goes back to «Sélection»; a drag on the text moves it, its x and z, not a `label_offset`. It is drawn over the structure without a ground or a bold line, a white halo parting it from a member, and is no label: it has no «Remettre l'étiquette». Selected, the pane on the right shows its content (a line per line, an `_` setting a subscript, a brace typed twice), «Insérer une variable» — the «Variables» tab in its choosing mode, every number of the export and of a «Formules» domain offered whatever its unit, the one chosen inserted where the cursor stands as a token `{1}` — and a card per variable, its symbol and domain, «valeur» or «symbole = valeur», and «Retirer» (`calc/tools/text_pane.py`, the four engines' one). A variable that can no longer be read is in the warning colour and prints its last value. Its size is the selection's own `text` factor.
