# The schemas tab

> *Schémas*: moved verbatim from [`window.md`](window.md) (§10quater) on 2026-10-10 (issue #31), the file having reached 218 KB. `window.md` keeps the window's frame and the index of the tabs; a reference to `docs/ui/window.md §10quater` finds this file there.

## 10quater (suite) — schemas tab

**The schemas tab (v0.72).** *Schémas / Schemas / Schemas* is where a connection detail is drawn (§3.3decies). Like the plan, the static models and the connectors tabs it lives in modules of its own: `calc/tools/schema_tab.py` holds the tab and its texts in the three languages under an `sc_` prefix, `calc/tools/schema_canvas.py` the drawing surface — ported from `statics_tab`/`statics_canvas`, whose arrangement this is. ⚠️ **Since v0.117 two views** (*Dessin · Affichage*, above): the block of calls left the properties; *Affichage* offers `show-schema` — width, the construction aids (`aids: false` writable since v0.117), the parts highlighted, the caption — and the loop over every schema.

**«Surfacique» with a position (v0.117).** The engineer, 08.10.2026: «lorsque je sélectionne l'outil «Surfacique», dans le volet de droite, je dois déjà voir les options pour cet outil. Je dois pouvoir indiquer la position, et, lorsque cela est fait, je dois pouvoir choisir si je dessine la largeur ou la hauteur de la position. Ensuite, je dessine l'élément qui possède donc une largeur fixe». While `area` is the tool, a card **«Options de l'outil»** stands first in the right pane (`tool_box`, shown by `canvas.tool_changed`, hidden for every other tool): the **drawing mode** (the tool bar's three modes, one value behind the two controls: `on_area_combo`, `on_tool_mode`), the **position** the element is of (`canvas.element_position`, offered from the very list the selection's combo offers, `fill_tool_positions`), and, for a rectangular section, **which dimension is drawn** — the other is the position's and is fixed. `domains/schema.area_fixed(record, draw)` says it (`section.sides` is the one reader of a dimension): a `rec` of 100/200 drawn by its width has a fixed height of 200 mm, drawn by its height a fixed width of 100 mm; a plate, a CLT panel (its thickness) or a round section (its diameter) has one dimension, which is the fixed one — the longer extent of a diagonal drag is then the length of the piece and the thickness lies across it —; a steel profile or no position gives the free drawing, with a sentence saying so. **The three modes stay** (`canvas.area_fixed`, `area_points`): by diagonal corners the dragged side follows the cursor and the fixed one grows the way the drag goes (`rectangle_with_fixed_side`); by an edge, or an edge and a typed width, the third click only says on which side of the edge the rectangle lies and its distance is ignored (`rectangle_with_fixed_width`). The element carries the position (`add_area` now applies `element_position`, as `add_linear` did), its points are in mm, and its b and h follow the position as ever. ⚠️ **What is not recorded** is which dimension was fixed: if the position's dimension changes later, the polygon keeps its size while the element's `h` follows the new one — known and accepted (spec D-011); `schema_version` stays 1.1.

- **Left**: the schemas, grouped (the widget of v0.69), with *Nouveau · Dupliquer · Supprimer*.
- **Centre**: three panes the engineer drags (v0.66's own arrangement) — the tool bar, the canvas, the lists. The bar is a `flowbar.FlowLayout` of **seven groups**, in a `flowbar.ToolBarArea` that **never scrolls up and down** (v0.75: what the height does not hold goes to the right, and a vertical wheel scrolls it sideways): **Navigation** (Sélection · Vue · *Cadrage* · Ajuster · the grid's step, auto / 1000 / 100 / 10 / 1 mm · Accrocher) · **Historique** (Annuler · Rétablir) · **Éléments** (*Linéaire*, two clicks; *Surfacique*, with the selector of its mode) · **Connecteur** (Clou · Broche · Vis · Boulon · Anneau, the five the Connecteurs tab's own bar names, drawn with the very symbols the figures draw, and since v0.74 *Grille…*, a **button and not a tool** — it places a whole rectangular group at once and has nothing on the grid to point at, exactly as *Trancher* and *Fusionner* do; the group is drawn as it is set out, and *Annuler* in the dialog puts the markers back as they were) · **Aides** (Centre · Point · the six lines, their symbols alone since v0.75, the group having been 950 px wide with their names · *Effacer les lignes*) · **Modification** (v0.73: *Déplacer · Dupliquer · Tourner · Symétrie*, four tools of two clicks, and *Trancher · Fusionner*, two **buttons** — they act on what is already selected, so there is nothing on the grid for them to point at and nothing for the exclusive tool group to hold checked) · **Cotation** (horizontale · verticale · parallèle · angle, their symbols alone since v0.87). Each verb's hint says what its own two clicks mean, one shared «cliquez» saying nothing about either. ⚠️ **The bar is compact since v0.87**, on the static models' measures (`flowbar.py`, above): at the window's default 1400 × 780 in French it went from 312 px in 3 rows **with a 452 px sideways scroll** to **200 px in 3 rows and no scroll**, and the drawing from 188 to **300 px**.
- **Centre, bottom**: six lists — *Éléments linéaires · Éléments surfaciques · Moyens d'assemblage · Points · Aides · Cotations* (the named points a list of their own since v0.77, right of the markers, each with *visible* and **connecteur** ticks: a point ticked «connecteur» is a fastener every module may take, «Connecteurs» and «Moments polaires» alike, `schema.fastener_places`; it stays an aid of kind `point` with `connector: true` in the source, an additive key; **a tick is centred in its cell**, and clicked there, since v0.77 — `check_cell.CentredCheck`, the delegate of every tick column, drawing and hit-testing the style's own indicator rectangle moved to the cell's middle, Qt having no alignment for it) — so everything can be entered twice over, by clicking on the grid or by typing into a cell. A cell that cannot be read keeps its old value and says so, `statics_tab`'s own rule; a dimension's measured **valeur** is read only, being the drawing's answer and not a cell to type into, and shown whatever the figure writes, its **nom** and **affichage** columns (v0.87, with a combo in the dimension's panel) saying what the figure writes (§3.3decies). Their columns are dragged wider or narrower by the header's dividers and keep the width given them (`flowbar.ResizableColumns`, v0.87, as in the static models' tab). Since v0.73 they take **Ctrl and Shift**, the way the left pane's folders do (v0.69) and the way an explorer does.

**What v0.75 added to the tab.** The **selection panel** of an element is what the element is: its *Position* chosen from the exported positions («T001 — Träger C24 100/200 mm», a number the export lost kept as `D001 (?)`), its *Vue*, its **b** and **h** each with a grey line saying which position it follows or an **orange** one naming what the position holds where it is written over, and *Reprendre*; and its *Étiquette*, the composed label as the field's placeholder. Every change is one step of *Annuler*, and the position chosen is given to the next piece drawn. The **lists** carry b and h as two columns (typed, written over; emptied, followed again) and *Ajouter* under each, putting one entry in the middle of the view; `shown` is a check box; the row under the pointer is lit on the drawing in its own colour, hovering selecting nothing; and Ctrl in a list keeps what the other lists hold selected, since a table holds one collection. A construction line is picked on the drawing (last, after the markers, the points and the pieces). *Tourner* takes **Tab and an angle** after its centre. The **crop** box gives *Dessiner* and *Tout le dessin*. The copied calls put `show-schema(id, highlight: …)` first when something is selected.

**The multiple selection (v0.73).** **Ctrl+click toggles** an entry on the drawing, and a plain click replaces the whole selection. ⚠️ The selection is **a focused entry and the rest** (`canvas.selection` and `canvas.extra`), not a flat list, and deliberately so: the properties panel, the list that scrolls itself into view and a drag all act on exactly one entry, the way the focused item of a Windows explorer selection does. The one just Ctrl+clicked takes the focus and what was focused joins the rest. Only the focused entry carries **handles** — they are what a drag grabs, and a set of them on every entry of a six-entry selection would be a field of dots with no one meaning — while every selected entry is outlined, a construction line included, which is what makes *Trancher*'s line visibly part of the selection. ⚠️ **A Ctrl+click starts no drag**: a drag begun on one would move a single entry out of a set the engineer was still building. ⚠️ **A table holds one collection**, so a selection of an element *and* the construction line it is to be cut on cannot be shown in one list at all: each list marks its own share of it and the tab holding the focused entry comes to the front (`sync_tables`). That is the tabbed layout's own limit, not a state the two got into — what holds the whole selection is the canvas, and the lists are a view of it. **Suppr deletes everything selected in one step of *Annuler***, since a multiple selection deleted in six steps would be undone in six.

**The canvas renders the figure once, not on every paint (v0.77).** Every movement of the mouse repaints the view — the snap marker, the tracking ray and the preview all move — and `SvgLayer.paint` rendered the whole SVG each time: measured at a device pixel ratio of 2 on a 1900 × 1000 view, about 300 ms a paint, Qt's DeviceCoordinateCache not being reused between paints, so the tab stalled under a moving pointer. The figure is now rendered into an image the size of the view and blitted, and rendered again only when the SVG, the transform, the view's size or its pixel ratio change (≈ 20 ms a paint, the grid drawn with one `drawLines` per pen and no antialiasing). ⚠️ The engineer described the whole computer stopping for two or three seconds, which was not reproduced here; so the window keeps a watch on itself from the moment it is shown (`calc/tools/stallwatch.py`): a timer beats on the event loop four times a second and **one** watch thread, for the life of the window, appends every thread's Python stack to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Woodcore\stalls.log` when the beat is more than 1.5 s old — `app.exec()` alone meaning a stall inside Qt's own C++ — and a line «System hitch» when the watch thread itself wakes more than 0.75 s late, which is the **whole computer** stopped rather than the window, with the process's memory at that moment. The log starts afresh past 512 KB. ⚠️ Until the second half of v0.77 it re-armed `faulthandler.dump_traceback_later` four times a second, and CPython starts a new operating-system thread at every re-arm — 14 000 threads an hour for an idle window, found while the engineer reported a freeze every ~50 s with Woodcore open. Four minutes of measurement on the engineer's machine (a latency probe and per-process counters) saw no system stop and Woodcore idle, so that freeze is **not** proven to be this; the churn is gone either way, and the next freeze writes which of the two it was.
- **Right**: the identity (Nom · Description · ID since v0.77, `identity.py`; the folder is set by a drop), what is selected, the Typst calls to copy, and the permanent *Annuler les modifications · Enregistrer* row. Between the identity and the selection, **Taille des symboles** (v0.99): the plans' and the static models' three sliders, with the same names and the same range, which a schema had none of — one drag is one step of «Annuler». And inside the selection's card, **Épaisseurs de cette entrée**: one slider per factor the entry chosen is drawn with and no row at all for one it is not (`figure_style.ENTRY_KEYS`), each **orange with «Reprendre»** the moment the entry states it — an overridden b/h's own way of saying it (v0.75, `stylefactors.EntryRows`). ⚠️ Setting a slider back to 100 % by hand states 1 and is **still** an override: «Reprendre» is what follows the drawing again, which is v0.75's rule in those words, «typing the very value the position holds still writes it over».

The canvas is the same instrument the static models' is: a grid that follows the zoom and that the pointer snaps to, AutoCAD-style tracking (resting on a point acquires it, the cursor then locks to a ray, **Tab** takes a distance along it and **Enter** places it), the wheel always zooming about the pointer and the middle button always panning, and a label dragged clear of what it names growing a leader back to it. ⚠️ Its scene unit is the **millimetre**, not the metre, so its zoom limits are millimetre limits; everything else the two share.

⚠️ **The three ways to draw a rectangle are two constructions.** Two diagonal clicks are their own shape; three clicks and «one click + Tab» both end as an edge and a signed width off it, so both call `schema.rectangle_from_edge` and land on the same box to the digit — which is what keeps the rectangle from jumping when the engineer switches mode.

⚠️ **Every tool shows its own symbol, and the five fasteners show the Connecteurs tab's.** `schema_svg.swatch()` draws the bar's icons, and a fastener's is `connector_svg.SWATCHES`' own — one symbol per kind, drawn once, so the two bars cannot come to show different things for the same fastener. Two traps are held by a test rather than by care: the five connector buttons were one plain dot each until it fired, and an aid line and a dimension share three kind names (`horizontal`, `vertical`, `parallel`), so a dimension tool asks for its icon under a name of its own or the four cotation buttons all come out as dashed construction lines.

*Enregistrer* checks the draft by `schema.check_source`'s own rules, refuses an id already taken (ignoring case, as the file system does), asks before a rename, writes `details/<id>.json` and exports the schemas at once by the `schema.export()` `export_all.py` runs. As in the other editors, the draft survives a language switch, and leaving it or closing the window asks first.

**The Text tool** (issue #43, v0.117; `docs/domain/variable-links.md` §3.3duovicies-bis). The last group of the bar, *Textes*: a click puts a text `T1` down at the point clicked (mm), selected, and the tool goes back to «Sélection»; it is dragged as a label is, moved, copied, turned and mirrored with the selection, and drawn over the pieces and the dimensions without a ground. Selected, the pane on the right shows its content (a line per line, an `_` setting a subscript, a brace typed twice), «Insérer une variable» — the «Variables» tab in its choosing mode, every number of the export and of a «Formules» domain offered whatever its unit, the one chosen inserted where the cursor stands as a token `{1}` — and a card per variable, its symbol and domain, «valeur» or «symbole = valeur», and «Retirer» (`calc/tools/text_pane.py`, the four engines' one). A variable that can no longer be read is in the warning colour and prints its last value. Its size is the selection's own `text` factor. Since this version the schemas' labels are set by the static models' text engine (`svg_text.py`, D-19), and move slightly.

⚠️ *Retired by the spec 2026-10-21-commandes-typst-refonte (lot 19): `schema` -- write #figure(image-schema(id), kind: image, caption: [...]). What follows describes it as it was.* **«Copier» copies a figure (v0.118, issue #62, UI-001).** `show-schema` retired, «Affichage» offers `schema()`'s options -- the width, the aids, what is highlighted, no caption -- and copies the figure the report writes: `#figure(schema("<id>", …), kind: image, caption: [<the schema's label>]) <fig-<id>>`, the label made of the id (`build_reference.figure_label`), the caption's markup escaped (`typst_content`); the loop over every schema is a figure captioned with its id (docs/domain/detail-schemas.md §3.3decies).
