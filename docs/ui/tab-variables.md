# The variables tab

> *Variables*: moved verbatim from [`window.md`](window.md) (§10quater) on 2026-10-10 (issue #31); rewritten on 2026-10-25 for issue #114 (spec `docs/requirements/completed/2026-10-24-onglet-variables-groupes.md`). How the tab was from v0.100 to v0.124 -- the view «Affichage», the provenance in a column, the ★ of a result, «Tous les domaines» -- is in [`history/2026-10-25-tab-variables-before-114.md`](../history/2026-10-25-tab-variables-before-114.md).

## 10quater (suite) — variables tab

**What it is for.** *Variables* (group *Référence*, between *Domaines* and *Commandes*): every value the project computed -- the export's (`data/`), the files Woodcore writes for the report (the domains of `formulas.json`) and the compiled documents' registries --, to find one, to print it elsewhere in the report, or to have a field of another tab follow it (issue #42). Prototype: `docs/requirements/prototypes/2026-10-24-onglet-variables/`. **One view, three panes** (D-1, D-4: «Affichage» retired, the card suffices): on the left the menu of Woodcore's tabs, the source and the domains of the filter; in the centre the search bar carrying the filter as tags and the list grouped by domain; on the right the card.

**One line per source** (D-2: «un calcul n'est porté normalement que par une seule source, et toutes les autres ne font que la rappeler»). The snow load `SL` is a domain three times -- the export's `snow-load · SL`, the file's `SL`, the report's `SL` -- joined by «Le même calcul ailleurs». ⚠️ **Which calculation a domain is, is read and never guessed** (`calc/variable_calc.py`, REQ-001 to REQ-004): the export's by its address; a file's by the path `domain-files.json` gives it (or, absent there, a domain of the *Domaines* tab, by `formulas.json`); a document's by its name looked up in those two tables (Q-001, A) -- a name found in two kinds, or in none, stays without a calculation, never matched by resemblance. Each domain carries `tab` (the key of the tab that computes it) and `calc` (`CalcRef`, (kind, id), the same for its three sources). A check's tab is read off its record -- `kind`, and `fire` for «Résistance au feu» --, never off the folder of its file (D-12: FE-01, a fire check, is written under `Timber-checks/Bending/`). The tables kind → tab are the only keys of tabs written outside `tab_registry` (ARCH-005), held to it by `tests/test_variable_calc.py`; issue #112 changes the lines of `buckling` and `lateral-torsional`. **The source that computes** is the export where it has the value, else the file, else the document; the others recall it. **Printed** is true where a compiled document holds a domain of the calculation, false where none does, and unknown while no document is read. **Results**: what the export declares for its case (`results`), or, for a check, `eta` (`member_check.RESULT_KEY`, the last `#eq` of every check's file, ARCH-012), set on every source of the calculation: `q_k` of A1 is a result in the export, the file and the report. The root domain of a document (`variables.REPORT_DOMAIN`) is no longer listed (D-5): 918 variables in 75 domains on the example project once its three documents are read (935 in 78 before).

**The left pane** (UI-002, UI-003). A button «Tous les onglets ▾» opens the menu, a tree in a popup (`calc/tools/tree_menu.py`, `Qt.Popup`, placed by `hover_bubble.place`; Escape or a click elsewhere closes it): Woodcore's navigation as the window draws it -- groups, sections, tabs, read from `tab_registry.NAV_GROUPS` and `NAV_SECTIONS` in the window's words when the tab assembles (ARCH-005; the menu's model, `calc/variable_query.py`, receives it and imports no tab, the rule of the sections held equal to the window's by `tests/test_variable_query.py`) --, plus, in the group «Document», one entry per document made (D-14) for the domains a document declares itself, and «Sans onglet» for a domain whose calculation is not found. Each entry says its number of variables in the source chosen; an entry at zero is greyed and cannot be chosen; a group or a section takes all its tabs. Under it the source, «Toutes · Export · Fichiers · Rapport» (`chrome.SegmentedSwitch`), and the domains of the filter under the title of their tab, in the order of the navigation: the case, its source tag (**export**, **fichier**, **RS**/BP/CU, the colours of `theme.json`), its number; a domain no document prints is grey («aucun document ne l'imprime»). A click chooses a domain, a second lets it go. 260 px wide at the start.

**The centre** (UI-004 to UI-010). The search bar (`calc/tools/tag_search_bar.py`, a brick with no word of its own) carries the filter as tags before the text typed -- «Actions › Actions variables ×», «source : export ×», «Charge utile · A1 [export] ×» --; a tag's × takes it off, ⌫ in an empty bar takes off the last (domain, source, menu); the words typed are searched **in** that filter, every one of them in any order («kn neige»), over the name, the symbol, the label, the description, the unit, the clause, the value, the case and the tab. Beside it the unit (the project's units, commonest first), «Résultats seulement» and the count. **The list is always grouped by domain** (`calc/tools/variables_list.py`, ARCH-007): a `QTableView` on a flat model, a title line per domain spanning the width (its case, its source, its tab, its label, «pas imprimé», its number), its variables under it; never a tree, which the keyboard would fold. Columns: the box, **the symbol** as the report prints it (`richtext.MathDelegate`, the name to write in its tooltip), the value, the unit, the label; one line per variable, a long text cut by «…», whole in its tooltip. The widths start at 120 / 120 / 76 px, **never follow a search** (no `ResizeToContents`), are dragged at the right edge of a heading and kept, and a double click on that edge gives the width of the start back. **A result's value is framed** in the accent of the theme, in the accent and in bold (UI-008, D-6; the ★ is gone).

**The choice «comme dans Windows»** (UI-009, `calc/variable_select.py`): a click takes the variable alone; Ctrl+click or its box adds or takes it away; Shift+click the range from the last one clicked, across the domains, never a title line -- Ctrl+Shift adding it --; the header's box takes every variable shown or lets them all go; the keyboard follows the mouse (arrows, Shift+arrow, Space, Ctrl+A). Not Qt's own selection: the view paints the model's. A choice survives a filter (D-15). The bar above the list -- «3 choisie(s) · Copier les 3 commandes · Tout désélectionner», hidden at none -- copies one `#var` line a variable, the ones shown in the order of the list, then those the filter hides (the bar counts them).

**The card** (UI-011, UI-012, `calc/variable_card.py` composes it, `calc/tools/variables_detail.py` shows it). At its head symbol = **value** unit -- the value framed for a result, «Résultat du calcul» under it --, the label (a workbook's label as written, D-3), the path «Actions › Actions variables › Neige · SL [export]»; **«Ouvrir l'onglet Neige ↗»**; the **command to copy** and what to include before it: the export's `#var(key, domain: (calculation, case))`, nothing to include; a file's `#var(key, domain: "id")` with its `#include domain-file(kind, id)` -- or `#include "/Domains/<id>.typ"` (`formula_domain.include_call`) -- higher up; a document's `#var(key, domain: "name")`, the line below the domain. The calls are `build_reference`'s `call_*` (D-18). Then the name to write, the description, the clause, the calculation (the step's formula), the choices (the one taken marked), the level, **«Vient de»** in words -- Woodcore (a value typed in a tab, `entered:`, or computed, `python:`); the norm; the document -- in place of the raw `source` (the workbook, its sheet and its cell, and «Ouvrir le classeur», until v0.125: no workbook is read since issue #109); and «Le même calcul ailleurs»: «Calculé dans export ; celle-ci le rappelle.» and a button per other source, which chooses the same variable there. The card shows the last variable clicked. A domain chosen alone shows its case and source, its path, «Ouvrir l'onglet …», the whole domain to copy (`#include domain-file(…)`) and its numbers of variables and results.

**«Ouvrir l'onglet»** (REQ-009, D-9, D-11) brings forward the tab of the **source that computes** -- the snow's for a value chosen in the report that recalls it -- through `MainWindow.show_tab(key, node)`: the tab by the navigation's road, then the case opened where that tab lists it (`GRAPH_KIND`, `lists_object`, `open_object` of the graph of dependencies, issue #71; the node is `dependency_graph.target`'s). Python and *Domaines* (no `GRAPH_KIND`) come forward without a case (a load case read from a workbook did too, until v0.125: every load case is a tab's since issue #109). `MainWindow.can_open_tab(key)` says why the button is greyed, in the window's words: the tab is not in the document shown, the document is not made, the tab is reserved (Séisme, Charge utile while it is, Combinaisons…), or a field is waiting for a value; a domain the document declares itself has no tab at all.

**Choosing for a field** (issue #42, REQ-008; `docs/domain/variable-links.md` §3.3duovicies). `MainWindow.begin_variable_pick(on_done, title, accept)` brings this tab forward, the navigation and Ctrl+1 … Ctrl+9 off. `accept(var, domain)` -- `variable_sources.linkable` -- is what the field may take, and the menu, the source, the domains, the numbers and the list offer nothing else: never a document's value (a link is resolved at the export, without a compile). One value: no box, no bar of the choice, the choice of before kept aside and back afterwards. The bar names the field («N de ZT-01 [kN]») with «Annuler»; the card carries the box «… · suivra Q_k de A1 · **Importer**», or says why a value is refused. Escape gives up.

**The tab and its browser** (ARCH-004, ARCH-009, D-17; issue #106). `variables_tab.VariablesTab` is a class of parameters on the type «Données» (`data_tab.DataTab`, [`building-a-tab.md`](building-a-tab.md)): `KEY`, `SERVICES` -- the type's banner, then `variables_browser.VariablesBrowser`, one whole service (`tab.service("browser")`: `mount`, `state`/`restore`, `shown`, `mode_changed`, `leave`), which reads no other tab, asks the window through its public methods and tells the banner above it what failed (`danger`: a reading, a file of `data/` that does not read, an export of an earlier Woodcore) or that a document changed since its reading (`warning`) --, and `EXPOSES`: `begin_pick`, `end_pick`, `pick_chosen`, `selected_key` (the last variable clicked, `(origin, domain, name)`, name None for a domain alone), `selected_keys` (all, in the order of the list), `variable`, `address`, `select_key`, and the attributes `search` (the `QLineEdit` of the bar: the command palette writes a variable's name in it), `domains`, `pick_bar`, `pick_label`, each read and written on the browser. The frame answers `view_state`, `restore`, `on_shown`, `on_mode`, `confirm_leave` and `splitter` (the browser's). Until issue #106 a `QWidget` shell forwarded each of them, one line each, and the banner was inside the browser, below the bar; it now stands above the bar, the type's. Its state: `source`/`domain`/`name` (the variable chosen, as before), `origin_filter` (the segment), `menu`, `tags` (the domain chosen), `widths`, `selection`, the words, the unit, «Résultats seulement», the splitter; a state of v0.117 is read and sets no source. The panes' sizes are kept under (`tab_variables`, `-`, `panes`): the view «Données» gone, the sizes of before are not read. ⚠️ **It is a browser and has no `dirty`** (what tells an editor from a browser in this window); its `confirm_leave` only waits for a reading's thread and never refuses.

**The export is read again at every showing**, its files some tens of kilobytes, so a polar moment saved on another tab is here when one comes back (D-014); a file not there is a project not exported yet (said in the empty list), one Woodcore wrote that is not what its schema says is named in the banner with the field at fault, the others listed all the same -- `domain-files.json`, `checks.json` and `steel.json` too, read beside them (`Export.index`), never counted among the files whose absence says «pas encore exporté» (ARCH-003).

⚠️ **Since v0.110 the reading is usually free, because the PDF carries it** (the engineer's choice of the three §14 put to him). `woodcore-document` attaches the whole registry to every PDF it writes -- `pdf.attach("woodcore-variables.json", bytes(json.encode(var-registry.final())))`, a few hundred bytes, **no extra page**, and **silently ignored for a PNG or SVG target**, so the 306 frozen example pages and the website's renders are untouched (both measured before it was written). `calc/variables.py`'s `from_pdf` reads it back with the pypdf already pinned, and `best()` is the policy in one place: the PDF's own attachment where that PDF is **newer than every source**, else a compile. Measured on the example project: **0.7 s against 4.4 s**, the same 90 variables in the same 8 domains, field for field. ⚠️ **It chooses a price and never an answer** -- both routes serialise the same `var-registry.final()` -- and a test compares the two on the example project rather than trusting it. ⚠️ **Typst resolves the attachment's name as a path relative to the file that calls it**, so what reaches the PDF is `_woodcore/lib/woodcore-variables.json`; Python matches on the **basename**, which is right whatever a project calls its generated folder. ⚠️ And the freshness test is the PDF's **modification time against the newest source**, not the `stamp`: a PDF carries no record of the sources it was made from, so what can be asked is whether anything has been touched since it was written -- a source saved in the same second reads as *not* current, which errs the safe way. ⚠️ **Since v0.111 a document whose compile left a stamp (`calc/stamps.py`, §12) is asked the stamp instead**: its PDF is current when not one of the files that compile read has changed since, whatever flags it was compiled with — a level chooses what is printed and never what is stored — and a digest and not a time, so a file saved again with the same bytes leaves it current; the modification-time rule above is what is left where there is no stamp, or where the PDF is not the one the stamp was taken of. And a reading's source set is **the document's own** (`stamps.enumerated(here, mode)`), never the whole Typst root, which in three documents is the project's folder and would take the other two documents, the workbooks and the archives for sources of this one's variables.

⚠️ **A reading is a full compile, and that is what decides when it happens (v0.100).** Measured at **8–10 s** on the example project against 9.8 s for `typst compile` of the same report: a state's final value is known only once the document has been laid out to convergence, so there is no cheaper question to ask. The engineer asked for «mieux encore, automatiquement importer depuis typst à l'ouverture de l'onglet», and automatic on *every* opening would be ten seconds of a hung tab per switch — the argument §10quater made for the RFEM tab (retired in 0.117), whose `on_shown` read nothing. So it reads **once**, the first time the tab is opened, in a thread with the tab usable meanwhile and the bar saying «Lecture du rapport…»; then it reads its **cache** until the report, the engine or the export has moved under it, and a banner says so. *Relire le rapport* is the one thing that reads again, its tooltip saying what it will cost. ⚠️ **A reading that fails keeps the values of the last good one on screen** and says so in the banner, in the danger colour, with **Typst's own message under the reason** — it names the file and the line, which nothing in the window could. The bar goes on saying what is listed rather than repeating the failure, and carries it only when a first reading failed and there is nothing else to describe. A file NextCloud holds open is tried once more and then said as what it is rather than as a report that does not compile (§13). A reading is refused while an export or a compilation runs — `data/` is in what it reads — with `app.busy()`'s message box for a click and a line in the bar for the automatic one, a modal put up by a tab merely being opened being worse than a tab that fills a moment later. `WOODCORE_VARIABLES_READ=0` turns the automatic reading off, which the suite does (`typst_check.ENV`'s own arrangement): a run that brings every tab forward to look at its buttons must not pay for a compile, nor leave a thread behind a window it is about to close.
