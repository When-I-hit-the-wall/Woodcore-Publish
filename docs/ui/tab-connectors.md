# The connectors tab

> *Moyens d'assemblage*: moved verbatim from [`window.md`](window.md) (§10quater) on 2026-10-10 (issue #31), the file having reached 218 KB. `window.md` keeps the window's frame and the index of the tabs; a reference to `docs/ui/window.md §10quater` finds this file there.

## 10quater (suite) — connectors tab

**The connectors tab (v0.68, laid out on the classeur in v0.70).** *Moyens d'assemblage / Verbindungsmittel / Fasteners* (*Connecteurs / Connections* until v0.79, one word per notion) is where a connector is defined and its shear resistance calculated (§3.3nonies). Like the plan and the static models tabs it lives in a module of its own, `calc/tools/connectors_tab.py`, which carries its texts in the three languages under a `cn_` prefix and its own `tab_connections` title; `woodcore_gui.py` merges them with the rest and lists the tab in `TABS`. Three panes. ⚠️ **Since v0.117 two views** (*Calcul · Affichage*, above): the calls left the right pane, which keeps the figures, the results and the warnings; *Affichage* offers `show-connector-resistance`, `show-connector`, `show-calc`, the `R_d` read by `var-of` and the loop over every connector.

On the **left**, the connectors of the project, grouped into folders (the paragraph above), and under them *Nouveau · Dupliquer · Supprimer*; the draft *Nouveau* or *Dupliquer* opens is listed as «(nouveau, non enregistré)» until it is saved, as in the other editor tabs (v0.79, B21). A connector is one file and one calculation, so *Dupliquer* is how two layouts are compared — the numbering `SD_01`, `SD_02` the engineer already used in Excel.

In the **middle**, at the top of the form, the **Schéma** row of *Identité*: the detail this connector is drawn on, with a line saying what it holds. Its elements fill each member's «Élément du schéma» combo — choosing one lights that piece on the drawing beside the form, the very `highlight` argument `show-schema` takes — and its markers fill *Disposition des broches*, which is a list with a tick against each marker this connector carries and its place beside it, read only (v0.74). Above that, a horizontal bar of the kinds of fastener, built like the static models' tool bar and drawn with the very symbols the figures draw (`connector_svg.SWATCHES`, in the palette's ink so they are legible on Windows' dark theme): **Clou · Broche · Vis · Boulon · Anneau** and the carpentry joints **Joint · Embrèvement · Queue d'aronde**. Choosing one **folds the bar away** and gives the form below it the room; *Changer de type…* opens it again, and the fold travels in `view_state()`. ⚠️ Only **Broche** is live. Every other kind is present and **disabled**, its tooltip saying which calculation is still missing — the v0.47 rule that a tool which draws nothing says so, rather than being absent and leaving the engineer to wonder. 👉 The three carpentry kinds are buttons and **not** entries of `connector.TYPES`: adding one means the module, `connector.TYPES` and `validation.CONNECTOR_TYPES` together, which `tests/test_constants.py` holds.

**Under the bar the form is the engineer's own list of boxes, in the classeur's order (v0.70):**

| box | what is in it |
|---|---|
| **Identité** | Schéma · Nom · Description · **ID**, composed of the name and writable over, and nothing else (v0.77, `identity.py`): the folder is set by a drop, the variant and the length stay what the file holds, the designation is folded into the description, and the members joined are read off the schema |
| **Assemblage** | the one list, and nothing else. It commands everything below it. The three configurations SIA 265 gives no k_β for are in the list, **disabled**, their tooltip naming 6.1.1.11 |
| **Géométrie et matériau** | four shapes, one per assembly. Always d and the connection's **one** Feuchteklasse; then one column per timber member (α, position, Festigkeitsklasse, Holzart, ρ_k, t, Vorbohrung) — two side by side for a Holz-Holz joint, one for a Stahl-Holz joint, which instead adds b, the Schlitzbreite t, the number of plates, t₁, t₂, t₁/t₂, k_β, k_β,₂ and p·k_β — and for outer plates (*Bleche aussen*) b, t₁ and k_β alone, the engineer's own list, equation (128) reading none of the rest (v0.75). A Stahl-Holz member shows no t: b is its thickness, and a t beside it was typed into and never read |
| **Disposition des broches** | the schema's connector markers **and its points ticked «connecteur»** (v0.77, a point saying so in its tooltip), with a tick against the ones this connector carries; *Tout cocher · Tout décocher · Reprendre du dessin* (which reads n and a₁ off the ticked pattern); the row under the pointer is lit on the pattern beside the form (v0.75) |
| **Broches** | n_tot and its override · p · then per member n, **n_quer** and a₁ · then the plate's steel (Stahl-Holz only) and f_u,k with the note on where it comes from · M_u,k. ⚠️ **It stands after *Disposition des broches*** and is plural since v0.84, and **its n_tot is the count of ticked markers**, read-only while it follows it: `draft()` has read n_tot off the drawing since v0.74 while the field showed whatever was loaded, so the form said one thing and the calculation used another. *Écraser le compte du dessin* is what frees the field, which is the one way the two ever differ |
| **Conditions** | séisme (η_t) and nœud de treillis (× 0,75). Not in the engineer's own list, and kept: those two have nowhere else to be said |

**Every row reads `sentence | symbol [entry] unit`.** The **symbol sits immediately left of the entry**, which is what the engineer asked for and what *Moments polaires* already does with b and h: a symbol written after the sentence is read as part of the sentence, and the eye cannot find it while typing. The sentence itself is the classeur's, **literally** — «Anzahl der in Faserrichtung des Holzes hintereinander angeordneten Verbindungsmittel» and not «Broches en file» — and its French and English come from the glossary (§4bis), which until v0.70 carried none of the connector's words at all, so a French window said «Stahl-Holz, Schlitzbleche». ⚠️ **Every row carries a unit**, «–» where the quantity genuinely has none, so that a missing unit is a decision somebody took and not one nobody noticed; the units sit in a column of their own rather than as a spin box's suffix, so they line up down the form and a **read-only** cell can carry one too. ⚠️ A word-wrapped label in a `QFormLayout` needs a **fixed** width, not a maximum: with a maximum Qt lays the row out at the label's one-line height and the rows draw on top of one another.

**The cells the calculation fills are read-only**, in the palette's grey — they are the formula cells of the classeur, and t₁, t₂, k_β, ρ_k, n_quer and M_u,k are read there rather than typed into. ⚠️ They live in the two boxes rebuilt when the assembly changes, so the map that names them is cleared with them: a `QLabel` Qt has deleted is otherwise still a key, and the next refresh raises «Internal C++ object already deleted». For the same reason a rebuilt widget is `setParent(None)`'d **before** `deleteLater()` — deletion waits for the next turn of the event loop, so without it the old rows went on painting under the new ones and the box was drawn twice over. ⚠️ **And `hide()`'d before either (v0.77)**: a widget detached while shown, never explicitly hidden, is a top-level window, and Qt showed it as one on that same turn — the white windows titled «Typst-Katalog» that flashed for under a second each time another connector was chosen, six a switch (the old «Diamètre d», «Pièce 1 / Pièce 2», «k_β» and «n_tot» rows). The polar tab's member groups and the static models' properties panel detach the same way and are hidden first too.

**Hovering a box lights the results it produces (v0.70).** The correspondence is read off the classeur's own structure, where a formula cell closes a run of manual entries: *Assemblage* → p; *Géométrie et matériau* → f_h,α,k per member, and in steel also t₁, t₂, k_β, k_β,₂ and p·k_β; *Stabdübel* → k_red and M_u,k; *Disposition* → n_tot and the minimum spacings. R_d and R_d,Verb belong to no box, being the product of all of them.

⚠️ **Hovering a *Pièce* box lights that member on BOTH drawings (v0.84).** Its element on the pattern, as since v0.77, and now its piece or pieces on the **section**: which piece that is is the assembly's own answer and not a rule about left and right — the Seitenholz of a *zweischnittig* joint is the **two** outer pieces and its Mittelholz the middle one, einschnittig they are simply the left and the right, and a Stahl-Holz joint enters one member, so every timber piece is that member's (`connector_svg.highlighted_pieces`). The ring is the schema's own highlight colour, so the two drawings mark one thing one way.

⚠️ **One box at a time, and its frame does not move (v0.77).** A lit box used to take a style sheet and an unlit one the platform's own frame — two frames laid out differently, so a box jumped the moment the pointer entered it — and the sheet's `QGroupBox` selector reached every box inside the lit one, so «Pièce 2» lit with «Pièce 1». Every box of the form now carries one style sheet (`BOX_STYLE`), and a box is lit through its own `hot` property; the box lit is the **innermost** watched one under the pointer, asked of the pointer's position (`update_hot`) because Qt sends a parent no Leave when the pointer moves into a child. A *Pièce* box, in *Géométrie et matériau* and in *Stabdübel* alike, lights its member's results and **its piece on the pattern**. ⚠️ That exposed a defect from v0.74: `connector_svg.pattern_of` handed its `(collection, id)` highlight to `connection_svg.overlay` as the index of a fastener, which it never equals — so neither a chosen element nor a hovered marker row had ever been lit on the pattern. An element goes to `mark` now, and a marker to the index it is numbered by. *Reprendre du dessin*, which the row of the markers box had lost though its method and its words remained, is back; and a schema carrying neither a marker nor a point ticked «connecteur» says so in orange (until the end of v0.77 a point was not offered here at all — see §3.3decies).

**A value taken from a position is pre-filled and writable over (v0.70).** There are no longer two radio buttons saying *D'une position / Saisie manuelle*: the Festigkeitsklasse and the Feuchteklasse are filled from the member's position, and the line under the field says where they came from — in grey while the value follows its position, **in orange the moment it has been written over**, naming what the position holds, with *Reprendre* beside it. ⚠️ **Since v0.77 a value that follows says so in its own field** — «C24 — T001», «2 — S001, T001» (v0.117; «C24, repris de T001» until then) — and the line under it exists only when there is something orange to say (below). ⚠️ An override **stays**: the source keeps the position *and* the grade, and the next *Importer Excel* does not undo it (§13). ⚠️ That line is composed by the window from structured fields of the record and is **not** translated from a German sentence: a sentence with a position id inside it is not a glossary key, and the two templates that tried to be one swallowed each other's text.

⚠️ **Where a value comes from is said in its field; a line under it only when written over (v0.77).** The engineer's rule, for every value taken from a position: the grade reads «C24 — T001» in its combo (v0.117; «C24, repris de T001» until then) — the item's *data* is the grade and its text what is shown, so nothing compares the text — and the moisture class's «follow» item reads «2, repris de S001, T001», the worst of the members' classes. The line under the field appears only for an override (orange, with *Reprendre*) or a position gone (orange); the grey line saying again what the field already said is gone. «Schémas» follows the same rule for an element's b and h: a side that follows its position carries «mm — T001» in its spin box (v0.117; «mm, repris de T001» until then), and the line under it appears only when the side is written over, the position is gone, or the position gives no b/h — every one of them orange. These are the only fields of Woodcore that follow a position.

**What a member is, shown whether or not the resistance calculates (v0.77).** *Essence* is the position's `material` column (Fichte), read through the member's element; ρ_k is SIA 265 Tabelle 8 or 9 for the grade in the field — the characteristic value, the 5 % fractile, the one density the calculation reads. ⚠️ A row with SIA 261 Tabelle 28's mean unit weight was added the same day and **removed** at the engineer's word, «INUTILE pour les calculs»; Tabellen 28-30 stay transcribed in `calc/norms/` and nothing reads them. Until now these came off the calculation's record and read «—» on a connector that could not be calculated yet — no marker ticked. The read-only cells carry the number alone, their unit being the row's own column (they read «350 kg/m³ kg/m³»).

**The steel is one field, and only where there is a plate (v0.77; two fields since v0.70).** The **plate's** grade is SIA 263 Tabelle 1 (S235 … S460 — the norm gives S460, which the classeur's `Daten` sheet does not), and its row is hidden for a Holz-Holz joint, which has no plate. The **dowel's** grade is back since 12.10.2026 (spec 2026-10-12-audit-issues-47-48-49, D-06): «Acier de la broche», S235 to S460 -- the grades of SIA 263 Tabelle 1 whose f_y meets SN EN 14592 Tabelle G.9 -- fills f_u,k with the grade's f_u, S355 by default; f_u,k stays writable, and a value typed over it sets the grade back to «—»; the grey note under it says where f_u,k comes from. **The plate's bearing** (D-14): for a Stahl-Holz joint, the plate's thickness, its smallest end distance along the force e1, its edge distance across it e2 and the hole d0 -- 0 while not stated, which the export says; p1 and p2 are read off the drawing. **The truss box** says under it the condition of SIA 265 5.3.6.2.2, the one sentence of the norm file the report prints under R_d,Fachwerk (D-08). **p and k_β show before a marker is ticked** (`connector.preview` → `dowel.preview`): p is 1 for Holz-Holz einschnittig and 2 for zweischnittig, and k_β is Tabelle 45's once the members' grades and thicknesses resolve — shown in the form, never exported, the record staying `calculated: false` until the pattern exists. 👉 SIA 263 Tabelle 3 is deliberately not transcribed — its «Stifte» are the pins of fastenings and supports, by its own Anwendungsbereich column, and not the Stabdübel of a timber connection.

On the **right**, the two figures one above the other, each in a `QSvgWidget` fed the SVG string the export writes; under them **the results in rich text**, so that `R_d` is R with a real subscript and not three characters (v0.70), and under those the **Minimale Abstände** table — equation (88) is conditional on those spacings (6.2.1.1), so they belong beside the resistance they qualify, and until v0.70 they were in the report and nowhere on screen — laid out as the report lays it out (`record_table`: the group line, the units, the totals bold, a symbol between dollars set with its subscript by `_math_html`/`_prose_html`, which the warnings go through too). ⚠️ The **Verschiebungsmodul** table stood beside it from v0.77 to v0.84 and is gone with the table itself: K_ser and K_ser,Verb are two rows of the results now, which is what that table showed. ⚠️ **A lit result sets its own ink with its ground** (v0.84, the engineer: «orange clair sur blanc ne se lit pas bien») — a pale ground under the palette's own text is unreadable in a dark theme, where that text is near-white, and barely there in a light one, so lighting a row was making it harder to read rather than easier. Then the warnings **in yellow inside the pane and never as a pop-up**, as asked, each **translated through the glossary** the report reads; the calls to copy and the import line; and the permanent *Annuler les modifications · Enregistrer* row. *Enregistrer* checks the draft by `connector.check_source`'s own rules, refuses an id already taken (ignoring case, as the file system does), asks before a rename, writes `connectors/<id>.json` and exports the connectors at once by the `connector.export()` `export_all.py` runs — so one connector is exported without the load workbooks being read again.

👉 Nothing in the tab computes. `domains/dowel.py` does, and the preview calls the very functions the export calls, so the numbers on screen are the numbers in the report. ⚠️ A position the export no longer lists is kept in its combo and marked `D001 (?)` rather than silently replaced by whichever entry happens to be first — the plan tab's own rule, and it matters here because the replacement would change a resistance without anyone asking.

**Numbers that follow a variable** (issue #42, v0.117; `docs/domain/variable-links.md` §3.3duovicies). The diameter, f_u,k, and each timber member's α, t and a₁ — for a Stahl-Holz joint the steel half's b (its member's t), the slot and t₁/t₂ — carry the button 𝑥 (`_field(…, link=(where, key))`, `linkable_spin.LinkableSpin`): the «Variables» tab offers a load, a script or a «Formules» domain of the field's dimension, never a connector or a polar moment (`links.JOINTS`). The link is the connector's own (`links`) or its member's, written by `draft_links` with `connector.stamp_version` (2.1 where one is left); `fill_links` shows each against the export's values as the form is built. A row's own widget is `widget.row_cell`, which `setRowVisible` names: a linked field's parent is its wrapper.
